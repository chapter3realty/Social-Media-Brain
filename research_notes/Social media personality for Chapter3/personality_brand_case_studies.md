# Personality-Led Brand Accounts: Case Studies, Mechanics, Backfires (for a Myrtle Beach, SC real estate brokerage persona)

Evidence labels used below: **[MEASURED]** = a number from a company filing, platform case study, controlled study or retail-scan data. **[SELF-REPORTED]** = a number from the brand, its agency or the platform selling ads (so the source has an interest in it). **[ANECDOTE]** = a quote, an observation or something a blog asserted without data. Research for this file was done on 2026-11-07, using web search snippets and secondary coverage. Where sources conflicted, both are noted.

## 1. Consumer brand case studies: mechanics and documented results (Wendy's, Duolingo, Ryanair, Liquid Death, CAH, Steak-umm, Scrub Daddy, Old Spice, Aviation Gin, etc.)

### Takeaway
The accounts that broke out share a pattern. One or two named writers own the voice. The brand writes down its voice rules (Wendy's calls its voice "challenger with charm"). Approval is short, often a single person giving a yes or no. The humor is pegged to a recurring ritual or "tentpole" (National Roast Day, Duo's "death"). The measured business results are real but modest and hard to attribute. Most "sales lift" numbers are self-reported or disputed (Old Spice is the clearest example). The firmest numbers are platform brand-lift studies and follower counts.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The repeatable mechanics are: (1) a named, written voice line ("challenger with charm"); (2) a consent rule for roasts (roast only those who ask; the brand jokes at itself first, as Ryanair does); (3) one owner and one approver; (4) recurring formats or tentpoles the audience can join; (5) a cheap attribution method such as Duolingo's "how did you hear about us" question. A brokerage can copy all five, and #5 maps onto a lead-intake question ("Where did you find us?").
- Self-deprecation about the category's known downside works (Ryanair on cramped seats, Liquid Death on "it's just water"). For a Myrtle Beach brokerage the equivalents could be humidity, tourist traffic, hurricane season, HOA fees, golf-cart culture and flood insurance. Joking about those builds an "honest insider" persona as long as it never makes light of a specific client's loss.
- Steak-umm shows a "sincere expert" mode working inside an irreverent account. That is the safest high-upside mode for a licensed firm: an opinionated explainer on market data, scams and misinformation.

### Gaps
- No verified 2024-2026 numbers were found this session for MoonPie, Taco Bell, Innocent Drinks, Merriam-Webster, NASA or National Park Service accounts, Surreal cereal or Gymshark. Background knowledge (not verified here): MoonPie's social was run by agency Tombras with absurdist tweets; Surreal's billboard humor used fake celebrity names; Innocent's "chatty" UK voice dates to the late 1990s; the NPS's humor posts are reviewed for safety messaging. Any figures for these need separate verification.
- No 2025 National Roast Day results. No independent (non-TikTok) brand lift data for Wendy's.
- Old Spice sales causation remains contested. Ryanair's ticket-sales link is a CEO claim only.

## 2. Regulated and trust-based brands with personality: what edge they use and how they stay compliant

### Takeaway
Regulated and trust-based accounts keep their personality to plain language, warmth, self-deprecation and local or community humor. The few documented examples share three things: a written tone-of-voice guide pre-approved by compliance/legal (so each post doesn't need sign-off), short approval chains, and humor that never touches the regulated promise itself (rates, returns, coverage, outcomes). Small-town police departments are the best analog for a local, trust-based, regulated entity. Several grew followings larger than their town's population with blunt humor, and a peer-reviewed case study warns that such accounts must not depend on one person.

### Cited Findings

**Fintech/banking**
- Monzo's social lead frames financial-services rules ("many specific laws (and punishable criminal offenses)") as an opportunity rather than a blocker. — [Sprout Social, Monzo social strategy](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/monzo-bank-social-strategy/)
- Monzo moves fast because it has a documented tone-of-voice guide and short approval chains, so the social team doesn't need management sign-off on every post. Its humor plays on shared customer feelings (e.g., the "Year in Monzo" recap people love and dread) and uses emoji, which is unusual for a bank. [ANECDOTE, agency case study] — [Whito case study](https://whito.co.uk/stolen-with-pride/monzo/); [Sprout community](https://community.sproutsocial.com/financial-services-hub-85/monzo-bank-s-investments-in-social-media-creativity-pay-dividends-2545)
- Monzo's public tone-of-voice principles stress plain language and avoiding "the curse of knowledge." — [Monzo on Medium](https://medium.com/monzo-bank/introducing-the-monzo-tone-of-voice-23d89ee20433); [Adactio](https://adactio.com/links/tags/monzo)
- Lemonade is cited as bringing "fun and simplicity" to an industry known for complicated policies. I found no source on how it handles compliance review. — [Kota](https://kota.co.uk/blog/5-cool-brands-in-characteristically-boring-industries)

**Local government / small-town police (closest analog to a local regulated firm)**
- Brimfield Township, Ohio (pop. ~10,000): the chief's blunt humor drew 155,000+ Facebook likes (91,000+ in 2013). He invited about 85,000 followers to a community festival. The chief said follower counts don't change operations. [MEASURED follower counts] — [Ohio AG Law Enforcement Bulletin](https://ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Media/Newsletters/Law-Enforcement-Bulletin/November-2013/Brimfield-Township-Police-Tally-More-Than-91-000-F); [Police1/AP](https://www.police1.com//chiefs-sheriffs/articles/ohio-police-chief-invites-85k-facebook-fans-to-party-oNmaIppsK9x3gtno)
- Bangor, Maine (pop. ~33,000): a recurring character prop, the "Duck of Justice," helped the page pass 20,000 likes and later 260,000+ followers. [MEASURED] — [Maine Public](https://www.mainepublic.org/post/why-hundreds-thousands-people-are-drawn-bangor-police-s-facebook-page); [Police1/AP](https://www.police1.com/social-media-for-cops/articles/duck-of-justice-gets-maine-police-facebook-boost-tWruPFZoFN3UNxwg)
- Pottsville, Arkansas (pop. 2,800) has 14,000+ followers. Chatham, Illinois reached 30,000. Bath Township, Michigan copied the format from a Texas department and passed its population in three years. Clinton, Maine (pop. ~3,400) reached several times its population (2026). [MEASURED] — [NewsChannel20](https://newschannel20.com/news/local/keeping-them-informed-while-having-fun-chatham-police-departments-facebook-page-reaches-30000-users); [Fox17](https://fox17online.com/2017/08/17/bath-twp-police-go-viral-with-social-media-wit); [Boston.com, Jan 2026](https://www.boston.com/news/off-beat/2026/01/28/clinton-maine-police-facebook)
- A peer-reviewed case study (*Policing: A Journal of Policy and Practice*) finds informal, humorous communication engages citizens. It also says agencies need a formal team for consistency and "to avoid reliance on a single manager of the accounts," and uses expectancy-violations theory to explain both the positive and negative outcomes. [MEASURED/academic] — [AMiner record](https://aminer.org/pub/5e5e192d93d709897ce58e38/small-police-agency-use-of-social-media-positive-and-negative-outcomes-noted)
- Critics ask whether humor "misses the point" for public institutions. NYPD's 2014 #myNYPD hashtag backfired. — [NHPR](https://www.nhpr.org/post/serve-protect-and-entertain-police-depts-social-media-can-be-slippery-slope)
- The formula ("Come for the laughs, stay for the public safety warnings"): humor is the delivery vehicle for useful local information. — [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/minnesota-police-on-twitter-come-for-the-laughs-stay-for-the-public-safety-warnings/485607781)

**Real estate compliance (directly relevant to the Myrtle Beach brokerage)**
- SC LLR guidance: a licensee's social post about an area of town generally counts as advertising their services, and failing to clearly identify the listing brokerage in ads is prohibited, even when permission is granted. [PRIMARY, SC regulator] — [SC LLR Advertising Memo H.4754](https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/News/AdvertisingMemo-H.4754.pdf); [SC LLR Reminder of Advertising Restrictions](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- Exam-prep sources say SC ads must carry the brokerage name as registered with SCREC, and that personal accounts marketing listings count as advertising (citing S.C. Code § 40-57-135). [SECONDARY, verify against statute] — [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sc-real-estate/south-carolina-statutes-practice/sc-advertising-practice); [EstatePass](https://www.estatepass.ai/questions/under-sc-real-estate-advertising-rules-all-advertising-by-a-licensee-must-includ/)
- For comparison, North Carolina rejects a "one-click" disclosure: the firm name must be in the post itself. Myrtle Beach agents often market into NC, so this matters. — [NC REALTORS](https://www.ncrealtors.org/?p=55041); [NCREC FAQs](https://ncrec.gov/Pdfs/genupdate/2023-2024%20Section%202%20NCREC%20On%20Your%20Side%20FAQs.pdf)
- Fair housing: online ads and social posts are covered by federal, state and local fair housing laws. HUD/DOJ enforcement shows regulators watch agents' social conduct. The firm is liable for an agent's violation. A written internal fair-housing policy is a meaningful defense. Video, virtual tours and narrated walkthroughs count as advertising. — [Bradley law firm white paper](https://www.bradley.com/-/media/files/insights/publications/2019/07/avoiding-fair-housing-violations-in-a-hyperconnected-world.pdf); [NAR, 6 tips for legal marketing](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/law-and-ethics/6-tips-for-keeping-your-marketing-legal); [Illinois REALTORS / NAR legal video](https://www.illinoisrealtors.org/blog/nar-legal-video-gives-4-best-practices-regarding-social-media-and-fair-housing/)

### Inferences
- For a licensed brokerage, the lowest-risk personality edge is: the honest insider and the self-deprecating local, plus humor about weather, tourists and buyer/seller tropes, wrapped around useful information (flood zones, insurance costs, HOA rules, scam warnings). This is the police-page formula of "come for the laughs, stay for the warnings."
- Copy Monzo's structure. Have compliance (the broker-in-charge) pre-approve a written voice guide with a list of "never joke about" topics. Day-to-day posts then need only a light check, while anything touching listings, pricing claims, neighborhoods or demographics gets a mandatory BIC review.
- Fair-housing exposure is the biggest legal risk of edgy humor in real estate. Jokes about "the kind of people" in a neighborhood, schools as a proxy for demographics, "family-friendly," religion or nationality-tinged tourist jokes can create liability. Steering-adjacent humor is off-limits for any persona.
- The firm name must be on every post (SC rule, stricter NC comparison). Mascot or character accounts must still clearly identify the brokerage.

### Gaps
- No primary sources found this session on: Chime/Robinhood humor (Robinhood's 2021 GameStop-era social backlash would be relevant); Progressive's Flo or GEICO's gecko as social accounts (both are agency-built mascots that have outlasted multiple writers, but this was not verified here); law firms on TikTok and bar-association advertising limits; healthcare accounts (HIPAA); local HVAC/plumbing viral accounts; or real estate agents who went viral with humor (e.g., comedic listing videos). These need follow-up.
- I could not verify the full text of S.C. Code § 40-57-135 or SC Reg. 105 advertising rules (snippets only). Verify directly with SC LLR.
- Lemonade compliance process: no source.

## 3. Backfires: tragedy jokes, politics, punching down, culture-war controversy, AI backlash, brand fatigue

### Takeaway
Most damage comes from four sources: (1) pre-scheduled or reflexive "relatable" posts during tragedies; (2) branding a tribute (logo or product in a death or anniversary post); (3) culture-war flashpoints where a brand appears to change its identity on its core audience (Bud Light, Cracker Barrel, Jaguar), which have measurable sales and stock costs; (4) a gap between persona and corporate action (Duolingo's playful owl versus the "AI-first" memo). The audience punished the account because the company's real behavior contradicted the voice. Accounts built on edge also tend to go "skittish" after a backlash, which by the CEO's own account cost Duolingo growth.

### Cited Findings

**Duolingo "AI-first" backlash (2025)**
- After CEO Luis von Ahn's April 2025 "AI-first" memo (contractors would be replaced by AI), Duolingo wiped its TikTok and Instagram posts in mid-May 2025. It reportedly lost 400K+ TikTok followers, then came back with a cryptic in-character message that didn't address the controversy. [Follower loss is third-party reported] — [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2025/05/22/duolingo-deleted-their-instagram-and-tiktok-posts-after-getting-backlash-over-ai/); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/duolingo-grapples-with-its-ai-first-promise-before-angry-social-mob/); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/events/duolingos-ai-first-controversy)
- On the earnings call, von Ahn said "stopping edgy posts" helped turn sentiment positive but may have hurt DAU. The company guided 40-45% DAU growth for Q2 2025 and landed at 40%, the bottom of the range. He said: "We have recovered sentiment, but we are not taking as many risks because honestly, we're skittish about it." [MEASURED guidance vs. actual; causal claim is the CEO's] — [AOL/Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/duolingos-ceo-says-learned-hard-062205679.html)
- Duolingo still reported Q3 2025 DAU of 50M+ (+36% YoY) and revenue +41%. Later reports show 58.7M DAU (+23% YoY) in Q2 2026, which suggests growth slowed but the business wasn't broken. [MEASURED, company release] — [Duolingo IR, Q3 2025](https://duolingo.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-surpasses-50-million-daily-active-users-grows-dau-36); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/duolingo-daily-active-users-reach-58-7-million-as-retention-hits-record-84-and-social-accounts-top-1-billion-organic-impressions/)

**Bud Light / Dylan Mulvaney (2023)**

### Inferences
- The two backfire types relevant to a Myrtle Beach brokerage are: (a) hurricane, flood and drowning-season posts (rip currents kill people on the Grand Strand every summer) and local tragedies, where a scheduled jokey post or a branded "thoughts and prayers" would be punished; (b) politics and culture war. Bud Light, Cracker Barrel and Jaguar show the damage comes from appearing to change identity on the core customer, not from a single joke. For a brokerage serving retirees, military, golf, out-of-state relocators and investors, staying out of partisan topics is the clear rule.
- The Duolingo lesson is consistency between voice and conduct. If the brokerage is "the honest insider," then a hidden fee, a fair-housing complaint or a pushy sales practice will be framed against that persona and will hurt more.
- Have a "kill switch" protocol: pause all scheduled posts when a hurricane watch or warning is issued, when there's a local fatality, or during national tragedies.

## 4. Research on humor and controversy in marketing

### Takeaway
Consumers say they want humor (Oracle 2022: about 91% prefer funny brands), but when asked what matters most on social they rank honesty, helpfulness and responsiveness above humor, and only about a third want snark (Sprout 2017/2023). Controversy drives conversation only up to a moderate level (Chen & Berger 2013). Past that, discomfort suppresses sharing. Most B2B creative is emotionally flat, and buyers say ads lack humor (LinkedIn/System1/Magna), which suggests professional-services categories are under-supplied with warmth and humor.

### Cited Findings
- **Oracle & Gretchen Rubin "Happiness Report" (June 2022):** 12,183 consumers and 3,125 business leaders across 14 countries (Savanta, January 2022). 91% prefer brands to be funny, 72% would choose a brand that uses humor over competitors, and 80% are more likely to buy again from a brand that uses humor. Only 20% of brands use humor offline and 18% online. 95% of business leaders fear using humor in customer interactions, and 85% lack the data and tools to do it well. Regional figures vary. [MEASURED survey, vendor-sponsored] — [Oracle press release](https://oracle.com/au/news/announcement/oracle-cx-happiness-research-study-2022-06-15); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/happiness-marketing-brand-consumer-oracle/625554/)
- **Sprout Social Index Q2 2017 ("#BrandsGetReal"):** 86% want honesty from brands on social. Friendly, helpful and funny follow. Only about a third want brands to bring snark "full force." Personality is preferred on Facebook (83%) versus LinkedIn (33%). Consumers prefer help over witty comebacks. [MEASURED survey, vendor-sponsored] — [Sprout investor release](https://investors.sproutsocial.com/news/news-details/2017/Data-Consumers-Value-Customer-Service-over-Attitude-from-Brands-on-Social/default.aspx); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/social-business/new-research-shows-consumers-prefer-help-over-witty-comebacks)
- **Sprout Social Index 2023:** 51% say the most memorable thing a brand can do on social is simply respond. 1 in 4 say speaking out on social issues makes a brand memorable. — [TipRanks / Sprout release](https://www.tipranks.com/news/press-releases/new-research-indicates-a-shift-in-what-consumers-find-memorable-on-social-media); [African Marketing Confederation](https://africanmarketingconfederation.org/theres-a-shift-in-what-consumers-find-memorable-on-social-media/)
- **Sprout Social Index 2025 (Edition XX):** 4,000+ consumers, 900 practitioners, 300 leaders, focused on memorable brands. Specific personality figures were not retrieved. — [Sprout Social Index](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/index)
- **Chen & Berger, "When, Why, and How Controversy Causes Conversation," *Journal of Consumer Research* (Oct 2013):** controversy increases the likelihood of discussion at low levels but decreases it beyond a moderate level. It raises interest (more talk) and discomfort (less talk) at the same time. Anonymity and talking with friends rather than strangers reduce the discomfort. Field data comes from 200+ Topix.com articles, where moderately controversial ones drew the most comments. Advice: "avoid evoking more than a moderate level of controversy." [MEASURED, peer-reviewed] — [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/handle/20.500.14332/39527); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/781447); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/finding-companys-controversy-sweet-spot/)
- **LinkedIn B2B Institute + System1:** 1,600 ads tested on 6M people. About 75% of B2B ads scored 1 star (lowest) for emotional response and long-term growth potential, and 77% of B2B vs. 53% of B2C ads scored 1/5. [MEASURED] — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/01/08/after-decades-consistent-decline-humor-advertising-roaring-back-2024); [Digital Leadership Associates](https://digital-leadership-associates.passle.net/post/102i3c7/analysis-from-linkedins-b2b-institute-of-more-than-600-b2b-ads-found-that-71-of)
- **LinkedIn/Magna:** 64% of business decision makers say B2B ads lack humor, 60% say they lack emotion, and 59% see no relatable characters. Creative ads drove 40% higher purchase consideration. [MEASURED, vendor-sponsored] — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/creative-b2b-ads-yield-40-higher-purchase-consideration-says-new-linkedin-magna-study)
- Industry commentary (The Drum, 2024) says humor in advertising is "roaring back" after decades of decline. Cannes Lions added a Humor category in 2024. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/01/08/after-decades-consistent-decline-humor-advertising-roaring-back-2024); [Brands Vietnam](https://www.brandsvietnam.com/congdong/topic/338219-Vi-sao-Cannes-Lions-bo-sung-hang-muc-Humor-trong-nam-2024)

### Inferences
- For a brokerage, "be helpful first, funny second" is backed by data. Fast responses to comments and DMs are themselves the "memorable" action (Sprout 2023), and humor raises preference (Oracle) as long as it isn't snark aimed at customers.
- Following Chen & Berger, mild contrarian takes ("Hot take: you don't need an ocean view to make money on a rental," "Myrtle Beach is not overpriced, here's why") sit in the productive "moderate controversy" zone. Political, cultural or identity topics push past it into discomfort.
- Platform matters. Personality plays better on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok than on LinkedIn, so it makes sense to tone the voice down for LinkedIn.

- Specific findings from the 2024/2025 Sprout Index on personality, humor or "brands acting like people" weren't retrieved.
- No academic research found specifically on humor in real estate or other regulated-services social media.

## 5. Persona frameworks: mascot vs founder vs "intern" voice; archetypes; which survive staff turnover

### Takeaway
Voice ownership comes in three models. **Character/mascot** (Duo, the Old Spice Guy, Bangor's Duck of Justice, and by background knowledge Flo and the GEICO gecko) survives turnover best, because the voice lives in a documented character rather than a person. Even so, Duolingo's character took the hit for corporate actions. **Founder/face voice** (Scrub Daddy's Krause, Ryan Reynolds for Aviation, Brimfield's police chief) has the highest trust and conversion but leaves when the person leaves, and it can be sold (Aviation). **"Social manager as auteur"** voice (Wendy's under Amy Brown, Steak-umm under Allebach, Duolingo under Parvez, Ryanair under Corcoran) produces breakout virality but is fragile. Each of those brands lost or diluted the voice when the writer left. The policing case study says outright that you need a formal team, not one manager.

### Cited Findings
- The *Policing* journal case study says agencies with informal voices need a formal team for consistency and to avoid relying on a single account manager. — [AMiner](https://aminer.org/pub/5e5e192d93d709897ce58e38/small-police-agency-use-of-social-media-positive-and-negative-outcomes-noted)
- Turnover events: Wendy's Amy Brown left after about four years, shortly after the 2017 peak. Steak-umm's Allebach ran it for about four years, until Dec 2021. Ryanair's head of social resigned publicly in 2023. Duolingo's Parvez left after more than five years. — [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/unclick/wendys-twitter-burn-interview/); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/nathan-allebach-behind-the-legendary-steak-umm-twitter-reveals-how-the-brand-immersed-itself-in-internet-culture-and-avoided-becoming-how-do-you-do-fellow-kids); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2023/11/10/lessons-ryanair-the-art-and-rules-going-gonzo-tiktok); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/)
- Character continuity: after Brown left, Wendy's kept the "challenger with charm" voice and institutionalized it as an annual National Roast Day, later moved to TikTok in 2023. This suggests that a written voice plus a ritual outlasts the writer. — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/wendys-national-roast-day-tiktok); [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/wendys-twitter-burn-interview)
- A mascot can carry big narrative stunts (Duo's "death" and "resurrection"). It also absorbs corporate backlash (the AI-first posts were deleted). — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/duolingo-says-its-mascot-duo-the-owl-is-dead/486944); [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2025/05/22/duolingo-deleted-their-instagram-and-tiktok-posts-after-getting-backlash-over-ai/)
- Founder voice: Scrub Daddy's CEO reads comments himself. Aviation's value was tied to Reynolds, who kept an interest after the $610M sale. — [Business Insider India](https://businessinsider.in/retail/news/scrub-daddy-ceo-only-sleeps-like-3-hours-per-night-because-hes-reading-all-the-viral-brands-social-media-comments/amp_articleshow/90988407.cms); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ryan-reynolds-aviation-american-gin-bought-diageo)

### Inferences (archetype fit for a Myrtle Beach brokerage)
- **Honest insider** (Steak-umm/Ryanair candor): "what agents won't tell you about flood insurance and HOA fees." This is the strongest fit for a trust-based, regulated business. It turns compliance-safe education into content and survives turnover if the rules are written down.
- **Local know-it-all** (police-page formula): Grand Strand traffic, tourist-season survival, hurricane prep, "where locals actually eat." It builds a following beyond buyers and sellers. Avoid neighborhood demographic comments for fair-housing reasons.
- **Contrarian analyst** (Chen & Berger moderate controversy): mild market hot takes backed by MLS data. It must avoid guarantees and investment-return promises, and any market stats need dates and sources.
- **Grumpy old pro / lovable mess:** these work as recurring characters (a mascot like a pelican, a golf cart or the "Duck of Justice" equivalent) or as a self-deprecating founder. The mascot variant survives staff turnover best. The grumpy variant risks reading as punching down at clients and should only roast the market, the weather and the agent themselves, never clients.
- Recommended hybrid: a **written character bible** (voice rules, banned topics, BIC pre-approval) owned by the brokerage, presented through a **founder or broker face** for trust, with **recurring formats/tentpoles** (e.g., an annual "Roast My Listing Photos" day where sellers opt in, a weekly myth-buster). Two or more trained writers prevent single-manager dependency, per the *Policing* finding.
- Approval design: one designated approver (as at Duolingo). The broker-in-charge pre-approves the voice guide (Monzo). There's a mandatory compliance check for anything on listings, prices, neighborhoods or financing, and a hurricane/tragedy pause protocol.

### Gaps
- No formal study found comparing turnover survival of mascot vs. founder vs. manager voices. The conclusions above come from case patterns.
- No documented case found of a real estate brokerage persona account with measured results. This is a significant gap. Suggest separate research on viral real estate agents and teams (e.g., comedy listing videos and agent TikTok creators) and on their compliance problems.
- Progressive's Flo and GEICO's gecko were not verified as social-first personas this session.
