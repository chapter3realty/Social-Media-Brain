Scope: South Carolina and US federal law, current as of November 2026. Business: Chapter3 Realty, a South Carolina real estate brokerage (Myrtle Beach; Broker-in-Charge Timmy Fredrick Nash). It does no lending. It has a business relationship with an affiliated lender, BrickWood Mortgage. One staff member, Devin Day, is a licensed mortgage loan originator (MLO). ## 1. South Carolina Real Estate Commission advertising rules (S.C. Code Title 40, Chapter 57)

### Takeaway
After the 2024 rewrite of the Real Estate Practice Act (H.4754), effective for advertising on May 15, 2025, every ad in any medium must clearly identify the brokerage firm's full name. That includes general "about the area" or trend posts on social media. The firm may meet this online with a link to its homepage. **The current statute does not require a telephone number.** Advertising another firm's listing, its photos, or even its address requires written authorization from the listing firm (owner consent can sit in the listing agreement), plus clear and conspicuous credit to the listing firm. The Broker-in-Charge is personally responsible for supervising compliance. Fines run up to $10,000 per violation.

### Cited Findings
**Firm-name rule (applies to every post)**
- § 40-57-135(E)(2) (text as quoted by the Commission): "When advertising his real estate services or marketing real estate owned, in whole or in part, by another person in any medium, a licensee clearly must: (a) Identify the full name of the real estate brokerage firm with which the licensee is employed and supervised in accordance with regulations. (b) If advertising on the Internet or in another electronic media, the above requirements may be met by including a link from the advertisement to the homepage of the brokerage firm or property management company." [fetched] — [SC REC, Reminder of Advertising Laws, June 12, 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf); [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)
- The Commission says § 40-57-135(E)(2) covers a licensee advertising "the licensee's own services generally," not only listings. Its examples include "A licensee making a social media post regarding an area of town generally" and "A licensee making a social media post regarding trends, without including any identifying features or specific properties." Subsection (E)(2) took effect May 15, 2025. [fetched] — [SC REC Advertising Memo updated after H.4754, June 2024](https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/News/AdvertisingMemo-H.4754.pdf)
- § 40-57-135(E)(3): "If a real estate brokerage firm operates under a trade or franchise name, the identity of the franchisee or holder of the trade name clearly must be revealed." [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- **Telephone number:** The current text of § 40-57-135(E) asks for the full firm name or a homepage link, not a phone number. The only telephone-number duty found in § 40-57-135 is (A)(8): the Broker-in-Charge must notify the Commission within ten days of any change to the office name, address, email, or telephone number. [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php). Third-party exam-prep sites still say "name and phone number." That appears to be outdated or loose, and nothing official found supports it. — [estatepass.ai (secondary, unreliable)](https://www.estatepass.ai/questions/under-sc-real-estate-advertising-rules-all-advertising-by-a-licensee-must-includ/)

**License status in personal deals**
- § 40-57-135(F)(1) (paraphrase from fetched statute text): in personal transactions, a licensee must clearly reveal license status at the first substantive contact, in all advertising in any media, and on the first page of certain contracts in bold, underlined capitals. [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)

**Another firm's listings, addresses, and photos**
- § 40-57-135(E)(1): a licensee may not advertise or market someone else's real estate without a written listing agreement. "Licensees not associated with the listing brokerage firm may advertise real estate owned, in whole or in part, by another person only if they have written authorization from the listing brokerage firm and acknowledge the listing brokerage firm in the advertisement in a clear and conspicuous way. Authorization may be contained and obtained from the owner through the listing agreement." [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- The Commission's 2025 reminder says complaints about unauthorized use of listings and property imagery are increasing. "Even if no imagery is used, simply referencing a property's address in any form—including social media captions, blogs, newsletters, print advertising, postcards, 'buyer success' reels, or market updates—is a form of advertising." Without authorization this is "prohibited by S.C. Code §40-57-135(E)(1)." [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- The reminder lists these as violations: "Sharing another brokerage's social media posting for a property without obtaining compliant written authorization," and MLS-derived marketing that "could give the appearance that your brokerage represents identified listings of another brokerage." This is distinguished from an IDX feed on the brokerage website. [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- Approved example from the Commission: "WOW! Look at this beautiful property just listed by XYZ Real Estate. Call me at ABC Real Estate if I can help you set up a tour of this gem." Written permission "can be as simple as an e-mail" to the listing agent, with the agent confirming that the listing agreement authorizes it. [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- The Commission says property-related information ("images, videos, data, location, drone footage, and remarks") must not be used "for branding or promotional purposes without written permission from the listing brokerage and property owner." This applies even to homes not for sale: filming someone's house for social content requires the homeowner's written permission. [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- Claims such as "We sold this home," "Secured for our buyers," or "Closed with our team" are allowed only when the advertising brokerage "had a direct and documented role in the transaction." The reminder cites § 40-57-710(A)(4) and (5), and also (A)(3), (21), (22), and (24). [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)
- On edits or comments added when reposting a listing: "It is the Commission's position that the licensee making the comments and/or edits would likely be found in violation rather than the listing brokerage firm." The Commission names fair housing as an example. [fetched] — [SC REC Advertising Memo 2024](https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/News/AdvertisingMemo-H.4754.pdf)

**Teams**
- § 40-57-360(D), current version (effective until May 15, 2027): "Team advertising must contain the team name and the full name of the real estate brokerage firm." (E): "Team names may not include the terms 'realty', 'real estate', 'realtors', or similar terms suggesting a brokerage." [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)
- § 40-57-360, version effective May 15, 2027: "All team advertising and marketing must contain the full name of the real estate brokerage firm." The team name may not be "more than twice the size, prominence, or frequency" of the firm name, and team names must end in a format that includes the firm's name. Exact wording to be confirmed. [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)

**Misleading statements and discrimination as grounds for discipline**
- § 40-57-710(A)(2) "makes a substantial misrepresentation involving a real estate transaction"; (A)(3) "makes false promises likely to influence, persuade, or induce"; (A)(4) "pursues a continued and flagrant course of misrepresentation or makes false and misleading promises through any medium of advertising or otherwise"; (A)(9) "is convicted of violating the federal and state fair housing laws." [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)
- § 40-57-350(G)(1): "A licensee shall treat all parties honestly and may not knowingly give them false or misleading information about the condition of the property." The firm's written policy must cover "compliance with state and federal fair housing laws" (§ 40-57-350(B)(6)). [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)

**Broker-in-Charge responsibility**
- § 40-57-30(7): the Broker-in-Charge is responsible for "ensuring compliance with all applicable laws and regulations." § 40-57-135(A)(1): the BIC shall "adequately supervise employees or supervised licensees to ensure their compliance." Under § 40-57-710(B), the BIC must attend a supervised licensee's disciplinary hearing or face discipline personally. [fetched] — [SC REC Advertising Memo 2024](https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/News/AdvertisingMemo-H.4754.pdf)

**Records**
- § 40-57-135(L) (paraphrase): licensees are not required to keep records of communications not designated for retention, including text messages, voicemail, and social media posts. [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)

**Penalties**
- § 40-57-720(E)(2): fine of "not more than ten thousand dollars for each violation." § 40-57-725(C) citations: first violation up to $500, second within five years up to $1,000, third or later up to $10,000. § 40-57-730(A): after revocation, a person "may not reapply for three years." Suspension and revocation run through § 40-1-120 (not fetched). [fetched] — [SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)

### Inferences
- The SC rule is stricter than many states for "roast the listing" content. Screenshotting a local listing that belongs to another firm to mock its décor ("Zillow Gone Wild" style), or naming its address in a joke, is advertising under the Commission's reading of § 40-57-135(E)(1) unless the listing firm gives written authorization and gets clear credit. So it is a license-law problem, not just a copyright problem.
- A "link in bio" may not satisfy (E)(2)(b), which speaks of "a link **from the advertisement**." The safer reading is to put "Chapter3 Realty" in every caption, on-screen graphic, or video watermark. A homepage link in each post also works where the platform allows clickable links.
- Duets, stitches, and reshares of another agent's listing video count as "sharing another brokerage's social media posting." They need written permission and the "listed by [Firm]" credit.
- Fair housing violations added in a repost caption fall on the poster (Commission position). A funny caption on someone else's listing is Chapter3's own exposure.
- Each post may count as a separate violation for fine purposes ("for each violation"). This is an inference; no case counting individual posts was found.

### Rule checks
- R1.1 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-135(E)(2)): the post text, image, or video must contain the exact string "Chapter3 Realty" (the registered firm name), or a link to the Chapter3 homepage. Recommended: require the name in every post regardless of links.
- R1.2 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-135(E)(2)): every profile on every platform (bio, page name, about section) shows "Chapter3 Realty." Agent profiles that advertise services must also show it.
- R1.3 **BEST PRACTICE**: include a phone number. It is not required by current statute but is helpful and was historically expected.
- R1.4 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-135(E)(1)): if the post contains a street address, an MLS number, listing photos, drone footage, or a reshare of a property not listed by Chapter3, block it unless (a) written authorization from the listing firm is on file and (b) the caption contains "listed by [Listing Firm full name]."
- R1.5 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-135(E); Commission guidance): footage or photos of any identifiable private home not listed by Chapter3 need the owner's written permission on file.
- R1.6 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-710(A)(4)–(5)): claims of "sold," "closed," "our buyers," "our listing," or a sales-volume or ranking claim must match a documented Chapter3 transaction or data source.
- R1.7 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-360(D)–(E)): any team name must appear alongside "Chapter3 Realty" and may not contain "Realty," "Real Estate," or "Realtors." From May 15, 2027, the team name may be at most twice as prominent as the firm name.
- R1.8 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-135(F)(1)): a post about a property owned by a Chapter3 licensee must disclose license status.
- R1.9 **BINDING** (§ 40-57-135(A)(1)): the BIC (Timmy Fredrick Nash) or a delegate approves posts, and an approval log is kept. The log is best practice; supervision is binding.

## 2. Fair housing: Fair Housing Act § 3604(c), HUD advertising rules and guidance, Meta targeting, SC Fair Housing Law, and 55+ (HOPA) advertising

### Takeaway
It is illegal for anyone, licensed or not, to publish any statement about selling or renting a dwelling that "indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination" based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, or national origin. Choosing ad media or audiences to exclude a protected group is itself a violation. Humor is not a defense: the test is how an "ordinary reader" would read the message. For a retiree-focused brokerage, the biggest exposure is familial status (children under 18). Only a qualifying HOPA 55+ community may be advertised as adults-only, and on Meta housing ads cannot be targeted by age. HUD's 2024 digital ad guidance was withdrawn effective September 17, 2025, but the statute and the 1989 regulations still bind.

### Cited Findings
- 24 CFR 100.75(a): "It shall be unlawful to make, print or publish, or cause to be made, printed or published, any notice, statement or advertisement with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling which indicates any preference, limitation or discrimination because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin." [fetched] — [24 CFR 100.75 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75)
- 24 CFR 100.75(b): the rule applies "to all written or oral notices or statements by a person." [fetched] — [24 CFR 100.75](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75)
- 24 CFR 100.75(c) gives these examples:
  - (c)(1) "Using words, phrases, photographs, illustrations, symbols or forms which convey that dwellings are available or not available to a particular group of persons"
  - (c)(2) expressing a preference to agents or others
  - (c)(3) "Selecting media or locations for advertising the sale or rental of dwellings which deny particular segments of the housing market information about housing opportunities"
  - (c)(4) refusing to publish or charging differently for advertising

  [fetched] — [24 CFR 100.75](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75)
- The statute: 42 U.S.C. § 3604(c). The single-family and owner-occupant exemptions in § 3603(b) do not exempt anyone from § 3604(c). [canonical, not re-fetched] — [42 U.S.C. § 3604](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604); [42 U.S.C. § 3603](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3603)
- HUD "Achtenberg memo" (Roberta Achtenberg, "Guidance Regarding Advertisements Under § 804(c) of the Fair Housing Act," January 9, 1995). Ads should describe the property, not the people.
  - Acceptable: "master bedroom," "family room," "desirable neighborhood," "great view," "walk-in closets," "jogging trails," "walk to bus stop."
  - Violations: "adults only," "no children," "Christian home," "no wheelchairs." Racially or ethnically coded descriptions of a neighborhood's residents are also violations.
  - Secular holiday greetings and naming a nearby landmark church as a location reference are generally acceptable, as long as they do not imply a religious preference.

  [canonical, not re-fetched; contents from background knowledge] — [HUD Achtenberg memo PDF](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/DOC_7784.PDF)
- HUD's April 29, 2024 guidance on applying the FHA to advertising through digital platforms (algorithmic ad targeting and delivery) was among eight FHEO guidance documents withdrawn by a Federal Register notice published April 6, 2026, retroactive to September 17, 2025. The withdrawn documents were described as non-binding. The Fair Housing Act itself is unchanged. [fetched via secondary] — [Orrick InfoBytes, April 10, 2026](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-04-10/hud-withdraws-eight-fair-housing-guidance-documents-as-part-of-deregulatory-review/); [Federal Register public inspection 2026-06624](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-06624.pdf); [NAHRO](https://www.nahro.org/news/hud-withdraws-eight-fair-housing-notices)
- *United States v. Meta Platforms* (S.D.N.Y., June 2022) settled DOJ's claim that Facebook's ad delivery system, including "Special Ad Audience," discriminated in housing ads under § 3604(c). Meta agreed to drop the tool, build a "Variance Reduction System," and pay the maximum FHA civil penalty. Earlier (March 2019 civil-rights settlements), Meta created the Housing "Special Ad Category," which bars targeting housing ads by age, gender, or ZIP code. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [DOJ press release, June 21, 2022](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-secures-groundbreaking-settlement-agreement-meta-platforms-formerly-known)
- SC Fair Housing Law § 31-21-40(3) makes it unlawful to "make, print, or publish... any notice, statement, or advertisement with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling" showing preference or limitation based on "race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin." [fetched] — [SC Code Title 31 Ch. 21](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t31c021.php)
- SC remedies: the SC Human Affairs Commission may order damages and civil penalties capped at federal FHA levels (§ 31-21-130(L)). Courts may award actual and punitive damages and attorney's fees (§ 31-21-140(B)). [fetched] — [SC Code Title 31 Ch. 21](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t31c021.php)
- SC "housing for older persons": § 31-21-30(8) covers housing intended and operated for occupancy by at least one person 55 or older per unit, with at least 80% of units so occupied, plus 62-and-older and government-program housing. § 31-21-70(L): "No provision in this chapter regarding familial status applies to housing for older persons." [fetched] — [SC Code Title 31 Ch. 21](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t31c021.php)
- Federal HOPA exemption: 42 U.S.C. § 3607(b)(2)(C) and 24 CFR 100.304–100.308. The community must show intent to operate as 55+, including through how it is described and advertised (24 CFR 100.306), and must verify age occupancy (24 CFR 100.307). [canonical, not re-fetched] — [42 U.S.C. § 3607](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3607); [24 CFR 100.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.306)
- Steering is separately prohibited: 24 CFR 100.70 covers discouraging people from, or directing them toward, neighborhoods because of protected class. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [24 CFR 100.70](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.70)
- NAR SoP 10-1 (amended June 5, 2025) bars REALTORS from volunteering "information regarding the racial, religious or ethnic composition of any neighborhood," and from panic selling. It allows "other demographic information." SoP 10-3 bars ads indicating a discriminatory preference. [fetched] — [NAR 2026 Code of Ethics](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics/2026-code-of-ethics-standards-of-practice)
- NAR Case Interpretation 10-11 (revised for 2026): a listing broker who published listing photos with a prominently displayed Confederate flag violated Article 10 (SoP 10-3 and 10-5). The broker "is indeed responsible for content he displays publicly when engaging in real estate brokerage." - Federal penalties: HUD administrative civil penalties under 42 U.S.C. § 3612(g)(3), inflation-adjusted at 24 CFR 180.671. DOJ pattern-or-practice suits under § 3614. Private suits under § 3613. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [24 CFR 180.671](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/180.671)

### Inferences
- **Age vs. familial status.** Age is not a protected class under the FHA or SC § 31-21-40, but familial status (households with children under 18) is. "Perfect for retirees," "empty-nester paradise," "no screaming kids," and "quiet adult neighborhood" can each be read as "not for families with children." That is a familial-status preference unless the property is a qualifying 55+ community. No HUD or court ruling was found that specifically condemns "empty nesters." Treating it as high-risk is best practice, built on the "ordinary reader" standard and the Achtenberg memo's "adults only" example.
- **Safe framing for the retiree niche.** Describe amenities and property features rather than the type of people: "single-level living," "no-stairs floor plan," "golf course views," "near hospitals and the beach." For real HOPA communities, "55+ community" is permitted, ideally after confirming the community's 55+ status.
- **Targeting is a § 100.75(c)(3) issue, independent of HUD's withdrawn 2024 guidance.** Running Chapter3 housing ads only to people 55+, or excluding ZIP codes, is "selecting media... which deny particular segments." On Meta it is also blocked by the Housing Special Ad Category, a platform contract term.
- **How humor becomes steering.** Jokes that sort neighborhoods by who lives there are statements indicating preference, even when meant ironically. Examples: "Move here if you want to avoid the spring-break crowd from [X]"; "[Area] — where the HOA is all Bible study"; "the part of town you don't want to end up in after dark"; "[Neighborhood] has a lot of… culture, if you know what I mean."
- **Allowed.** Roasting property features (popcorn ceilings, carpet in bathrooms), weather, traffic, tourists in general, and local food, as long as nothing ties back to a protected class.
- **Schools, crime, and safety.** No federal rule bans mentioning schools or crime. But "safe neighborhood," "good schools," and "low crime" ratings have long served as racial or familial proxies, and fair housing trainers advise linking to objective third-party sources instead of making characterizations. This is best practice.
- **Churches.** Naming a church as a landmark ("across from First Baptist") is generally acceptable. "Great Christian neighborhood" or "walk to church every Sunday!" as a selling point implies a religious preference.

### Rule checks
- R2.1 **BINDING** (§ 3604(c); 24 CFR 100.75; § 31-21-40(3)): flag any post about housing, an area, or neighborhoods that mentions or implies a protected class, whether about people living there or a preferred buyer. The seven classes are race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, and national origin. Flag words for people (e.g., "families," "kids," "adults," "singles," "bachelor," "Christian," "Hispanic," "immigrants," "ethnic," "wheelchair," "handicapped," "able-bodied," "mature"). Also flag image-based cues, including models, symbols, and flags.
- R2.2 **BINDING** (familial status; 42 U.S.C. § 3607(b)): block "adults only," "no children," "no kids," and "child-free," and their joke versions ("no tiny humans"), unless the property is flagged as a verified HOPA 55+ community. Flag "empty nesters," "retirees only," "perfect for retirees," "quiet adult community," and "family-friendly" for human review (BEST PRACTICE threshold).
- R2.3 **BINDING** (24 CFR 100.75(c)(3); Meta Housing Special Ad Category): paid housing ads must use the platform's housing category. No age, gender, or ZIP exclusion targeting, and no lookalike audiences built from age-skewed seed lists.
- R2.4 **BEST PRACTICE**: flag "safe," "unsafe," "sketchy," "bad part of town," "ghetto," "hood," "crime," "good schools," "best schools," "exclusive," "restricted," and "private community" for human review. Require an objective source link for school or crime data.
- R2.5 **BINDING**: a joke that contrasts neighborhoods by their residents ("people there are…") is blocked. A joke about a neighborhood's things (traffic, golf carts, Spanish moss, bad parking) is allowed.
- R2.6 **BINDING** (NAR SoP 10-1, for NAR members; best practice otherwise): no volunteering racial, religious, or ethnic composition of any area, including in jokes or "local culture" explainers.
- R2.7 **BINDING** (NAR SoP 10-3 and Case 10-11, for NAR members; best practice otherwise): screen listing photos and reshared images for symbols that could convey preference, such as Confederate flags or hate symbols.

## 3. NAR Code of Ethics (applies only if Chapter3 or its licensees are NAR members)

### Takeaway
For members, Article 15 (with SoPs 15-2 and 15-3) directly limits roasting competitors. No false or misleading statements about other real estate professionals, including reposting someone else's false claim, and a duty to remove or correct once known false. Article 12 requires a "true picture," professional status that is "readily apparent," and the firm name in all ads (SoP 12-5). Since June 5, 2025, NAR has removed "hate speech" from SoP 10-5 and narrowed it to harassment, and the whole Code applies only to professional-capacity conduct. A business brand account is always professional capacity. A non-member can't be disciplined under the Code but can't use the REALTOR® marks, and Article 15-type statements still carry defamation and § 40-57-710 risk.

### Cited Findings
- The 2026 Code of Ethics and Standards of Practice took effect January 1, 2026. [fetched] — [NAR 2026 Code of Ethics](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics/2026-code-of-ethics-standards-of-practice)
- Article 12: "REALTORS® shall be honest and truthful in their real estate communications and shall present a true picture in their advertising, marketing, and other representations. REALTORS® shall ensure that their status as real estate professionals is readily apparent in their advertising, marketing, and other representations..." [fetched] — [NAR 2026 Code](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics/2026-code-of-ethics-standards-of-practice)

  - "Agent Bob lost his client $40k"
  - "they lie about their sales numbers"

### Rule checks

- R3.4 **BINDING for members** (SoP 12-1): never say "free" for brokerage services.
- R3.5 **BINDING for members** (SoP 10-5): no content harassing anyone based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin, sexual orientation, or gender identity. Applies to staff personal accounts that function as business accounts.
- R3.6 **BINDING** (NAR trademark rules; applies if non-member): do not use "REALTOR®" or "REALTORS®" unless the individual or firm is a member. Never use it as a generic word for "agent."

## 4. RESPA Section 8 and co-marketing with the affiliated lender (BrickWood Mortgage)

### Takeaway
Chapter3 can promote BrickWood on social media. But nothing of value can flow to Chapter3 for referring settlement business, unless it is a properly disclosed Affiliated Business Arrangement (AfBA) paid only as a return on ownership, or a fair-market-value payment for services actually performed. Free or subsidized ads, a lender paying more than its share of co-branded posts, and leads swapped for "likes" or boosts are classic Section 8 problems. If the relationship is ownership-based, a written AfBA disclosure must reach the consumer at or before the referral, and a social post that merely advertises is not itself a referral.

### Cited Findings
- 12 U.S.C. § 2607(a): no person shall give or accept "any fee, kickback, or thing of value" under an agreement to refer settlement service business on a federally related mortgage loan. § 2607(b) bars unearned fee splits. § 2607(c)(4) is the AfBA exemption. It requires disclosure of the relationship and an estimate of charges, no required use of the affiliate, and nothing of value other than a return on ownership. § 2607(d): criminal penalties up to $10,000 and/or one year; civil liability of three times the charge. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [12 U.S.C. § 2607](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/2607)
- 12 CFR 1024.14 (Regulation X) defines "thing of value" broadly, including payments, advances, services, and the opportunity to participate in a money-making program. It says a referral includes any oral or written action that affirmatively influences selection of a settlement service provider. 12 CFR 1024.15 and Appendix D cover the AfBA disclosure format and timing. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [12 CFR 1024.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1024.14); [12 CFR 1024.15](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1024.15)
- CFPB RESPA FAQs (October 2020) say co-marketing is permissible when each party pays its proportionate share and payments are not tied to referrals. Payments for marketing services must be fair market value for services actually performed. [canonical, not re-fetched; status in 2026 unverified] — [CFPB RESPA FAQs](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act-faqs/)

### Inferences
- **Social posts that promote BrickWood.** Posts like "Our friends at BrickWood got our client to the closing table in 21 days" are allowed if Chapter3 receives nothing of value from BrickWood for them, or pays or receives only fair-market value with documentation.
- **Ownership-based relationship.** If any Chapter3 owner holds an interest in BrickWood (an AfBA), posts should not pressure people to use BrickWood ("you must use our lender"). The § 2607(c)(4) disclosure must accompany any actual referral, such as a DM handing a lead to BrickWood. Posts should say consumers are free to choose any lender.
- **Co-branded paid ads.** Split costs pro rata to each party's share of the ad's space and benefit, and keep invoices.
- **Devin Day, the dual role.** If Devin is also a real estate licensee at Chapter3 and an MLO at BrickWood, a joint post where he pitches both creates referral-fee questions. Get compliance review of his compensation arrangement. This is an inference.

### Rule checks
- R4.1 **BINDING** (§ 2607(a)): if a post mentions BrickWood or any lender, title company, attorney, inspector, or insurer, require a recorded answer to "Is anyone paying for or subsidizing this post or its boost?" If yes, there must be a fair-market-value agreement on file.
- R4.2 **BINDING** (§ 2607(c)(4); 12 CFR 1024.15), only if AfBA: a social DM or message that refers a specific consumer to BrickWood must carry or link the AfBA disclosure. Never use "required" or "must use."
- R4.3 **BEST PRACTICE**: include "You are free to choose any lender" on posts recommending BrickWood.
- R4.4 **BEST PRACTICE**: say "affiliated lender," "partner," or "we may have a business relationship" on BrickWood promotions (it also supports the FTC material-connection rule; see §6).

### Gaps
- The exact ownership structure between Chapter3 and BrickWood is unknown, and it decides whether the AfBA rules apply.
- It was not verified whether the CFPB withdrew its 2015 marketing-services-agreement bulletin or other RESPA guidance in its 2025 guidance rollback. The statute and Regulation X are unaffected either way.

## 5. Mortgage advertising: Regulation Z trigger terms, Regulation N (MAP Rule), SAFE Act/NMLS, and SC mortgage law

### Takeaway
Any person who advertises credit, including a real estate broker, must follow Regulation Z's ad rules. Mentioning a specific payment, down payment, number of payments or term, or finance charge in a closed-end mortgage ad ("only $1,900/month!", "3% down") triggers a full set of disclosures, including the APR. Rates must be stated as an "annual percentage rate." Regulation N bans misleading mortgage ad claims by anyone. South Carolina requires MLOs and mortgage licensees to show their NMLS unique identifier on all advertisements, including websites and business cards, which in practice includes social posts. Federal law requires the NMLS ID only on loan documents.

### Cited Findings
- 12 CFR 1026.24(d) (closed-end credit trigger terms): if an ad states the amount or percentage of any down payment, the number of payments or period of repayment, the amount of any payment, or the amount of any finance charge, it must also disclose (i) the down payment amount or percentage, (ii) the terms of repayment, and (iii) the "annual percentage rate," using that term, and whether it may increase. 1026.24(c): a rate must be stated as an APR (a simple rate may accompany it but not more prominently). 1026.24(f) covers dwelling-secured payment disclosures. 1026.24(i) prohibits misleading use of "fixed," misleading comparisons, implied government endorsement, misleading "counseling" claims, and other practices in closed-end dwelling-secured ads. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [12 CFR 1026.24 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.24)
- Reg Z official commentary 1026.2(a)(2)-2: the advertising rules apply to all persons, not just creditors. Real estate brokers are a classic example. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [12 CFR 1026 Supplement I, comment 2(a)(2)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/interp-2/)
- Regulation N (Mortgage Acts and Practices – Advertising), 12 CFR 1014.3, prohibits material misrepresentation in any commercial communication about mortgage credit. Examples include interest rates, fees, payment amounts, "no fees," fixed vs. variable, and government affiliation. 1014.5 requires keeping ads for 24 months. It applies to non-bank persons, including real estate brokers who advertise mortgage terms. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [12 CFR Part 1014](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1014)
- Federal: 12 CFR 1026.36(g) requires the loan originator's NMLS ID on the credit application, note, and security instrument, not on ads. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [12 CFR 1026.36](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.36)
- SC: § 37-22-210(F): "A licensee clearly shall display the unique identifier assigned by the Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry on all mortgage loan forms, solicitations, or advertisements including business cards or websites." § 37-22-110(4) defines "advertising" as "a commercial message in a medium that promotes, either directly or indirectly, a mortgage loan transaction." [fetched] — [SC Code Title 37 Ch. 22](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c022.php)
- SC Reg. 15-64(B)(2): the NMLS identifier of the licensed lender or servicer, and of the loan originator when the originator is included, must be used in all advertising. [fetched via search summary] — [S.C. Code Regs. 15-64 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/south-carolina/chapter-15/article-4/section-15-64); SC Consumer Finance guidance says all NMLS licensees must use their identifier in all advertising — [SC Consumer Finance, Maintaining Mortgage License](https://consumerfinance.sc.gov/sites/consumerfinance/files/Documents/Maintaining%20Mortgage%20License%2007-13-21.pdf)
- SC § 37-22-190(A):
  - (6) bars advertising "mortgage loans including rates, margins, discounts, points, fees, commissions, or other material information" unless the person can make the loans "available as advertised to qualified applicants"
  - (16) bars false advertising or misuse of names in violation of 18 U.S.C. § 709
  - (17) bars false advertising "through any material including, but not limited to, business card, stationery, or signage"

  § 37-22-140(N): "All advertisements of mortgage loans must comply with the Truth in Lending Act." Penalties: up to $10,000 per violation (§ 37-22-200(C)); a willful violation is a misdemeanor (§ 37-22-230). [fetched] — [SC Code Title 37 Ch. 22](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c022.php)

### Inferences
- **Chapter3's own posts.** Chapter3 is not a mortgage licensee, so § 37-22-210(F) does not bind Chapter3 directly. Reg Z and Reg N do apply to Chapter3's posts that state credit terms. Two examples:
  - "Rates just dropped to 5.9%!" needs the APR.
  - "Own this condo for $1,450/mo" is a trigger term and needs the down payment, terms, and APR.

  The safest pattern for a humor account is to never state payment, rate, or down-payment numbers. Point to BrickWood for a quote instead.
- **Devin Day's posts.** Any post by Devin, or any brand post featuring him, that promotes mortgages must show his individual NMLS ID and his sponsoring company's NMLS ID (§ 37-22-210(F); Reg. 15-64). Devin's social profiles should show his NMLS ID permanently.
- **BrickWood promotions.** A Chapter3 post promoting BrickWood's mortgage products is "advertising" that "indirectly" promotes a mortgage loan transaction. Including BrickWood's company NMLS ID is the low-cost safe choice. This is best practice, binding on BrickWood if it is BrickWood's ad.
- **Jokes with numbers.** "Rates are so low you'll cry" carries no trigger terms. "3% down, no excuses" is a trigger term ("percentage of any down payment").

### Rule checks
- R5.1 **BINDING** (12 CFR 1026.24(d)): regex-flag `\$\s?\d[\d,]*\s?(/|per)\s?(mo|month)`, `\d+(\.\d+)?\s?%\s?down`, `\d+[- ]?(year|yr)\s?(fixed|mortgage|loan|term)`, "monthly payment," "down payment of," "finance charge," "x payments." If any match, block unless the full disclosures are present (down payment, repayment terms, APR, whether the rate can increase) and verified by BrickWood compliance.
- R5.2 **BINDING** (12 CFR 1026.24(c)): any interest rate (`\d+(\.\d+)?\s?%` near "rate," "interest," or "mortgage") must be stated as "APR" or "annual percentage rate."
- R5.3 **BINDING** (12 CFR 1014.3; 1026.24(i)): block "no closing costs," "no fees," "guaranteed approval," "lowest rate in town," "fixed" (for an ARM), and "government loan"/"FHA-approved" implying endorsement, unless substantiated by BrickWood compliance.
- R5.4 **BINDING** (§ 37-22-210(F); Reg. 15-64): any post featuring Devin Day or promoting a mortgage product must include Devin's NMLS ID (if he appears) and the lender's company NMLS ID.
- R5.5 **BINDING** (12 CFR 1014.5): archive every mortgage-related post for at least 24 months.
- R5.6 **BEST PRACTICE**: route all mortgage-number content to BrickWood's compliance reviewer before posting.

## 6. FTC Endorsement Guides (2023) and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, 2024)

### Takeaway
Testimonials and reviews must be real, reflect honest opinions, and disclose any "material connection." That covers employees, agents, family, and paid creators praising Chapter3 or BrickWood. Since October 21, 2024, the FTC can seek civil penalties per violation for:
- fake reviews or testimonials, including AI-generated ones or ones by people who don't exist
- buying reviews conditioned on sentiment
- undisclosed insider reviews
- review suppression
- buying fake followers, views, or likes

### Cited Findings
- 16 CFR Part 465 (Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials) took effect October 21, 2024. Its sections:
  - 465.2: fake or false consumer reviews, consumer testimonials, or celebrity testimonials
  - 465.4: buying positive or negative reviews
  - 465.5: insider consumer reviews and testimonials
  - 465.6: company-controlled review websites
  - 465.7: review suppression
  - 465.8: misuse of fake indicators of social media influence

  [fetched: section titles] — [16 CFR Part 465 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-465); [Benesch, "Five Stars, Zero Tolerance"](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/)
- The FTC's announcement of the final rule (August 14, 2024) described the fake-review ban as covering reviews by people who don't exist, "such as AI-generated fake reviews," and reviews by people with no actual experience. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [FTC press release, Aug. 14, 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)
- The first public enforcement under the rule was warning letters to 10 companies in late 2025 that signaled civil penalties. No completed civil-penalty case was found. One source cites up to $51,744 per violation (a 2024 inflation-adjusted figure, adjusted annually). [fetched via search summaries] — [Venable, Dec. 2025](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new); [Arnold & Porter, Jan. 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2026/01/ftc-warning-letters-over-consumer-review-rule)
- The FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255), revised June 29, 2023:
  - endorsements must reflect honest opinions and actual use (255.1)
  - consumer endorsements must reflect typical results or clearly disclose otherwise (255.2)
  - material connections, including employment, family, and free or discounted services, must be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously" (255.5)
  - on social media, disclosures must be unavoidable and not hidden behind "more" or only in a profile

  [canonical, not re-fetched] — [16 CFR Part 255 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255); [FTC, Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)

### Inferences
- **Employee and agent posts.** Chapter3 agents posting "best brokerage in Myrtle Beach!" need a disclosure such as "I'm an agent at Chapter3" or #employee. Devin praising BrickWood must disclose his employment. Chapter3 praising BrickWood should disclose the business relationship.
- **Skits and AI content.** Comedy skits with actors playing "satisfied clients" must be clearly fictional or labeled "dramatization." A post must never present them as real reviews. AI-generated "client quotes," and AI avatars presented as real clients, are banned outright under 465.2.
- **Real reviews.** Client testimonials need the client's permission (privacy, and SC REC respect for the owner's control) and must be real. Selectively reposting only five-star reviews is allowed. Suppressing negative reviews by threats or legal intimidation is not (465.7).
- **Engagement.** Buying followers, likes, or views, or using engagement pods that fake influence, is prohibited under 465.8 when used to misrepresent influence for commercial purposes.
- **Giveaways.** "Review us for a chance to win" must not be conditioned on positive sentiment (465.4).

### Rule checks
- R6.1 **BINDING** (16 CFR 465.2): every testimonial or review in a post links to a verifiable source: a client record plus written consent, or a public review URL. AI-generated or composite testimonials are blocked. Skits must carry "Dramatization" or "Actors portrayed" labels.
- R6.2 **BINDING** (16 CFR 255.5; 465.5): if the speaker is an employee, agent, family member, or partner, or received anything of value, a disclosure ("#ad," "Chapter3 agent," "paid partnership," "BrickWood is our affiliated lender") must appear in the first lines or on-screen, not only in hashtags at the end.
- R6.3 **BINDING** (16 CFR 465.4): no review incentives conditioned on positive sentiment.
- R6.4 **BINDING** (16 CFR 465.8): no purchased followers, likes, views, or bot engagement.
- R6.5 **BINDING** (16 CFR 255.2): results claims ("sold in 3 days," "$50k over asking") need substantiation. If they are not typical, add a clarification.

## 7. Defamation, disparagement, Section 230, copyright of listing photos, and MLS rules

### Takeaway
The brand's own jokes about a named competitor, builder, HOA, condo building, or official are not protected by Section 230. That law shields platforms and users only for content created by others. A joke that implies a false, provable fact ("Builder X's condos are falling apart," "the HOA president embezzles") is actionable defamation. Statements that injure someone in their trade or business are especially dangerous. Listing photos are copyrighted (VHT v. Zillow), and in SC another firm's listing imagery also triggers § 40-57-135(E).

### Cited Findings
- 47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(1) protects a provider or user of an interactive computer service from being treated as publisher of information "provided by another information content provider." A party that creates or develops the content is the information content provider (§ 230(f)(3)) and gets no immunity for its own posts. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [47 U.S.C. § 230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230)
- *Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.*, 497 U.S. 1 (1990): there is no wholesale "opinion" privilege. A statement implying a provably false assertion of fact is actionable even if framed as opinion. Rhetorical hyperbole that cannot reasonably be read as stating facts is protected. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [Milkovich (Justia)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/1/)
- SC defamation elements, including that defamation per se covers statements imputing unfitness in one's business or profession: *Erickson v. Jones Street Publishers, LLC*, 368 S.C. 444, 629 S.E.2d 653 (2006). [canonical, not re-fetched] — [Erickson v. Jones Street Publishers (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/cases/south-carolina/supreme-court/2006/26150.html)
- Comparative false advertising: Lanham Act § 43(a)(1)(B), 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(1)(B), gives competitors a federal claim for misrepresentations in commercial advertising about another's services. The SC Unfair Trade Practices Act, S.C. Code § 39-5-20, bars unfair or deceptive acts in trade. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [15 U.S.C. § 1125](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125); [SC Code Title 39 Ch. 5](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c005.php)
- *VHT, Inc. v. Zillow Group, Inc.*, 918 F.3d 723 (9th Cir. 2019), and 69 F.4th 983 (9th Cir. 2023): real estate photos licensed to brokers remain the photographer's copyrighted works. Zillow was liable for displaying and searchably indexing thousands of them without a license, and fair use was rejected for the searchable database use. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [VHT v. Zillow, 9th Cir. 2019 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/17-35587/17-35587-2019-03-15.html)
- McMansion Hell (Kate Wagner), June 2017: Zillow sent a cease-and-desist citing its terms of use and copyright in listing photos. EFF responded that the commentary was fair use, and Zillow withdrew. The episode shows both the risk (C&D letters, account takedowns) and the fair-use defense available for genuine commentary and criticism. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [EFF, June 2017](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2017/06/eff-mcmansion-hell-zillow-backs-down)
- Copyright statutory damages range up to $150,000 per work for willful infringement (17 U.S.C. § 504(c)). Fair use factors are in 17 U.S.C. § 107. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [17 U.S.C. § 504](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/504)
- The SC REC says MLS rules "are governed independently and must be followed in addition to state law." It distinguishes IDX feeds on brokerage websites, where listing broker name display is required, from social reposting. [fetched] — [SC REC Reminder 2025](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Joke-writing test.** Ask "Could a reasonable viewer think this states a fact about a named or identifiable person or business, and is it false or unprovable?"
  - Safe-ish: absurd hyperbole about visible, non-factual matters. "This kitchen has more beige than a Myrtle Beach rental in 1994."
  - Dangerous: insinuations of crime, fraud, incompetence, structural defects, health violations, or financial distress about a named builder, HOA, building, agent, or official.
- **Public officials.** Officials must prove actual malice under *New York Times v. Sullivan*, which gives more room for satire about officials' public acts. But false factual claims of crimes are still actionable, and a licensee also carries reputational and NAR (for members) exposure.
- **"Zillow Gone Wild"-style roasts of local listings.** Three layers of risk:
  - copyright in the photos (photographer or MLS-licensed)
  - SC § 40-57-135(E) (another firm's listing, address, or imagery)
  - the homeowner's dignity and privacy (Commission guidance; also potential false-light claims)

  Roasting out-of-state listings, without addresses, is outside SC license-law listing rules if the property isn't SC real estate. That is an inference, and copyright risk remains. Self-roasting Chapter3's own listings needs seller consent.
- **MLS sold data.** Most MLSs (including any Myrtle Beach MLS) restrict using MLS data outside IDX/VOW displays. Rules for "just sold" posts and market stats usually require attribution and may bar using another broker's sold data. Check the local MLS rules.

### Rule checks
- R7.1 **BINDING** (defamation law; § 39-5-20; Lanham § 43(a)): extract any named or identifiable person, business, building, HOA, builder, or official. Flag every factual assertion about them (crime, fraud, defects, lawsuits, finances, competence, sales numbers). Block unless a documented source or public record is attached. Human review for anything that passes.
- R7.2 **BINDING** (17 U.S.C. § 106/501): any image or video not created by Chapter3 needs a license or permission record, or a documented fair-use rationale (commentary on the work itself, minimal portion). Listing photos from MLS, Zillow, or Realtor.com default to blocked.
- R7.3 **BINDING** (MLS rules, contract): MLS-sourced figures (sold prices, DOM, stats) must follow MLS attribution and use rules. Cite "Source: [MLS name]" if required.
- R7.4 **BEST PRACTICE**: for user comments on Chapter3 posts that defame others, Section 230 protects Chapter3 from liability for user comments. For NAR members, SoP 15-3 still requires removal of false statements about other professionals once known.

### Gaps
- The local MLS rules (likely the Coastal Carolinas Association of REALTORS® MLS for Myrtle Beach) were not retrieved.
- The current Zillow and Realtor.com terms on screenshots were not retrieved.
- The case citations above (Erickson, VHT, Milkovich) were not re-fetched this session.

## 8. TCPA, the SC Telephone Privacy Protection Act, and CAN-SPAM for leads and newsletters

### Takeaway
Texting or calling social-media leads for marketing is regulated even when the lead came in through a DM. Under SC law, texts are explicitly "telephone solicitations." They are allowed only 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., require identification and an opt-out, cannot go to Do-Not-Call numbers without an exemption, and carry $1,000 per violation in private damages ($5,000 if willful). Federal TCPA consent rules apply to autodialed or prerecorded messages. CAN-SPAM governs email newsletters.

### Cited Findings
- SC Telephone Privacy Protection Act, S.C. Code §§ 37-21-10 to -100 (2018). [fetched] — [SC Code Title 37 Ch. 21](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c021.php)
  - § 37-21-20(6): "telephone solicitation" covers calls to SC residences or wireless numbers with SC area codes to offer goods or services. It expressly includes a "text or media message sent." Excluded: calls with prior express written permission, an established business relationship, personal relationships, and others.
  - Established business relationship: a transaction within the prior 18 months, or an inquiry or application within the prior 3 months. Prior express invitation requires a signed or e-signed written agreement that includes the phone number.
  - § 37-21-30: contact only between 8:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m. local time, absent prior written consent.
  - § 37-21-40: at the start, give first and last name, sponsoring business name, contact phone and address, and purpose; offer to add the consumer to the in-house do-not-call list; end immediately if the consumer is not interested.
  - § 37-21-50: no misleading or spoofed caller ID; no SC area-code display without SC presence, under an intent standard.
  - § 37-21-70: honor stop requests for at least 5 years; no calls to National DNC numbers; good-faith compliance is an affirmative defense.
  - § 37-21-80: $1,000 per violation, up to $5,000 if willful, plus attorney's fees.
  - § 37-21-90: administrative and AG penalties up to $5,000 per violation.
- Federal TCPA, 47 U.S.C. § 227, and 47 CFR 64.1200: prior express written consent for autodialed or prerecorded marketing calls and texts to cell phones; National DNC protections; quiet hours of 8 a.m.–9 p.m.; statutory damages of $500 per violation, trebled to $1,500 if willful. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [47 U.S.C. § 227](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227); [47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
- The FCC's "one-to-one consent" rule for lead generators was vacated by the 11th Circuit in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC* (January 24, 2025). *Facebook v. Duguid*, 592 U.S. 395 (2021), narrowed the federal "autodialer" definition. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [Facebook v. Duguid (Justia)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/592/19-511/)
- CAN-SPAM Act, 15 U.S.C. § 7704, and 16 CFR Part 316: no false header or subject lines; identify the message as an ad (unless affirmatively consented); include a valid physical postal address; provide a working opt-out honored within 10 business days. Per-email civil penalties are inflation-adjusted. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [FTC CAN-SPAM Compliance Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

### Inferences
- Replying in-platform to a DM (Instagram or Facebook messages) is generally not a "telephone solicitation" under the SC Act. The definition of text message excludes messages over IP-based messaging services to users of the same service. Moving a lead to SMS is covered.
- A social lead who asks "text me about listings" has made an inquiry, which creates a 3-month established business relationship under SC law. Best practice is to capture written consent that includes the phone number.

### Rule checks
- R8.1 **BINDING** (§ 37-21-30; 47 CFR 64.1200(c)(1)): marketing texts or calls to leads only between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time.
- R8.2 **BINDING** (§ 37-21-40): the first marketing text includes the sender's first and last name, "Chapter3 Realty," a callback number and address (or a link), the purpose, and opt-out instructions.
- R8.3 **BINDING** (§ 37-21-70; TCPA): scrub against the National DNC list (no more than 31 days old) and the internal DNC list. Honor STOP for at least 5 years.
- R8.4 **BINDING** (TCPA): autodialed or templated mass texts require prior express written consent records.
- R8.5 **BINDING** (CAN-SPAM): newsletters carry a postal address, an unsubscribe link, and non-deceptive subjects. Process opt-outs within 10 business days.

### Gaps
- Whether a 2025–26 amendment changed the SC Act's damages or definitions was not checked beyond the current code text.
- The current FCC revocation-of-consent rules (effective April 2025, partially delayed) were not verified.

## 9. Real-world discipline for social posts, and political or controversial topics (legal vs. reputational risk)

### Takeaway
No public SC REC disciplinary order for a social media post was found. Most agent "social media discipline" is firing by brokerages or NAR association action, not license action. Political speech is legal, and under NAR's 2026 interpretations political affiliation isn't protected. But SC law makes it a crime for an employer to discharge someone for political opinions, which limits how Chapter3 can police staff politics. The real exposure from controversial content is the overlap categories: protected-class harassment, fair-housing-coded jokes, and false facts about identifiable people.

### Cited Findings
- No SC-specific case of an agent disciplined by the SC REC for a social post was found. The search returned out-of-state examples only. [fetched search] — [search results incl. Inman, RISMedia](https://inman.com/2022/08/17/how-to-handle-an-agents-social-media-post-gone-sideways)
- Ohio (2017): the Ohio Division of Real Estate sought discipline against a licensee for comments on her public personal Facebook page. The charge was that the comments did not reflect "high standards of professional conduct and integrity," and the article called it a first for a personal social media case. The outcome was not reported. [fetched] — [Hondros, Oct. 6, 2017](https://www.hondros.com/resources/blog/can-real-estate-commission-discipline-personal-facebook-page/)
- Brokerage firings over posts: a Beverly Hills agent fired for racist Instagram comments (2016) and an Illinois agent fired after a Twitter fight with comedian Patton Oswalt (2017). [headlines fetched via search, details not verified] — [Inman 2016](https://inman.com/2016/03/18/beverly-hills-realtor-fired-for-racist-comments-on-instagram/amp); [RISMedia 2017](https://www.rismedia.com/2017/02/06/illinois-real-estate-agent-fired-twitter-beef-comedian-patton-oswalt/)
- A British Columbia (Canada) regulator reprimanded a licensee for a hateful social media message. This is not US law; it is an illustration only. [headline via search] — [BCFSA](https://www.bcfsa.ca/about-us/news/news-release/real-estate-licensee-reprimanded-hateful-social-media-message)
- NAR 2026 Case 10-7: political insults on a business account were not an Article 10 violation because political affiliation is not protected. Case 10-6: racist slurs on a "personal" account used for business were a violation. [fetched] — [NAR 2026 Summary of PS Changes](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/MNREALTOR/488b06e6-56a7-4890-bb7a-ab79ba70337a/UploadedImages/Webpages/Legal/Documents/2026-Summary-of-PS-Changes-Effective-01-01-2026.pdf)
- S.C. Code § 16-17-560 makes it a misdemeanor to discharge a citizen from employment because of political opinions or the exercise of political rights. [canonical, not re-fetched] — [SC Code Title 16 Ch. 17](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c017.php)

### Inferences
Topics that are legal but dangerous for a SC licensee's brand account, in rough order of compliance risk:
1. Immigration and immigrants "moving here." National origin is a protected class, so "keep [group] out of [area]" jokes are FHA statements.
2. Religion as a neighborhood selling point.
3. Kids, families, spring breakers, and "the wrong crowd," where tourist jokes slide into familial status, race, or national origin.
4. Crime and safety by area.
5. LGBTQ or gender identity topics: NAR SoP 10-5 for members; not FHA-protected per se, though sex discrimination is.
6. Disability jokes ("handicap," "crazy neighbors").
7. Named local officials, builders, HOAs, and developers (defamation).
8. Hurricanes and flooding claims about specific properties or areas (misrepresentation under § 40-57-710(A); SC disclosure duties).
9. Partisan politics: legal, mostly reputational, plus consumer alienation.

Other points:
- **Staff politics.** Because of § 16-17-560, Chapter3 can require staff not to post politics on brand accounts and not to tie personal political posts to Chapter3 branding. Disciplining or firing staff for personal political opinions carries SC legal risk; get legal review.
- **Staff "personal" accounts.** These become professional when they advertise real estate (SC REC: any medium; NAR Case 10-6). A brokerage policy should require a disclaimer and separation, or accept that the BIC supervises such accounts.

### Rule checks
- R9.1 **BEST PRACTICE**: topic classifier with tiers. "Red" (block): protected-class jokes, immigration, religion-as-amenity, crime by area. "Amber" (BIC review): partisan politics, named officials, disasters, lawsuits. "Green": property quirks, weather, food, traffic, self-deprecation.
- R9.2 **BINDING** (NAR SoP 10-5, members): no harassment based on protected characteristics in any professional-capacity post.
- R9.3 **BEST PRACTICE**: no partisan endorsements or candidate content on the Chapter3 brand account. Respect § 16-17-560 in staff policy.
- R9.4 **BEST PRACTICE**: screenshot and archive all posts. A post is the evidence in any complaint (SC REC complaints rely on screenshots; NAR cases cite screenshots).

- The SC REC public disciplinary actions list on llr.sc.gov was not searched for social-media-related consent orders, so "no SC case found" reflects limited searching, not confirmed absence.
- The text of § 16-17-560 and SC case law applying it to social media firings were not verified this session.
