# Real Estate, Housing and Mortgage Voices on X/Twitter, LinkedIn, Threads and Reddit: Landscape and Gaps for Chapter3 Realty

Research date: 2026-11-07. Method: web search plus page fetches. I could not fetch X or LinkedIn profiles directly (they need login), so almost every follower count below comes from a third party or is self-reported, with the date or staleness noted. Treat all counts as approximate. The research hit the tool-call budget before covering every named account. The Gaps sections list what is still unverified.

## 1. Data/macro voices (Gerli, Mohtashami, Simonsen, Lambert, Parsons, McBride, Zelman and others): who they are, how they grow, and what being wrong on crash calls costs them

### Takeaway
Housing Twitter's data voices grow on a repeatable formula: a chart, a one-line thesis, often a named-metro "crash" or "oversupply" call, and a push to a paid product (an app, a newsletter, a terminal). A bull-vs-bear split drives engagement and gives each side an identity. Nick Gerli (Reventure) is the most visible bear. Logan Mohtashami (HousingWire) has built his brand on calling "crash addicts" wrong. Accuracy consequences are soft: bears rebrand misses as "regional crashes" (Austin, Florida, Seattle). No independent audits of forecast track records turned up.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The growth driver is specificity plus stakes. Named-metro calls ("Seattle is the most oversupplied market," "Austin buy signal coming") outperform national generalities, and local TV picks them up, which feeds more followers. This is a template Chapter3 can scale down to building- and neighborhood-level Grand Strand data.
- The bull/bear beef structure (Gerli/Lindsay vs. Mohtashami) means a neutral "receipts" voice that publishes local data and scores predictions has room to stand out.
- Being wrong is cheap for media/data companies but costly for a licensed local broker. Sellers and buyers read a "crash" call from their agent as a conflict of interest or a lost listing. Chapter3 should publish measured indicators (inventory, months of supply, days on market, price cuts by building), not crash calls.
- Gerli's funnel (free ZIP data, then a paid forecast) maps onto a brokerage funnel: free building-level data, a gated deeper report, then a consult.

### Gaps
- Current X follower counts for Gerli, Mohtashami, Simonsen, Lambert, Bill McBride (Calculated Risk), Daryl Fairweather (Redfin), Rick Palacios (John Burns), Ivy Zelman, Parcl Labs, Amherst and the Zillow/Realtor.com economists were not verifiable without logged-in access or a fresh tracker. These need manual checks on the profiles.
- I found no documentation of a direct X beef between Gerli and Mohtashami. Their opposition is visible through debates and framing, not a sourced exchange.
- No independent audit of Reventure's or other crash-callers' forecast accuracy was found.
- Not researched for lack of budget: Bill McBride's "not a crash" calls in 2022–24, and Redfin/Zillow economists' institutional voices.

## 2. Agent/broker/investor personalities: who they are, and text-platform vs. video growth

### Takeaway
The agent personalities with the biggest audiences (Glennda Baker, Ricky Carruth, Ryan Serhant, Tom Ferry, Kevin Ward) grew on video: TikTok, YouTube, Reels and podcasts. Text platforms (X, LinkedIn) are where the data economists and analysts grew. Agent coaches mostly monetize by selling coaching to other agents rather than to consumers.

### Cited Findings
- Glennda Baker (Atlanta, Ansley Atlanta Real Estate) started TikTok in Oct 2020 with 122 followers and passed 565,000 about a year later. Her TikTok-sourced business was reported as $108K GCI in the first 5 months of 2021 (Inman) and $241K GCI for 2021 (NAR). Both figures are self-reported and cover different periods. Her team repurposes topics across TikTok, LinkedIn and Instagram. — [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok); [Inman, 2021](https://inman.com/2021/06/04/heres-how-this-agent-generated-108k-on-tiktok-in-2021-so-far); [Inman, 2023](https://www.inman.com/2023/04/25/glennda-bakers-recipe-to-shine-on-social-media-with-short-form-video/)
- Ricky Carruth (Alabama, formerly the #1 RE/MAX agent in AL in 2014) went "from no social media to 10M monthly organic views" (Inman, Dec 2025). He runs Zero to Diamond (free coaching and community for agents) and The Ricky Carruth Show podcast. His advice is to document and share your daily activity and prospect directly. — [Inman, Dec 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/12/01/how-this-agent-turned-coach-went-from-no-social-media-to-10m-monthly-organic-views/); [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/id1812027308)
- A Tampa agent (@tampa_bre) shows the Florida agent-TikTok archetype, with a mid-six-figure TikTok audience per a creator-stats tracker (Aug 2026). — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/tampa_bre/)
- Inman documented an earlier "agent becomes influencer" arc in a podcast series ("from rockstar agent to real estate influencer"). — [Libsyn](https://sites.libsyn.com/473616/website/agent-series-14-from-rockstar-agent-to-real-estate-influencer)

### Inferences
- For Chapter3, video is the reach engine for agent personas, while X and LinkedIn are the credibility engine for data. A data brokerage should lead on text (charts and threads) and repurpose into short video, not the reverse.
- Most agent-celebrity monetization (coaching, courses) targets other agents. Chapter3's audience is consumers (relocators, retirees, STR investors), so the better model is the data/newsletter funnel in section 1, not the coach funnel.

### Gaps

- Anonymous or parody agent accounts and "real estate memes" accounts were not researched.

## 4. Controversial takes that drive engagement, and which ones hurt licensed agents

### Takeaway
Post-NAR-settlement commission content is the most engagement-rich and the most dangerous topic for licensed agents. Agents flooded TikTok with complaints, brokerages made viral anti-NAR videos, and steering or commission-related posts invite ethics scrutiny. Crash predictions, insurance and HOA costs in coastal markets, and "Zillow vs. agents" are the other proven engagement topics. For a licensed broker, takes framed as consumer-protective data (insurance, HOA reserves) are safer than takes on commission or crashes.

### Cited Findings
- Agents "storm TikTok with flood of complaints after NAR settlement" (Inman headline). — [Inman tag page](https://www.inman.com/tag/settlement/page/4/)
- An Arizona brokerage's viral 2024 video portrayed NAR as an FBI/SWAT-style raid team. NAR declined to comment. — [Inman, June 2024](https://inman.com/2024/06/26/nar-swat-team-strikes-arizona-home-in-wild-new-marketing-video)
- A 2024 industry survey found 62% disapproved of how NAR handled the commission fallout. — [The Real Deal, June 2024](https://therealdeal.com/chicago/2024/06/28/real-estate-industry-survey-shows-disapproval-of-nar)
- NAR presidential turmoil in 2024 sparked agent frustration that played out publicly. — [The Real Deal, Jan 2024](https://therealdeal.com/national/2024/01/11/nar-presidents-resignation-sparks-agent-frustration)
- Reported: 42.6% of agents experienced commission-related pushback from clients after the settlement. This is a secondary aggregator figure, original survey not confirmed. — [nowbam NAR settlement tag](https://nowbam.com/tag/nar-settlement/page/5/)
- Low confidence: a search snippet described a viral video from a Fresno Realtor with about 114K Instagram followers about a seller's agent and commissions. Coverage noted that steering violates the Realtor Code of Ethics. I could not reach the primary article, so verify before use. — [nowbam NAR settlement tag](https://nowbam.com/tag/nar-settlement/page/5/)
- Commission clarity costs and consumer-transparency debates are ongoing (HousingWire). — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/the-cost-of-real-estate-commission-clarity/)
- Crash and bubble takes earn national pickup (Newsweek "crash stage" coverage of Reventure). — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/housing-market-entering-crash-stage-multiple-cities-1920530)
- Coastal insurance and HOA assessment pain is a live consumer topic in Myrtle Beach (see section 3 Reddit and BiggerPockets citations). Condo insurance is split between the HOA master policy and HO-6, and flood coverage is separate. — [Offermarket](https://offermarket.us/blog/rental-condo-insurance-at-beach-sc)

### Inferences
- Commission takes split audiences: consumers cheer anti-commission posts and agents attack them, while pro-commission posts get dunked on by consumers. For a new brokerage, posting transparent fee structures as data (what buyers paid, how buyer-broker agreements work in SC) is safer than taking a side.
- Crash predictions carry asymmetric risk for a licensed local broker. Being wrong damages credibility with sellers, and being "right" scares away listings. Indicator-style posts ("months of supply in oceanfront condos hit X") keep the engagement without the forecast liability.
- Insurance, HOA reserves, special assessments, flood zones and STR rules are high-engagement, consumer-protective topics where a data brokerage looks trustworthy rather than self-serving. This matches Chapter3's 55+ and relocator audiences.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence was gathered on engagement for rent-vs-buy, "housing is not an investment," Gen Z homeownership, lock-in effect, iBuyer or climate-risk takes specifically.
- No confirmed case of an agent disciplined or fired over a viral post was found. Realtor association complaint mechanics for social posts (advertising rules, Article 12 truth-in-advertising) were not researched.
- I did not research Myrtle Beach or Horry County STR ordinance controversies on social media.

## 5. Content formats that perform on X vs. LinkedIn, and listing-roast accounts (Zillow Gone Wild, McMansion Hell) and their legal risk

### Takeaway
On X, the winning format is a chart plus a one-line thesis, extended into a numbered thread that ends in a product CTA (Gerli's pattern). LinkedIn rewards economist-style commentary from a professional identity (Parsons). Listing roasts are huge but legally risky because listing photos are copyrighted by photographers: Zillow Gone Wild was sued for $300K in 2024, and McMansion Hell got a Zillow cease-and-desist in 2017. A licensed brokerage should not roast other agents' listings using their photos.

### Cited Findings
- Gerli's threads are numbered (e.g., tweet "12)", "13)") with data per ZIP and a final CTA to the app. — [X post](https://x.com/nickgerli1/status/1940488750448161223); [X post](https://x.com/nickgerli1/status/1925919577898209546)
- Thread Reader App archives Gerli's threads, showing the long-thread format. — [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/user/nickgerli1)
- Parsons: about half a million impressions per week, LinkedIn-heavy. — [NMHC bio](https://www.nmhc.org/globalassets/meetings/speaker-headshots/bios/jay-parsons-bio-2026.pdf)
- **Zillow Gone Wild:** photographer Jennifer Bouma sued parent company Kale Salad Inc. for $300K (filed July 29, 2024, E.D.N.Y.) over two photos of a Washington home used without contact. The account had been spun off into an HGTV series. — [Inman, Aug 2024](https://inman.com/2024/08/05/zillow-gone-wild-gets-hit-with-300k-copyright-infringement-suit); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/08/05/real-estate-photographer-sues-zillow-gone-wild-for-using-property-pictures); [artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/photographer-copyright-infringement-suit-zillow-gone-wild-2521183)
  - Sept 2025: the judge ruled Zillow Gone Wild must face the infringement claims (each photo used in its entirety), but barred statutory damages and fees because the use predated registration by about 5 months. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/zillow-gone-wild-fails-to-avoid-copyright-infringement-claims)
  - Oct 2025: the parties ended the suit, with terms undisclosed. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/zillow-gone-wild-photographer-end-copyright-infringement-suit)
  - Bouma's lawyer warned of a possible "flood" of photographer complaints. Experts quoted elsewhere argued fair use was possible. — [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2024/08/05/zillow-gone-wild-sued-by-real-estate-photographer)
- **McMansion Hell (Kate Wagner):** in June 2017 Zillow sent a cease-and-desist citing photo copyright and the CFAA, and the blog went offline briefly. The EFF represented her. Zillow dropped the threat after backlash, and Wagner kept her posts but agreed to stop sourcing photos from Zillow. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMansion_Hell); [Indy Week](https://indyweek.com/news/archives-news/zillow-threatens-n-c-s-kate-wagner-mcmansion-hell-blog/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/303724/mcmansion-hell-resolves-battle-with-zillow-wont.html); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mcmansion-hell-defeats-zillow-in-pointless-lawsuit-and-revives-its-fantastic-architecture-for-the-masses-blog)
- Zillow itself lost an $8.3M jury verdict (later halved) over listing images, which shows how aggressively real estate photo copyright is enforced. — [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/other/mcmansion-hell-shut-down-by-cease-and-desist-from-zillow-76019)

### Inferences
- Formats that fit Chapter3:
  - On X: one chart, one takeaway, "receipts" threads ("I tracked every oceanfront condo sale in North Myrtle Beach…"), and polls to relocators ("Ohio folks: what surprised you about SC taxes?").
  - On LinkedIn: monthly local-market commentary, permit-pipeline analyses, and HOA-insurance explainers aimed at relocators' professional networks and referral partners (lenders, insurance agents, CPAs).
- Roast-style humor should use Chapter3's own photos, public permit records, or commissioned or licensed images. Another agent's listing photos risk copyright claims and violate MLS photo-use rules, plus professional-courtesy and ethics friction.
- "Things buyers get wrong" lists (insurance, HO-6 vs. master policy, flood zones, reserves) are natural, low-risk evergreen posts for retirees and relocators.

### Gaps
- No quantitative engagement benchmarks (impressions per format on X vs. LinkedIn) for real estate were found.
- Threads-specific real estate growth data was not found.
- Before/after and deal-breakdown performance data was not sourced.

## 6. How these voices turn followers into clients and leads

### Takeaway
The proven funnel is free public data, then a gated or paid tier, then a high-touch service. Gerli uses free ZIP data, a $39 premium forecast and consulting. Lambert uses a free newsletter, a $299/yr PRO tier, a terminal and a conference. Parsons uses a podcast plus consulting and speaking. Agent-influencers (Baker, Carruth) convert through video volume into direct inbound clients, or by selling coaching to agents.

### Inferences
- A Chapter3 funnel modeled on Gerli and Lambert:
  1. Free public charts on X and LinkedIn.
  2. A free weekly "Grand Strand by the Numbers" newsletter.
  3. Gated lead magnets: a building-by-building rental-return database, an HOA reserve and insurance lookup, and relocation guides per origin state (OH/NY/NJ/PA/MD).
  4. A calculator (STR net yield after HOA, insurance and accommodations tax).
  5. A consult or buyer agreement.
- The ResiClub webinar shows national data brands now encourage agents to become local newsletter publishers. Competitors may adopt this, so being early in the Grand Strand matters.
