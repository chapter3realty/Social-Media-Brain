# Growth Playbook for a New (Zero-Follower) Account on X and LinkedIn, 2025-2026, for a Myrtle Beach Real Estate Brokerage

Scope note: This covers account structure, reply and comment strategy, cadence, content mix, growth timelines, turning followers into leads, metrics, AI drafting, and approval workflows. Research was done in November 2026. Most "growth playbook" material online comes from tool vendors and creators with something to sell. Measured data is flagged as **[MEASURED]**. Vendor or anecdotal claims are flagged as **[VENDOR/ANECDOTE]**. Widely repeated claims with no traceable source are flagged as **[FOLKLORE]**.

---

## 1. Founder-led vs brand account vs both (reach of personal vs company accounts; employee-advocacy multiplier; one voice across several people)

### Takeaway
On LinkedIn, personal profiles reliably beat company pages on reach and engagement. The best measured figure is van der Blom's 2025 finding that a company page post reaches only about 1.6% of its followers. The size of the gap is disputed, though: headline multipliers such as "561%" or "5-10x" are poorly sourced. For a three-person brokerage, the evidence supports running two or three personal profiles as the main growth engines, with a thin brand page or handle acting as a hub and a legitimacy signal.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, secondary report of primary]** Richard van der Blom's *Algorithm InSights 2025* report (about 1.8M posts analysed) found a company page post reaches on average about 1.6% of the page's followers. The same report is cited for personal profiles getting "561% more visibility" than pages. — [Search summary citing van der Blom via contentin.io / Ordinal](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/); [Ordinal: Company page reach Jan 2026](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)
- **[MEASURED, secondary]** Coverage of the same van der Blom 2025 dataset reports platform-wide organic declines year on year: views down about 50%, engagement down about 25%, follower growth down about 59%. — [Mercer MacKay summary](https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/); [Entrepreneur: "Organic reach on LinkedIn is cratering"](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/organic-reach-on-linkedin-is-cratering-heres-how-to/497713)
- **[CONTRADICTION]** One analysis argues that, in recent published data, impressions per post are almost identical for the two formats: 812.64 for company pages and 817.67 for personal profiles. Engagement rates still differ: 2.60% for personal profiles vs 1.74% for pages. The author notes the two numbers come from separate studies, so the comparison is only directional. — [SocialNexis: when company pages outperform personal posts](https://socialnexis.com/guides/company-page-personal-post-conditions)
- **[VENDOR]** A Refine Labs study is cited as finding that personal-profile posts got about 3x the impressions and about 5x the engagement of company posts, even with half the followers. — [LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/blog/linkedin-company-page-vs-personal-profile-which-drives-more-sales-in-2025)
- **[MEASURED, LinkedIn official]** LinkedIn's own Elevate infographic says a company's employees have on average about 10x as many connections as the company has followers, and that only about 3% of employees share company content. — [LinkedIn Elevate infographic (PDF)](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/elevate/Resources/pdf/elevate-infographic.pdf)
- **[FOLKLORE-ish]** The widely repeated claim that employee-shared content gets "2x CTR" is attributed to LinkedIn by secondary sites. The official infographic only shows a "Higher CTR" label, so the 2x figure could not be confirmed. — [LinkedIn Elevate infographic](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/elevate/Resources/pdf/elevate-infographic.pdf); [Oktopost compilation](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/employee-advocacy-statistics/)
- **[MEASURED]** In the Edelman Trust Barometer 2025, "my employer" remains the most trusted institution at 76%, a 3-point drop, and it is the only institution trusted by low-income respondents. — [Fortune on Edelman 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/01/14/edelman-ceo-essay-trust-barometer-25th-anniversary/); [LumApps summary](https://www.lumapps.com/insights/blog/trust-issues-at-work-blame-the-boss-and-fix-it)
- **[MEASURED, older]** Edelman's 2016 research found employees are among the most trusted company spokespeople, more trusted than the CEO. No 2025 figure directly comparing employee and CEO spokesperson credibility was found. — [Cutting Edge PR summary of Edelman spokesperson data](https://cuttingedgepr.com/articles/how-trusted-are-different-spokespersons/)
- **[MEASURED]** Sprout Social Index 2025 (4,044 consumers in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, fielded Sept 2024): authenticity and relatability are the two brand traits consumers value most, and about half say original content is what makes their favourite brands stand out. — [Sprout Social press release, Jan 7 2025](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)
- **[MEASURED, LinkedIn's algorithm report]** A Scribd excerpt of van der Blom 2025 says that visiting a connection's profile raises the chance their next post appears in your feed by about 60%. That makes profile-to-profile interaction between team members and target prospects valuable. — [Scribd excerpt, Algorithm InSights 2025 ch.1](https://www.scribd.com/document/984921783/Algorithm-Insights-Report-2025-chapter-1-Richard-Van-der-Blom)

### Inferences
- Recommended structure for three people plus AI:
  - **LinkedIn:** two or three personal profiles do the growth work, for example the founder/broker, the veteran agent and the "data person". A company page acts as the credibility hub: logo, reviews, a link to the newsletter and the listings feed. Brand-page posts should mostly reshare or adapt the best personal posts. Company page reach (about 1.6% of followers) is too low to grow from zero.
  - **X:** a single strong, persona-driven account will likely beat several weak ones, because growth on X depends heavily on reply volume (see section 2). The best choice is one founder or "data guy" account, plus an optional brand handle reserved for listings and alerts.
- Keeping one voice across several people:
  - Use a shared voice document: tone, banned phrases, the do-not-joke list and claims you can back up.
  - Split the people by role so they don't overlap. For example: "the data guy" posts weekly numbers, "the veteran" posts stories and neighbourhood knowledge, and "the founder" posts opinions and market commentary.
  - Run every post through the same AI draft-then-check pass against the voice document.
- Employee trust data (Edelman) supports putting the named humans in front. High-price, high-trust buyers (relocators, retirees) are buying a person, not a logo.

### Gaps
- No primary-source copy of van der Blom 2025 was retrieved. The 1.6% and 561% figures come from secondary summaries.
- No documented case of a small company running a brand account plus two or three personal accounts with published before/after numbers was found. Large-company examples (e.g., HubSpot, Gong) are commonly mentioned online but were not verified this session.
- No X-specific data on brand vs personal account reach was found.

## 2. The "reply guy" strategy on X and "comment first" on LinkedIn (evidence, recommended volumes, spam risk)

### Takeaway
Replies are the most heavily weighted positive engagement signal in every version of X's published ranking weights. The consistent advice for small accounts is many thoughtful replies to mid-sized accounts in your niche. However, the evidence for specific daily volumes is anecdotal: suggestions range from 10 to over 100 replies a day, with roughly 10 to 30 the most common. On LinkedIn, commenting plausibly drives profile views. LinkedIn's own creator account has claimed "commenting once a week can triple profile views", but no underlying data was published.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, open-source code, but numbers disputed]** X's 2023 open-sourced Heavy Ranker weights are commonly reported as: Like 0.5, Reply 13.5, reply that the author engages with ("conversation") 75, Report −369. An arXiv paper says X's August 2026 published weights cut the reply weight from 13.5 to 5.0. A 2026 guide lists "share via copy link" at 20.0 as the heaviest positive weight. — [SocialPilot X algorithm](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/twitter-algorithm); [arXiv: Why does misinformation propagate faster? (X algorithm)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28947); [PostWizard X algorithm cheat sheet](https://postwizard.ai/x-algorithm-cheat-sheet)
- **[MEASURED, structure]** X open-sourced its recommender in March 2023 and again in January 2026. The 2026 version is described as a Grok-based transformer ("Phoenix") that predicts multiple user actions and combines them with weights. — [explainx: X algorithm open source 2026](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/x-algorithm-open-source-github-elon-musk-2026); [explainx: X discloses ranking weights Aug 2026](https://explainx.ai/blog/x-algorithm-for-you-timeline-open-source-ranking-weights-august-2026)
- **[ANECDOTE]** Tom Butler (May 2025) reported going from 0 to 470+ followers in 10 weeks with the reply-guy strategy, growing about 50% per week. — [Tom Butler on X](https://x.com/tomlbutler/status/1923972320973967595)
- **[ANECDOTE]** Another creator reported growing from 20 to 66 followers in the first week of replying. A separate creator reported 193K+ impressions and 440+ profile views in four weeks from about an hour a day of replies. — [Medium: How to grow on X, 4-week playbook](https://medium.com/the-efficient-entrepreneur/how-to-grow-on-x-twitter-in-14-days-f7802e1a21b8); [Indieradar: Reply guy method](https://indieradar.app/blog/reply-guy-method-grow-x-twitter-zero-followers)
- **[VENDOR]** Recommended reply volumes vary widely:
  - 10 to 20 per day for beginners
  - 15 to 25 as a "sweet spot"
  - 50 to 80 for very small accounts
  - tiers of 30 to 50 ("committed") and 100+ ("all-in")
  
  Most guides stress that daily consistency matters more than the exact number. — [Teract: Reply guy strategy 2026 (70/30 rule)](https://www.teract.ai/resources/twitter-reply-guy-strategy-2026); [Bisonary](https://www.bisonary.com/blog/how-to-grow-on-twitter-with-replies-in-2026); [Indieradar](https://indieradar.app/blog/reply-guy-method-grow-x-twitter-zero-followers)
- **[VENDOR]** Targeting advice: reply to accounts of roughly 20K to 2M followers, because replies get buried on mega-accounts. Reply to no more than one or two posts per account per day, and spread replies across 10 to 15 accounts. 100+ low-effort replies a day reads as spam and is said to get throttled, but this is unverified. — [ReachMore: X reply strategy](https://reachmore.co/blogs/x-reply-strategy-2026); [Teract](https://www.teract.ai/resources/twitter-reply-guy-strategy-2026)
- **[FOLKLORE]** A claimed X cap of about 500 replies a day for established accounts is unsourced. — [Teract](https://www.teract.ai/resources/twitter-reply-guy-strategy-2026)
- **[CLAIMED BY LINKEDIN, no data]** LinkedIn's "LinkedIn Guide for Creating" account said that "commenting once a week can triple users' profile views", as reported by Lindsey Gamble. No underlying data was given. — [Lindsey Gamble](https://lindseygamble.com/blog/linkedin-shares-commenting-can-triple-profile-views)
- **[SMALL SELF-EXPERIMENT]** In a Hootsuite test, longer, personalised LinkedIn comments produced more profile views than short ones (24 vs 14 in week 1). Connection requests differed little (10 vs 8). — [Hootsuite: long vs short LinkedIn comments](https://blog.hootsuite.com/long-vs-short-linkedin-comments/)
- **[VENDOR]** Claims such as "commenting 10 to 20 times a day increases profile views by 50%" or "200 to 400% more weekly profile views" come without methodology. Taplio notes that LinkedIn has never officially said commenting grows the commenter's own reach. Taplio cites a LinkedHub study of 657,722 comments suggesting early comments earn more impressions. — [Taplio: how to be the comment guy](https://taplio.com/blog/how-to-be-the-comment-guy-linkedin); [Koka Sexton](https://kokasexton.com/linkedin-commenting-strategy-highest-roi-tactic/)
- **[SECONDARY]** Comments count more than likes in LinkedIn ranking: roughly 2x a like, with a save worth roughly 2x a meaningful comment (AuthoredUp-attributed). Dwell time and the first hour after posting are cited as critical. — [SocialPilot LinkedIn algorithm](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm); [meet-lea LinkedIn algorithm data](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-algorithm-explained)

### Inferences
- **For X:** a disciplined reply programme of about 20 to 30 substantive replies a day (roughly 45 to 60 minutes) fits a three-person team. It is the midpoint of coach and vendor advice and below obvious spam levels.
  - Spread the replies across a curated list of 30 to 50 target accounts:
    - real estate and housing-data accounts
    - mortgage and rate commentators
    - Myrtle Beach and Grand Strand local media
    - retirement and relocation accounts
    - short-term rental investor accounts (e.g., AirDNA-type analysts)
  - Replies that start conversations the original author answers are worth the most, since "conversation" was the highest positive weight in 2023.
- Because X's reply weight was reportedly cut in 2026, replies alone may be less powerful than they were in 2023. Pair them with original posts that invite replies.
- **For LinkedIn:** "comment first" means 5 to 15 thoughtful comments a day on posts by target-audience people and local or real-estate creators, posted early (within the first hour). This is low risk and plausibly drives profile views, but the size of the effect is unmeasured.
- **Spam risk:** use AI only to suggest angles, never to auto-post replies. Generic replies ("Great point!") are exactly the pattern guides say gets ignored or throttled.

### Gaps
- Specific reply-volume recommendations from the named coaches (Dickie Bush/Ship 30, Nicolas Cole, Justin Welsh, Dan Koe, Sahil Bloom) were not verified from primary sources this session. A frequently repeated attribution, that Justin Welsh did about 20 comments a day early on, is unverified.
- No controlled study of the reply strategy's effect on follower growth exists in the results. All the evidence is anecdotal or vendor-sourced.
- X's current official rate limits on replies were not found.

## 3. Cadence and best times (X and LinkedIn; does frequent LinkedIn posting cannibalise reach?)

### Takeaway
For LinkedIn, the strongest 2025 dataset (Buffer, 2M+ posts from 94K+ accounts) shows that more posts per week goes with more impressions and engagement *per post*, with no detectable penalty. 2 to 5 posts a week is the sustainable floor, and 6 to 10 accelerates growth. For X, no current, rigorous frequency study was found. Old Buffer guidance (2014) and current coach norms suggest multiple posts a day, but the evidence is weak.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, observational]** Buffer (Aug 2025; data scientist Julian Winternheimer; 2M+ LinkedIn posts from 94,000+ accounts) compared each account's busy weeks with its own quiet weeks, using Z-scores and fixed-effects regression:

  | Posts per week | Extra impressions per post (vs 1/week) | Engagement rate change |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2 to 5 | +1,182 | +0.23 pp |
  | 6 to 10 | +5,001 | +0.76 pp |
  | 11+ | +16,946 | +1.40 pp (about 3x engagements per post) |

  Buffer reports no penalty or cap for frequent posting. Results hold across accounts of about 500 to 50K followers. The findings are associative, not causal, and quality still matters. — [Buffer: How often to post on LinkedIn](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/); [Social Media Today coverage](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-shows-posting-frequency-key-to-linkedin-success/758918/)
- **[MEASURED]** In the same Buffer dataset, by format:
  - Carousels/PDFs get about 278% more engagement than video, 303% more than images and nearly 600% more than text.
  - Images get about 72% more than text, and video about 84% more than text.
  - Link posts have the lowest median engagement rate.
  
  — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- **[NOT MEASURED]** Buffer's analysis does not test cannibalisation, meaning whether a new post suppresses the previous one. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- **[OLD DATA]** Buffer's 2014 frequency guide suggested about 14 tweets a day, no more than once an hour on weekdays. It cited SumAll (3+ a day) and Track Social: per-day engagement kept rising up to 30 tweets a day, but response *per post* fell beyond one a day. — [Buffer social media frequency guide (2014)](https://blog.bufferapp.com/social-media-frequency-guide)
- **[MEASURED, timing]** Buffer analysed 8.7M+ tweets for best time, day and format, but the summary retrieved did not include frequency figures. A Buffer resource says the biggest lift comes from creators who keep a consistent posting habit for 20+ weeks. — [Buffer resource guide](https://support.buffer.com/articles/growing-on-social-media-a-buffer-resource-guide-Vt0q4k0Sqr.md)
- **[VENDOR]** Best-time studies disagree: "every study disagrees somewhere"; the answer depends on audience time zone. — [socialk.it best time to post on X](https://socialk.it/en/best-time-to-post/x)

### Inferences
- **LinkedIn cadence:**
  - Start each personal profile at 3 posts a week in the first 90 days.
  - Move the main voice to 5 to 7 a week once there is a backlog of real local stories and data.
  - Use carousels/PDFs for the weekly market numbers (the highest-engagement format in Buffer's data).
  - Keep external links out of the main post body, since link posts have the lowest median engagement.
- **X cadence (practitioner norm, not measured):** 1 to 3 original posts a day, one thread or long post a week (e.g., the weekly Grand Strand market recap), and reply work every day.
- **Timing:** the audience splits into out-of-state relocators and retirees (often Northeast and Midwest, so Eastern/Central time, the same as SC) and local buyers. Default to weekday mornings Eastern, then test with your own analytics. Industry best-time charts should not override account data.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 Hootsuite, Sprout or AuthoredUp frequency dataset was retrieved this session (for example AuthoredUp's posting-gap or cannibalisation analysis). Treat any claim about "waiting 12 to 24 hours between LinkedIn posts" as **folklore** unless sourced.
- No current, rigorous X posts-per-day study was found.

## 4. Content mix frameworks, recurring series, trend-jacking, hooks and formats

### Takeaway
No rigorous evidence supports any specific pillar ratio (e.g., 70/20/10 or 4-1-1). Those are practitioner heuristics. What *is* measured: consumers value authenticity, relatability and original content (Sprout 2025). A third of consumers find brand trend-jacking embarrassing, and 27% think it only works within 24 to 48 hours. Document and carousel formats lead LinkedIn engagement.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED]** Sprout Social Index 2025 (4,044 consumers, Sept 2024):
  - 93% say brands should keep up with online culture.
  - About a third think brands jumping on viral trends is "embarrassing".
  - 27% say trend participation is effective only within 24 to 48 hours.
  - About half say original content makes their favourite brands stand out.
  - 73% would buy from a competitor if a brand doesn't respond on social.
  - X is used by 50% of consumers, vs Facebook 90% and Instagram 82%.
  
  — [Sprout Social press release](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/); [Sprout Social Index 2025](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/index/)
- **[MEASURED]** Sprout 2025: consumers want brands that listen rather than broadcast, and 93% say brands need to do more to combat misinformation. — [Sprout Social Index 2025](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/index/)
- **[MEASURED]** LinkedIn format ranking (Buffer 2M posts): carousel/PDF > video > image > text, and link posts are lowest. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- **[VENDOR]** The "70/30 rule" for X (70% replies or engagement vs 30% original posts) is promoted by a reply-tool vendor. — [Teract](https://www.teract.ai/resources/twitter-reply-guy-strategy-2026)
- **[MEASURED, secondary]** van der Blom 2025 is cited as attributing performance mainly to topic relevance and authority, i.e., staying consistently on a niche. — [Mercer MacKay](https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/); [Agorapulse LinkedIn algorithm 2026](https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/linkedin/linkedin-algorithm-2025/)

### Inferences
- **Suggested pillars** (heuristic, to be tuned with your own data):
  1. **Local market data and explainers (about 40%).** Weekly Grand Strand numbers carousel, flood-zone and insurance explainers, HOA/condo-fee realities, STR regulation by municipality.
  2. **Place and lifestyle for relocators and retirees (about 25%).** "Myrtle Beach myth of the week", neighbourhood-by-neighbourhood guides, cost-of-living comparisons with Ohio, New York, New Jersey and Pennsylvania.
  3. **Stories and personality (about 20%).** Deals gone sideways (anonymised), veteran-broker lessons, behind-the-scenes.
  4. **Offers and CTAs (about 15% or less).** Newsletter, calculator, listings, "DM me 'relocate'".
- **Recurring series fit the evidence on consistency and niche authority.** Candidates: "Friday Numbers", "Myth of the Week", "Roast my listing" (only for consenting sellers or your own listings), "STR math teardown".
- **Trend-jacking:** given that a third of consumers find it embarrassing and it only works within 24 to 48 hours, use it sparingly and only where there is a genuine local or real-estate angle (rate moves, hurricane season, NAR rule changes, insurance news). It needs a fast approval lane (section 9).
- **Polls and memes:** no 2025-2026 measured data was found. Treat them as low-cost experiments.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025-2026 data was found on which hook types perform best on X vs LinkedIn.
- No measured data was found on polls' current reach on LinkedIn (folklore says LinkedIn throttled polls in 2023-24, but this was unverified).
- The Hootsuite Social Trends 2025/2026 reports were not retrieved this session.

## 5. Realistic growth timelines (months to 1k and 10k; documented case studies)

### Takeaway
There is little rigorous, documented trajectory data. Available numbers are anecdotal:
- Tom Butler on X: about 470 followers in 10 weeks via replies.
- Justin Welsh on LinkedIn: about 2,500 followers in 2019, and his first post got 12 views, rising to about 30K views a year later. He passed 690K by mid-2022 (his own claim).

Platform-wide organic growth slowed sharply in 2025 (van der Blom: follower growth down about 59%). Benchmarks from 2019 to 2022 overstate what is achievable today.

### Cited Findings
- **[ANECDOTE]** X: 0 to 470+ followers in 10 weeks, about 50% weekly growth, using replies. — [Tom Butler on X](https://x.com/tomlbutler/status/1923972320973967595)
- **[ANECDOTE]** X: 20 to 66 followers in week one of replying. — [Medium 4-week playbook](https://medium.com/the-efficient-entrepreneur/how-to-grow-on-x-twitter-in-14-days-f7802e1a21b8)
- **[ANECDOTE, conflicting secondary]** Justin Welsh: his first LinkedIn post got 12 views and posts reached about 30K views a year later. He had about 2,500 followers in 2019. Follower totals are inconsistent across sources: 250K LinkedIn plus 150K X in 2023; "690K followers" (likely cross-platform) and $2.41M from the platform in 2021, per his own 2022 thread. — [Kleo: Justin Welsh story](https://kleo.so/blog/justin-welsh); [Beehiiv: One man army](https://karthick-call-to-action.beehiiv.com/p/one-man-army); [Threadreader: Justin Welsh thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1552291739012063232); [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/justin-welsh/)
- **[MEASURED, secondary]** LinkedIn platform-wide in 2025: follower growth down about 59% and views down about 50% year on year. — [Mercer MacKay summary of van der Blom](https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/)
- **[MEASURED]** Buffer: the biggest lift comes from creators who post consistently for 20+ weeks. — [Buffer resource guide](https://support.buffer.com/articles/growing-on-social-media-a-buffer-resource-guide-Vt0q4k0Sqr.md)

### Inferences
- Planning ranges for a disciplined local account (inference only; not benchmarks):
  - **X:** roughly 300 to 1,000 followers in the first 3 months with daily replies.
  - **LinkedIn:** reaching 1,000 followers on a new personal profile in 3 to 6 months is plausible, especially by connecting with local professionals.
  - **10K:** for a *local* real-estate niche, 10K on either platform is probably a 12 to 24+ month goal and may never be necessary. The total addressable audience is narrow.
- For a local high-price service, follower count is a weak proxy. 500 followers who are Grand Strand prospects, relocating professionals, lenders, inspectors and STR investors are worth more than 10K generic real estate followers. Set goals on qualified conversations, not followers.

### Gaps
- No documented case study of a *local* real estate business (or another local high-trust service) growing on X or LinkedIn with month-by-month numbers was found.
- Dickie Bush/Ship 30, Sahil Bloom, Jasmin Alić, Lara Acosta and Chris Donnelly growth curves were not retrieved this session.

## 6. Turning attention into business (newsletter capture, lead magnets, DMs, link placement, attribution, benchmarks)

### Takeaway
For realtors, social media is the most-cited source of quality leads. In NAR's 2025 Technology Survey, 39% of respondents named it, ahead of CRM (23%), MLS (17%) and brokerage websites (13%). No benchmark was found for social-follower-to-closed-deal conversion. Link handling differs: on LinkedIn, link posts have the lowest median engagement. On X, evidence on a link penalty is contradictory, and 2026 statements from X say links in posts are no longer penalised.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, survey]** NAR 2025 REALTORS Technology Survey (July 2025; 1,241 usable responses from 49,233 invited):
  - Social media produces the highest number of quality leads (39%), ahead of CRM (23%), local MLS (17%) and brokerage website (13%).
  - 75% of Realtors use social media, second only to eSignature at 79%.
  
  — [NAR 2025 Technology Survey (PDF)](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf); [HousingWire coverage](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey-realtor-tech-usage-trends/)
- **[NOT FOUND]** The NAR survey does not report a social-to-closed-deal conversion rate. — [NAR 2025 Technology Survey](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf)
- **[MEASURED]** LinkedIn link posts have the lowest median engagement rate of all formats (Buffer 2M posts). — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- **[CONTRADICTORY, X]** Some SEO guides say X penalises posts with external links, including claims of a penalty "starting March 2026". A more recent guide reports that in July 2026 X's head of product said links-in-replies workarounds were no longer needed, and that Musk said links had not been penalised "for over a year". One August 2026 weight table lists "open link" with a small positive weight (0.2). — [PostInstantly X algorithm guide](https://postinstantly.com/guides/x-algorithm); [explainx Aug 2026 weights](https://explainx.ai/blog/x-algorithm-for-you-timeline-open-source-ranking-weights-august-2026); [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/twitter-algorithm)
- **[MEASURED]** 73% of consumers would buy from a competitor if a brand doesn't respond on social, so response speed in DMs and replies matters. — [Sprout Social press release](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)
- **[VENDOR]** Real estate DM follow-up: Instagram's messaging window closes 24 hours after the lead's last reply, a practical reason to answer DMs fast. — [Creatorflow: Real estate leads 24-hour rule](https://creatorflow.so/blog/real-estate-leads-social-media/)

### Inferences
- **Funnel for this brokerage:** posts and replies → profile (clear headline: "Myrtle Beach broker for relocators and STR investors") → a single link hub → (a) weekly newsletter (LinkedIn newsletter for LinkedIn followers, plus Beehiiv or Substack to own the email list) and (b) lead magnets. Magnet candidates:
  - "Myrtle Beach Relocation Cost Calculator"
  - "STR Cash-on-Cash Calculator by neighbourhood"
  - "Flood zone and insurance cheat sheet"
  - quarterly "Grand Strand Market Report" PDF
- **Link placement:** on LinkedIn, keep links out of the post body (comments or profile), since link posts have the lowest engagement. On X, test links in the post vs in a reply directly, because the evidence conflicts.
- **DM conversion:** use keyword CTAs ("Reply RELOCATE and I'll send the checklist"). Respond the same day, then move to email or phone.
- **Attribution:**
  - UTM links for every platform and person.
  - A "How did you hear about us?" field on every form, with a social/person option.
  - CRM source tags.
  - Monthly review of social-sourced leads, appointments and closings.

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found for follower-to-lead or follower-to-newsletter conversion rates for local service businesses.
- No newsletter conversion benchmarks were retrieved for Beehiiv, Substack or LinkedIn newsletters.
- No real estate-specific case of LinkedIn or X leads converting to closings was found. NAR data does not break social leads out by platform.

## 7. Which metrics matter and benchmarks

### Takeaway
Measured LinkedIn engagement-rate references: personal profiles about 2.6% vs company pages about 1.7% (directional). Company pages reach about 1.6% of followers per post. For a local service business, the metrics that matter are downstream: profile visits from target-audience people, replies or DMs from prospects, newsletter signups, booked consults and closings.

### Cited Findings
- **[DIRECTIONAL]** Engagement rate: personal profiles 2.60% vs company pages 1.74%. — [SocialNexis](https://socialnexis.com/guides/company-page-personal-post-conditions)
- **[MEASURED, secondary]** Company page post reach is about 1.6% of followers (van der Blom 2025). — [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)
- **[MEASURED]** Buffer's caution on comparing engagement rates across cadences: accounts posting once a week show higher engagement rate but fewer total engagements, so rate alone is misleading. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- **[SECONDARY]** LinkedIn signals ranked: saves > comments > likes, with dwell time and first-hour engagement important. — [SocialPilot LinkedIn algorithm](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)
- **[MEASURED, survey]** The lead-source share in NAR 2025 (39% social) is a starting point for checking whether social is pulling its weight in your own pipeline. — [NAR 2025 Technology Survey](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested weekly scorecard (in order of importance):
  1. Qualified conversations: DMs or replies from local, relocating or investor prospects.
  2. Newsletter signups and lead-magnet downloads.
  3. Profile visits.
  4. Follower growth rate, as a percentage per week. Early on, 5 to 15% per week is plausible from a small base, given the Tom Butler anecdote.
  5. Engagement per post: comments and saves rather than likes.
  6. Link clicks.
  7. Monthly: appointments and closings attributed to social.
- Track per person (founder, veteran, data guy) to see which voice converts.

## 8. AI in drafting (audience reaction, detection, "AI slop" backlash, human-in-the-loop workflows)

### Takeaway
About half of long-form LinkedIn posts from influential profiles are now likely AI-written, according to Originality.ai, which sells an AI detector. Likely-AI posts reportedly earn about 45% less engagement, but AI-drafted posts *edited by a human* reportedly match or beat purely human posts. Consumer surveys show a real but minority trust penalty for brand AI content (about 20 to 32% say it lowers trust), and enthusiasm for AI creator content fell sharply (60% in 2023 to 26% in 2025, eMarketer via Outlier). The defensible workflow: humans supply the opinions, stories and local facts; AI drafts and checks.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, vendor-conflicted]** Originality.ai: 53.7% of 3,368 long-form posts from 99 influential LinkedIn profiles were classified as likely AI-written. An earlier analysis of 8,795 posts (2018 to Oct 2024) found about 54%. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/); [Mind Matters](https://mindmatters.ai/brief/study-over-half-of-linkedin-posts-now-ai-generated/)
- **[SECONDARY]** Likely-AI LinkedIn posts reportedly get about 45% less engagement than likely-human posts. In leadership and inspiration content, AI posts reportedly outperformed by 75%. In healthcare, human posts drew 208 interactions vs 116 for AI. AI drafts edited by a human averaged 6.85% engagement vs 6.22% for purely human posts. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/); [SocialNexis: AI posts 45% less engagement](https://socialnexis.com/guides/ai-posts-engagement-decay-rate); [SocialNexis: format and AI posts](https://socialnexis.com/guides/ai-post-performance-by-format)
- **[MEASURED, reported]** A Pangram study of about a million feeds found LinkedIn leads all platforms in the share of AI-written posts (July 2026). — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320534/20260715/linkedin-leads-all-platforms-ai-written-posts-study-million-feeds-confirms.htm)
- **[MEASURED, survey]** Klaviyo: 61% of consumers are neutral about brands using AI-generated marketing content and 32% say it lowers trust. Another cut found 20% would trust a favourite brand less with heavy AI use (14% significantly). Nearly one in five see low-quality or generic AI brand content weekly. — [Klaviyo: Consumer trust in AI](https://www.klaviyo.com/solutions/ai/consumer-trust-in-ai); [Klaviyo AI consumer trends](https://www.klaviyo.com/marketing-resources/ai-consumer-trends)
- **[MEASURED, reported]** Gartner: 50% of US consumers prefer brands that keep generative AI out of consumer-facing content. eMarketer data shows consumer enthusiasm for AI-generated creator content fell from 60% (2023) to 26% (2025). — [Outlier Report](https://www.outlierreport.com/en/news/consumer-excitement-for-ai-content-crashed-from-60-to-26-and-now-anti-ai-is-the-premium)
- **[MEASURED, reported]** DoubleVerify (22,000 consumers, 22 markets): 42% would feel negatively toward a brand whose ads appeared next to low-quality, spam-like AI content. A 2026 study warns "AI slop poses growing risk to brand trust". — [Advanced Television, Aug 2026](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/08/06/study-ai-slop-poses-growing-risk-to-brand-trust); [Fortune, June 2026: Businesses declaring war on AI slop](https://fortune.com/2026/06/05/war-ai-slop-publicis-groupe-hachette-publishers-association/)
- **[SECONDARY]** A 2026 algorithm analysis claims generic AI content is being demoted on LinkedIn. Some sources claim fully AI-generated posts get about 2.8x less reach, but this appears in only one secondary source and is unverified. — [ZoomSphere: LinkedIn algorithm 2026](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-why-generic-ai-content-kills-your-organic-reach)
- **[MEASURED, survey]** NAR 2025 Technology Survey coverage: Realtors are embracing AI tools to improve client service. This is a sector norm, so the risk is in *how* AI is used, not whether. — [RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/09/19/survey-agents-embrace-ai-digital-tools-enhance-client-services/)

### Inferences
- **Workflow for three people plus AI:**
  1. Each human records a weekly 10-minute voice memo: deals, client questions, opinions, local observations.
  2. AI turns it into drafts in each person's voice profile.
  3. The human adds or edits one specific detail: a street name, a number, a feeling.
  4. An AI checker pass reviews claims, Fair Housing language, and voice-document and do-not-joke compliance.
  5. Approve and schedule.
  
  Never auto-post replies.
- **Avoid "AI tells":** generic inspirational framing, em-dash-heavy listicles, "Here's the thing", and invented stats. Local specificity (named neighbourhoods, actual price and insurance numbers, photos taken by the team) is the best defence and the main differentiator.
- **Disclosure:** no evidence was found that disclosing AI *assistance* on routine posts helps or hurts. A sensible policy is that the human named on the account owns every claim. Disclose AI for any synthetic imagery, such as AI-staged photos. That is also a real-estate advertising-ethics issue.

### Gaps
- The original Originality.ai report with the 45% engagement-gap methodology was not retrieved. The figure is secondary.
- No study was found on audience reaction to *disclosed* AI assistance on social posts specifically.
- No X-specific data on how AI-written posts perform was found.

## 9. Persona with a human-in-the-loop approval queue (edgy content, newsjacking speed, crisis playbooks, do-not-joke lists)

### Takeaway
Little published evidence exists on approval workflows for small teams. The one measured anchor is the newsjacking window: 27% of consumers think trend participation only works within 24 to 48 hours (Sprout 2025). Executive trust in social teams is also imperfect: 57% of marketing leaders say executives trust their social teams, vs 74% of executives who say they do. A two-lane approval system, with pre-approved templates for speed and full review for anything edgy, is an inference from practice rather than a measured best practice.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED]** 27% of consumers say trend-jumping is effective only within 24 to 48 hours. A third find brands jumping on trends embarrassing. — [Sprout Social press release](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)
- **[MEASURED]** 94% of social practitioners feel they must be "chronically online". Over a third report more creative fatigue than a year ago. 93% believe AI can help combat creative fatigue. — [Sprout Social press release](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)
- **[MEASURED]** 57% of marketing leaders say their executives trust their social teams, vs 74% of executives. — [Sprout Social press release](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)
- **[MEASURED]** 93% of consumers say brands need to do more to combat misinformation. This is relevant to a persona that "busts myths": claims must be accurate. — [Sprout Social Index 2025](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/index/)
- **[MEASURED]** 73% of consumers would switch to a competitor if a brand doesn't respond on social. Silence during a complaint or crisis is costly. — [Sprout Social press release](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)

### Inferences
- **Two-lane approval queue:**
  - **Green lane** (any one of the three can approve; target under 2 hours): weekly numbers, myth-busting backed by a cited source, neighbourhood content, reshares, replies that stick to data.
  - **Red lane** (founder or broker-in-charge must approve; target same day): anything edgy, humour about current events, political or adjacent topics (taxes, regulation, insurance, migration), named competitors, named people or properties, client stories, and anything touching Fair Housing protected classes.
  - **Pre-approved templates** keep newsjacking inside the 24 to 48 hour window. Examples: "Rates moved X — here's what it means for a $400K Myrtle Beach condo", hurricane prep and insurance explainers, NAR settlement and commission explainers. Only the numbers get filled in at publish time.
- **Do-not-joke list:**
  - hurricanes, storms and flooding while a storm is active or causing damage
  - deaths, crime and drownings
  - protected classes and neighbourhood "who lives there" framing (a Fair Housing risk; steering)
  - specific clients or other agents' listings without consent
  - politics and partisan figures
  - religion
  - local tragedies
  - elderly or retiree stereotypes, given the core retiree audience
  - mortgage hardship and foreclosures
- **Crisis and escalation playbook:**
  1. Pause scheduled posts (especially during hurricane events).
  2. Name one responder.
  3. Acknowledge publicly within hours, then move to DM or phone.
  4. Never delete critical comments unless they are abusive.
  5. Log incidents.
  6. Do a post-mortem with the voice document updated.
- **AI's role:** AI can draft the newsjacking take within minutes of the news and pre-check it against the do-not-joke list, but a human always presses publish.

### Gaps
- No published case studies or measured data were found on approval-workflow speed vs quality for small social teams.
- Real estate-specific compliance guidance (NAR Code of Ethics Article 12 on advertising, HUD Fair Housing advertising rules, SC Real Estate Commission advertising rules requiring the brokerage name) was **not researched in this session**. It should be verified separately before launch.
- No 2025-2026 social crisis playbook data (e.g., from Sprout or Hootsuite) was retrieved.
