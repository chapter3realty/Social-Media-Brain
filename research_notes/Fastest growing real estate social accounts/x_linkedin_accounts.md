# Fastest-growing real estate and housing accounts on X/Twitter and LinkedIn (2023-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-08. End reader: a small new brokerage in Myrtle Beach, SC.

**How the numbers were collected (read first):**
- "Measured" X counts come from api.fxtwitter.com, a public third-party mirror that returns the live X profile object (followers, join date, post count). I queried it on 2026-11-08. It is not an official X tool, and it returned false 404s for some handles because of rate limits. So a missing number means "not retrieved", not "account doesn't exist".
- "Measured" LinkedIn counts come from the follower number embedded in public LinkedIn post pages (JSON-LD `FollowAction` count), fetched on 2026-11-08.
- "Self-reported" means the creator's own site or bio. "Snapshot" means a search-engine cached profile page with an unclear date.
- I could not get historical follower curves (Social Blade-type data) for any X account. Wayback Machine snapshots of X profiles are JavaScript shells with no follower counts in them. So growth speed rests on scattered dated data points, and is thin for most accounts.

## Q1. Ranked shortlist: who grew biggest and fastest on X/LinkedIn, and what exactly they posted

### Takeaway
The biggest *text-platform* housing audiences built in 2021-2026 belong to two groups. The first is **data-chart analysts with a strong regional or contrarian angle**: Nick Gerli/Reventure, about 164K on X; Lance Lambert/ResiClub, about 110K on X, grown from roughly 3K in late 2021; Jay Parsons, about 131K on LinkedIn; and Calculated Risk, about 89K on X. The second is **one entertainment account**: Zillow Gone Wild, about 613K on X, which has been flat since 2024. The analysts all use the same format: one chart or table plus a short, numbers-first take. Each one turned the audience into a data product, newsletter or consulting business.

### Inferences
- The winning text-platform formula across Gerli, Lambert, Parsons, Mohtashami and Simonsen is the same: **one chart or table, a number-first opening line naming a specific metro, a 3-6 line take, and sometimes a closing question.** Gerli adds an alarming or contrarian frame ("crash", "panic mode"). Lambert stays neutral and data-only. Both grew, so tone matters less than having regional specificity and a steady chart supply.
- Growth came from **regional data that national media ignored**: Lambert's Boise/Austin/Tampa tables and Gerli's Florida/Texas/Seattle inventory posts. That suggests a Myrtle Beach brokerage owning "Grand Strand inventory and price-cut charts" is the closest replicable lane. Myrtle Beach is a Sun Belt/Florida-adjacent market of the kind these accounts already post about.
- Lambert's experience suggests X growth is back-loaded. He had about 3K followers for years, then grew roughly 35x in about 3 years (Nov 2021 to Nov 2026). The breakout coincided with the 2022 rate shock, when housing data became news.
- The bare CTA posts measured here (Lambert's newsletter plugs) reached a few thousand views, against ~150K for Gerli's chart posts. So the monetization move is a CTA attached to a chart or the end of a thread (Gerli's "11) ... head to the app" pattern), not standalone promo.
- Zillow Gone Wild shows entertainment accounts can get huge fast but plateau. The analyst accounts kept compounding and built real businesses: a data app, a newsletter plus research, consulting.

## Q3. What made LinkedIn real estate creators grow in 2024-2026, and documented agent/broker cases

### Takeaway
The best-documented LinkedIn housing growth case is an economist, not an agent: Jay Parsons, 131K followers, built with data-heavy text posts that end in a question. Platform data from 2025-2026 shows document (PDF) carousels have the highest LinkedIn engagement rate. I found no rigorous, dated case of a residential agent or broker building a six-figure LinkedIn following. A Charlotte broker with 10K followers posting daily gets only 1-2 reactions per post.

### Cited Findings
- Document carousels perform best (Inman, July 6, 2026):
  - 7% engagement rate, the highest of any LinkedIn format (Socialinsider, 1.3M posts, April 2026). Multi-image posts 6.45%; video 6%.
  - 39% more reach and 30% more engagement than the average post (AuthoredUp, 3M posts, Mar 2025-Feb 2026).
  - 12.92% of all saves, about 2.6x their share of content, yet only 4.88% of profiles post them regularly.
  - Source: [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/07/06/latest-data-reveals-social-media-engagement/)
- Overall engagement is falling: a Metricool study of 670K+ posts (Jan-Feb 2025 vs 2026) found likes down 13%, comments down 17%, shares down 10%, and clicks (including carousel swipes) up 5% — [via search summary of Chicago Agent / Metricool](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/2026/02/27/want-more-real-estate-leads-from-linkedin-heres-how-ai-can-make-it-happen/)

### Inferences
- On LinkedIn, the growth driver is **insight density, not posting volume.** Compare Parsons (data-rich, ~200-270 likes per post) with Braun (high-frequency tips and listings, 1-2 reactions).
- The format gap is the opportunity. Document carousels get the best engagement, yet fewer than 5% of profiles use them. A monthly "Grand Strand market in 8 slides" carousel and a weekly single-chart post are the evidence-backed starting set.
- Large LinkedIn followings in real estate cluster in CRE and multifamily, where the audience is investors and professionals. For a residential brokerage, LinkedIn is better for referral partners (relocation, employers, lenders, investors buying second homes) than for consumer reach.

### Gaps
- No documented, dated case found of a US residential agent or small brokerage growing a large LinkedIn following in 2024-2026 with measured numbers.
- No LinkedIn follower data for Glennda Baker, Jason Mitchell or Daryl Fairweather. LinkedIn returned HTTP 999 or nothing useful.
- Poll performance data on LinkedIn for real estate was not found.
