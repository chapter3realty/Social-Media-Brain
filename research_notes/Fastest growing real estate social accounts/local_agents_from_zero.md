# Local Agents, Teams and Small Brokerages That Grew Social From Near Zero Into Real Business (for Chapter3 Realty, Myrtle Beach SC)

Research date: 2026-11-08. Almost every business number below is **self-reported** by the agent, a podcast blurb, a paid press release or a vendor or coach selling services. None of the figures was checked against MLS records. Subscriber counts from Feedspot and similar directories are third-party snapshots and may be out of date. Where a number is self-reported, it says so.

## Q1. Which local agents grew from zero and documented results? (case studies with numbers, content and what made it work)

### Takeaway
The strongest documented "zero to business" cases come from relocation-focused, long-form YouTube channels named after a place ("Living in Dallas", "Living in Montana", "moving to Savannah"). A short-form TikTok personality (Glennda Baker) and a hyper-local Facebook group (Laura Griffin's Loudoun Moms) are the best-documented non-YouTube cases. Typical timelines are a first lead about 3 months after the first video and steady volume at about 10 months. The big outcomes, such as 164 sales in a year or 95% of leads from YouTube, are all self-reported.

### Cited Findings

**Case 1: Levi Lascsak, "Living in Dallas Texas" team (Dallas-Fort Worth, TX), YouTube, relocation**
- He started as a brand-new agent. First video: **December 5, 2020**. He reports **164 closed transactions in one year from organic video**, about **$90M+ production** and **$2.7M in commissions**, with zero ad spend. He co-founded the Living in Dallas Texas Team and treats YouTube "as a search engine, not just a social platform." Source: Wallet Watch podcast ep. 19, Jan 13, 2026 (self-reported) — [Wallet Watch](https://wallet-watch-mccauley.captivate.fm/episode/the-organic-lead-playbook-with-levi-lascsak)

- First two transactions closed in **April 2021**, about 4 months after the first video. The content was relocation-focused. — [Jamil Academy](https://www.jamilacademy.com/blog/youtube-for-real-estate-agents) (a coaching vendor's summary of the podcasts)
- Later podcast titles give bigger, inconsistent totals: "He's Sold 385 Homes with $0 in Ad Spend, Just YouTube" — [Everand/Rich Somers E152](https://www.everand.com/podcast/707639273/He-s-Sold-385-Homes-with-0-in-Ad-Spend-Just-YouTube-Levi-Lascsak-E152). **Case 2: Will Friedner, "Living in Montana" (YouTube, rural relocation market)**
- **36,000 subscribers and 3M+ views within 10 months**. Subscribers roughly doubled over the following year (no exact figure given). His top video has **3.5M views**, and about 95% of his YouTube traffic comes from that one video. He says **"95% of my leads come from YouTube."** Deal counts are not given. Source: Inbound REM agency spotlight, Feb 26, 2026 (self-reported plus agency write-up) — [Inbound REM](https://inboundrem.com/real-estate-youtube-spotlight-will-friedner-of-living-in-montana/)
- **What he posted:** The breakout was a **7-minute candid "reasons NOT to move to Montana" video** covering wildlife, local laws, weather, inconveniences and locals who resist change. Follow-ups covered off-grid and unusual towns, legal issues, Yellowstone's impact, the end of the housing boom and population loss. He says the best performers are plain **town tours**: "the videos that do the best are the ones where I just tour a town in Montana." Titles use tension and curiosity hooks. — [Inbound REM](https://inboundrem.com/real-estate-youtube-spotlight-will-friedner-of-living-in-montana/)
- **What made it work:** He targeted a search phrase with little competition (about 1,100 YouTube searches a month), was honest about the negatives during the COVID relocation surge, chose timely topics, and had few local copycats in an under-served rural market. — [Inbound REM](https://inboundrem.com/real-estate-youtube-spotlight-will-friedner-of-living-in-montana/)

- About **850,000 TikTok followers** by January 2023. One podcast title says she "Made Over $1.6M From Social Media Videos" (self-reported, and the figure probably includes non-commission income such as speaking and brand deals). — [Movoto agent help, 1/20/2023](https://agenthelp.movoto.com/pro-plus/glennda-baker-1/20/2023); [Everand](https://www.everand.com/podcast/710934340/How-Glennda-Baker-Made-Over-1-6M-From-Social-Media-Videos-Viral-sensation-and-top-Atlanta-real-estate-agent-Glennda-Baker-shares-how-she-built-an-ic)

She has a signature look (star wardrobe) and catchphrase ("Oh, my stars!"), and turns follower questions into videos. **Cadence:** she started at 2 videos a week, moved to daily, and **batch-records 30+ videos in one day each month**. — [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)

### Inferences
- The repeatable pattern in Cases 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 is to own a low-competition search phrase ("Living in X", "moving to X", "transferring to [base]") with long-form YouTube. Buyers searching those phrases are already planning a move, which is why a channel with only 2K–6K subscribers can still produce six figures of GCI.
- A single evergreen video can carry a business: Friedner gets about 95% of his traffic from one video, Carr reports $100K+ from one video, and Lawson reports 7–8 deals from one video. For Chapter3, this argues for a small number of very well-targeted search videos ("Moving to Myrtle Beach: pros and cons", "Myrtle Beach for retirees", "Buying a Myrtle Beach condo as a short-term rental") before chasing volume.
- Short-form personality (Baker) and Facebook community (Griffin) work too, but they depend more on persona and daily or community effort. Baker's lead flow skewed toward referrals and relocation inquiries rather than local listings.
- Expect about 3 months to the first lead and about 10 months to steady volume, so plan the budget and patience around a 12-month horizon.

### Gaps
- No case found with audited or MLS-verified attribution. All "deals from social" numbers are self-reported.
- Levi Lascsak's exact video topics and cadence were not available in text form (they are only in podcast audio).
- No documented case found for Arizona, the Carolinas or Tennessee with business numbers. See Q2 and Q3.
- BiggerPockets / Real Estate Rookie and Tom Ferry, Sean Cannell and Jeff Mack student interviews with numbers were not located in this pass.

## Q4. Common formats that convert for relocation markets

### Takeaway
Across the documented cases and relocation-agent channels in FL, SC, NC and TN, the converting formats are search-intent long-form videos: "pros and cons / downsides of moving to X," "watch this before moving to X," "don't move here unless," town and neighborhood tours or drive-throughs, cost of living, city-vs-city comparisons (often retirement-framed), "moving from [state] to X," military or employer transfer guides, new-construction community tours, and HOA, tax and condo-fee explainers. A free relocation guide and a Zoom consult usually follow as the call to action.

### Cited Findings
