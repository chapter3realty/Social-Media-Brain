# Fastest-Growing Real Estate TikTok / Instagram Accounts: What They Posted and How They Grew

Research date: 2026-11-08. About 19 tool calls. Several key pages were blocked (NPR 503, Dwell 403, Apartment Therapy 403, Curaytor 404), so some facts come from search-result snippets rather than full-page reads. Those are marked "(snippet)". Numbers marked "self-reported" come from the creator or their company. "Aggregator" means CreatorDB, VidIQ, OutlierKit, Favikon or a vendor blog: these are measured by a third party but not audited.

## 1. Ranked shortlist: who grew biggest and fastest, with measured growth

### Takeaway
The best-documented fast climb by a working agent is Glennda Baker: 122 to more than 565K TikTok followers in about 12 months (Oct 2020 to late 2021), reaching about 870-880K by 2023, with $241K of 2021 commission credited to TikTok (self-reported). The biggest real estate accounts overall are not ordinary agents. They are a listing-curation meme page (Zillow Gone Wild, about 4M+ across platforms by 2024), a luxury house-tour YouTuber (Enes Yilmazer, about 4.2M YouTube subscribers), and a TV-star broker (Ryan Serhant, about 6M across platforms).

## 2. Exactly what they posted: formats, hooks, series, length, cadence, persona and conversion

### Takeaway
The winners each picked one repeatable format and posted it at high frequency. Glennda Baker posted daily "overheard friend interview" storytelling with a strong persona. Enes posts long-form, unscripted luxury tours. Zillow Gone Wild reposts bizarre listings with jokey captions. DuBois posts hide-and-seek listing videos, and Gen Z agents post pop-culture parodies. Conversion came through relocation inquiries, agent referrals, listing leads, and for the biggest names, paid shoots, TV, coaching or a brokerage.

### Cited Findings

**Glennda Baker**
- **Format:** interview-style videos framed like a friend overhearing a conversation. Her videographer, Denver Bailey, plays the "friend" off camera. — [NAR Magazine, Apr 2022](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)
- **Topics:** everyday agent life, career anecdotes, the feeling after a big commission check, and things viewers don't already know, not only fancy listings. Follower questions in comments often become new videos. A recurring story is selling 27 houses through Match.com — [NAR Magazine](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)
- **Persona:** candid, tell-it-like-it-is, Southern drawl, catchphrase "Oh, my stars!", and star-patterned clothing as a signature look — [NAR Magazine](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)
- **Cadence:** started at 2 videos a week, then moved to daily. She batch-records 30+ videos in one day each month, and followers DM her if she skips a day — [NAR Magazine](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)
- **Engagement:** daily videos get 30K to 2M likes. A "Why are you following me?" video drew 8,000+ comments — [NAR Magazine](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)
- **Business results (self-reported):** $241,000 in GCI from TikTok in 2021 — [NAR Magazine](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok). ### Inferences
- **Patterns across winners:**
  - **One locked format, repeated:** Baker's overheard interview, DuBois's hide-and-seek, Enes's tour, ZGW's weird listing.
  - **A recognizable persona or signature:** Baker's catchphrase and stars, Serhant's TV persona.
  - **Daily or near-daily posting, made possible by batching:** Baker's 30 videos in one monthly shoot day; Carruth's 10+ Threads posts a day.
  - **Audience questions recycled into content.**
  - **Humor or spectacle over plain listing walkthroughs.**

## 3. Zillow Gone Wild's growth and its legal problems; other backfires

### Takeaway
Zillow Gone Wild grew by reposting other people's listing photos with comedy captions. That led to a July 2024 copyright suit by a real estate photographer. In September 2025 a judge let the claims proceed but barred statutory damages and attorneys' fees, because the photos were registered after the alleged infringement. The parties then dismissed the case with prejudice on October 10, 2025, with no terms disclosed. Agent-level backfires found were mainly employment and fair-housing conduct cases, not content discipline.

### Cited Findings
- Photographer Jennifer Bouma sued Zillow Gone Wild in July 2024 over two images of a Monroe, Washington home, seeking up to $150K per photo. Inman framed it as a "$300K copyright infringement suit" — [PetaPixel, Aug 5 2024](https://petapixel.com/2024/08/05/real-estate-photographer-sues-zillow-gone-wild-for-using-property-pictures); [Inman, Aug 5 2024](https://inman.com/2024/08/05/zillow-gone-wild-gets-hit-with-300k-copyright-infringement-suit/amp); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91167268/zillow-gone-wild-sued-copyright-infringement)
- In September 2025 the court refused to dismiss the claims over social media and blog posts using her images. Statutory damages and attorneys' fees were barred because the infringement came about 5 months before she registered the photos with the Copyright Office (snippet) — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/zillow-gone-wild-fails-to-avoid-copyright-infringement-claims)
- The parties filed a dismissal with prejudice on October 10, 2025 in the Eastern District of New York. No terms were disclosed (snippet) — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/zillow-gone-wild-photographer-end-copyright-infringement-suit)
- **Puyallup, WA:** a viral TikTok of an HOA dispute involving an agent and her husband (the HOA president) led to an arrest and loss of jobs. Terrafin Real Estate said it terminated the couple, while the couple said they left. A complaint was forwarded to the Human Rights Commission, with no finding seen (snippet) — [FOX 13 Seattle](https://www.fox13seattle.com/news/puyallup-hoa-realtor-neighbor-viral-tiktok)
- **Michigan:** a Realtor was fired over a racially insensitive social media post and was later sued under the Fair Housing Act (not TikTok-specific) — [Inman, Jan 20 2021](https://inman.com/2021/01/20/interracial-couple-suing-michigan-agents-for-alleged-discrimination)
- NAR Legal published social media and fair housing best practices for agents — [Illinois Realtors](https://www.illinoisrealtors.org/blog/nar-legal-video-gives-4-best-practices-regarding-social-media-and-fair-housing/)
- On Million Dollar Listing, Serhant's co-stars (Fredrik Eklund, Luis Ortiz) accused him of buying Instagram followers. This is an on-show accusation, not verified (snippet) — [Favikon](https://favikon.com/blog/who-is-ryan-serhant)

### Inferences
- **For a brokerage copying a "roast the listing" or "Zillow Gone Wild" format:**
  - Use only photos you own or have licensed, such as your own listings or your own photographer's work with rights in the contract.
  - Do not repost MLS photos of other agents' listings.
  - Get seller consent before featuring their homes in humor content.
  - Avoid neighborhood descriptions that steer buyers by protected class, such as "great for young families" or comments on who lives there.

### Gaps
- No documented case was found of a state commission or Realtor association disciplining an agent specifically for TikTok or Instagram content about seller privacy or other agents' listings. One may exist, but it did not surface here.
- Settlement terms in Bouma v. Zillow Gone Wild are undisclosed.
- I could not read the Dwell article on whether ZGW virality helps or hurts sales (403 error).
