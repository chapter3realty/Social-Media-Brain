# Chapter3 Wins Social With Receipts, Not Roasts

Chapter3 Realty should build its social personality as an **"honest insider with receipts."** That means a written character owned by the brokerage, not by any one employee. It leads with the building-level data that nobody on the Grand Strand publishes on text platforms, and it wraps that data in humor about weather, tourists, traffic, HOA paperwork, the real estate industry's habits and Chapter3's own newness. It never jokes about clients, protected groups, named competitors or members of the team. Three findings drive the recommendation. **First, the ranking systems:** X's open-sourced 2026 ranker gives replies, quotes and shares far more weight than likes, but it charges **-234 for a predicted report and -58.8 for a mute**. A voice that starts arguments grows, and a voice that annoys people sinks. LinkedIn's October 2026 classifiers cut views of generic "AI slop" by a reported **40%**, so only specific, local, opinionated posts travel. **Second, the law:** South Carolina treats even a general "market trends" post by a licensee as advertising that must name the firm. Fair housing law, Regulation Z, RESPA, the FTC's review rules and defamation law turn most classic "edgy brand" moves (roasting other agents' listings, neighborhood jokes, rate teasers, skits with fake clients) into liability. **Third, the track record:** the brands that made humor pay (Wendy's, Duolingo, Monzo, small-town police pages) all had a written voice, a consent rule for roasts, one fast approver and recurring formats. The ones that failed tripped on tragedy timing, culture-war fights, or a gap between the voice and the company's real conduct. An AI-assisted "Social Media Brain" makes this safe only if it **drafts and checks but never posts or replies on its own**. It should draft from the website's own data, run about two dozen machine-enforceable compliance checks, send edgy content to the Broker-in-Charge, pause itself during hurricanes and local tragedies, and leave every reply, comment and publish click to a human. Expect hundreds of followers in the first quarter, not tens of thousands. So the targets should be qualified conversations and website-tool users, not follower counts.

## One report on X cancels about 47 replies, and LinkedIn now buries generic AI posts

**X's "For You" feed is ranked by a Grok-based transformer called Phoenix**, and its code is public. X published the ranking code under Apache 2.0 in January 2026 and added the scoring weights in August 2026 ([xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm); [Keywords Everywhere timeline](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/social-algorithm-updates/x-algorithm-open-source/)). For each viewer, the model predicts the probability of each action, multiplies each prediction by a weight and sums the results. The current weights file, read on 7 November 2026, shows where the value sits ([param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)):

| Signal (predicted probability × weight) | Weight | What it means for Chapter3 |
|---|---|---|
| Share via copy link | 20.0 | Posts people forward to a spouse or business partner ("send this to whoever is moving with you") are the most valuable |
| Reply | 5.0 | A post that invites an answer beats a post that invites a like |
| Reply from a mutual follow | +15.0 boost | Two-way relationships with local accounts make their replies worth four times as much |
| Quote, share via DM | 5.0 each | Data that other accounts want to argue with or pass on |
| Follow author | 4.0 | Each post should give a reason to follow (a recurring series) |
| Like | 0.708 | Close to worthless on its own |
| Bookmark, profile click, video open | 0.0 | Not directly rewarded |
| Report | -234.0 | One predicted report cancels about 47 replies |
| Mute author / Not interested / Block | -58.8 / -53.07 / -31.2 | Reflexive snark aimed at people is very costly |

The surrounding rules matter as much as the weights. An **AgeFilter drops posts older than 48 hours** from For You. Each author's second and later posts in a viewer's session are discounted. Posts from accounts the viewer does not follow are discounted. A **new-author boost** lifts accounts that are below an impression threshold, and the code sets a cold-start threshold of 200 impressions ([README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm); [param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)). For a new account, this rewards steady daily posting spaced through the day, not bursts. Commits from late summer 2026 reportedly added an **LLM reply-spam classifier** that takes the replier's follower count and the original poster's bio as inputs ([Keywords Everywhere](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/social-algorithm-updates/x-algorithm-open-source/)). In other words, mass "reply guy" behavior is now policed by a model. Two points remain unsettled. Buffer's study of about 18.8M posts found median reach under 100 impressions per post for free accounts, about 600 for Premium and about 1,550 for Premium+, yet the 2026 code contains no visible Premium weight ([Social Media Today on Buffer](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-shows-paying-for-x-twitter-premkum-has-significant-reach-benefits/801881)). On links, X leadership said in July 2026 that link posts have not been penalized "for over a year." The code gives "open link" a small positive weight, though critics argue link posts still lose reach because they earn less dwell time ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/x-drops-year-old-link-penalty-musk-tells-zuckerberg-on-platform/)). One cost is not disputed: under X's pay-per-use API, a post with a link reportedly costs **$0.20, against $0.015 for a plain post** ([Postproxy](https://postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/xs-20-fold-api-surge-for-link-posts-hits-news-aggregators-and-developers-hard/)). The Brain should therefore send link posts through X's native scheduler, not through the API.

**LinkedIn rebuilt its feed around LLMs in March 2026, and since October 2026 it screens every post shown beyond the author's first-degree network for "AI slop."** LinkedIn defines slop as "generic and/or repetitive posts that added no real insight." It reports 94% precision for the classifier and a 40% fall in views for flagged content. LinkedIn's product chief said AI-assisted posts that contain specific examples or a clear point of view are not the target ([SocialDay](https://socialday.live/features/linkedin-will-cut-reach-of-ai-generated-posts-with-94-precision-screening-every)). In February 2026 LinkedIn said comments posted "through a third party auto-script or a browser plugin" without human review will be hidden from the default comment view, and accounts that keep doing it may be restricted ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-outlines-more-measures-to-combat-engagement-pods/812290/)). The User Agreement separately bans bots, scraping plugins and "inauthentic engagement" ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). Organic reach is falling. Richard van der Blom's 2026 report is cited for **views down about 50% and follower growth down about 59%**, though another reading of the data puts the median reach decline nearer 19% ([Perroly](https://www.perroly.com/blog/linkedin-organic-2026-what-works); [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-why-generic-ai-content-kills-your-organic-reach)). Format data is consistent across sources. **Document carousels lead at 7.00% engagement by impressions, and link posts trail at 3.3%** ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/linkedin-content-study/)). Buffer's study of 2M+ posts found that accounts get more impressions per post in their busier weeks and showed no penalty for frequent posting ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)). Company pages are weak growth vehicles: a page post reaches about **1.6% of the page's followers** ([Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)).

The secondary platforms differ in scale and in what they demand. **Threads had 147M daily users in July 2026, up 21% year over year. Bluesky's mobile monthly users fell to about 10.4M, roughly half its late-2024 peak** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/blueskys-active-user-base-is-shrinking-as-its-focus-expands-beyond-the-app/)). No source in the research measured how real estate content performs on either platform. Reddit has **no sitewide self-promotion ratio** anymore; each subreddit's rules govern, and some ban fully AI-written posts outright ([ForkOff audit](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/reddit-marketing/reddit-self-promotion-rules-2026)). Since March 2026, Reddit requires human verification from accounts that show automated or "fishy" behavior ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/26/reddit-human-verification-changes/)). Google began quoting Reddit and forum threads in AI Overviews in May 2026, so a good answer in r/MyrtleBeach keeps working long after it is posted. Reddit's share of ChatGPT citations, however, reportedly collapsed in Q3 2026 ([AI Rank Lab](https://www.airanklab.com/blog/reddit-ai-search-visibility)).

## Funny brands won on written rules, consent and one fast approver

The successful personality accounts are less spontaneous than they look. **Wendy's wrote down its voice as "challenger with charm"** and set a consent rule for roasts: "When folks say, 'roast me,' we're going to have fun with that" ([Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/unclick/wendys-twitter-burn-interview/)). After its original writer left, the voice survived because it had been turned into a ritual, National Roast Day. TikTok's own case study (self-reported) credits the 2023 edition with 116M hashtag views and 153,900 new followers ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/wendys-national-roast-day-tiktok)). Duolingo's social lead described approval as "me pitching her an idea and she's able to be like 'yes, no, yes, no,'" and called long approval chains "the biggest enemy of creativity." The company measured impact with a plain "how did you hear about us" question at sign-up ([Link in Bio](https://www.milkkarten.net/p/lets-talk-about-duolingo-on-tiktok); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot)). Ryanair jokes openly about its own known downside, cheap and no-frills service ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2023/11/10/lessons-ryanair-the-art-and-rules-going-gonzo-tiktok)). Steak-umm proved that a sincere, opinionated explainer, its 2020 misinformation thread, can live inside an irreverent account and earn national coverage ([Pittsburgh Post-Gazette](https://post-gazette.com/business/tech-news/2020/04/28/steak-umm-twitter-coronavirus-pandemic-viral-allebach-communications/stories/202004280087)).

**Regulated and trust-based brands keep their edge away from the regulated promise itself.** Monzo moves fast because compliance pre-approved a written tone-of-voice guide, so individual posts do not each need management sign-off ([Whito case study](https://whito.co.uk/stolen-with-pride/monzo/); [Monzo tone of voice](https://medium.com/monzo-bank/introducing-the-monzo-tone-of-voice-23d89ee20433)). The closest analog to a small local licensed firm is the small-town police page. Brimfield Township, Ohio, with about 10,000 residents, drew 155,000+ Facebook likes with blunt humor. Bangor, Maine's recurring "Duck of Justice" prop helped its page pass 260,000 followers ([Ohio AG bulletin](https://ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Media/Newsletters/Law-Enforcement-Bulletin/November-2013/Brimfield-Township-Police-Tally-More-Than-91-000-F); [Maine Public](https://www.mainepublic.org/post/why-hundreds-thousands-people-are-drawn-bangor-police-s-facebook-page)). The formula, in one paper's words, is "come for the laughs, stay for the public safety warnings" ([Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/minnesota-police-on-twitter-come-for-the-laughs-stay-for-the-public-safety-warnings/485607781)). A peer-reviewed study of these pages warns that agencies need a formal team "to avoid reliance on a single manager of the accounts" ([Policing case study](https://aminer.org/pub/5e5e192d93d709897ce58e38/small-police-agency-use-of-social-media-positive-and-negative-outcomes-noted)). That warning applies directly to a three-person firm whose social media runs through one person.

The research on humor supports "helpful first, funny second." In Oracle's 14-country survey, **91% of consumers said they prefer funny brands**, yet only 18% of brands use humor online ([Oracle](https://oracle.com/au/news/announcement/oracle-cx-happiness-research-study-2022-06-15)). When Sprout Social asked what consumers want on social, honesty (86%) and helpfulness ranked above humor. Only about a third wanted snark "full force," and only 33% wanted personality on LinkedIn, against 83% on Facebook ([Sprout Social 2017](https://investors.sproutsocial.com/news/news-details/2017/Data-Consumers-Value-Customer-Service-over-Attitude-from-Brands-on-Social/default.aspx)). Simply responding is the most memorable thing a brand can do for 51% of consumers ([Sprout 2023](https://www.tipranks.com/news/press-releases/new-research-indicates-a-shift-in-what-consumers-find-memorable-on-social-media)). On controversy, Chen and Berger's *Journal of Consumer Research* study found that **controversy increases conversation only up to a moderate level**. Past that point, discomfort suppresses sharing ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/finding-companys-controversy-sweet-spot/)). This study is the single most useful guide for a "willing to be controversial" brokerage. In professional categories the bar is low: about 75% of B2B ads tested by System1 scored the lowest rating for emotional response ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/01/08/after-decades-consistent-decline-humor-advertising-roaring-back-2024)).

**Backfires come from four places, and three of them hit a coastal brokerage directly.** The first is tragedy timing. Epicurious's scheduled promotional tweets tied to the Boston Marathon bombing forced more than a dozen apologies, and Cheerios's logo-branded Prince tribute was deleted ([MarTech](https://martech.org/epicurious-becomes-latest-brand-to-suffer-social-backlash-from-tragedy-related-tweets); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/04/21/cheerios-tweet-prince)). The Grand Strand has hurricane seasons and rip-current drownings every summer. The second is culture-war fights that make a brand appear to change sides on its core customer. Bud Light's retail dollar sales fell 24.6% in four weeks, and Cracker Barrel's stock fell more than 12% in a day before it reversed a logo change ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/bud-light-sales-modelo-especial-top-selling); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/08/26/business/cracker-barrel-logo-scrapped/)). The third is a gap between the persona and the company's conduct. After Duolingo's CEO announced an "AI-first" plan to replace contractors, the playful owl account had to wipe its posts and reportedly lost 400K+ TikTok followers ([YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2025/05/22/duolingo-deleted-their-instagram-and-tiktok-posts-after-getting-backlash-over-ai/)). The CEO later said the company was "skittish" about edgy posts, and daily-user growth landed at the bottom of guidance ([Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/duolingos-ceo-says-learned-hard-062205679.html)). This case is the most relevant warning for an AI-assisted brand: if Chapter3's persona is "the honest insider," then bragging about AI replacing human judgment, a hidden fee or a pushy sales practice will be judged against that persona. The fourth is losing the voice's owner. Wendy's, Steak-umm, Ryanair and Duolingo all lost their signature writers ([The Drum on Ryanair](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2023/11/10/lessons-ryanair-the-art-and-rules-going-gonzo-tiktok)).

## Nobody on the Grand Strand owns building-level data on text platforms

National housing voices grow on a repeatable formula: **a chart, a one-line thesis, often a named-metro call, and a link to a paid product.** Nick Gerli's numbered threads end with calls to action such as "sign up for a premium account for $39" for Reventure's ZIP-level forecasts ([X post](https://x.com/nickgerli1/status/2032116996460925110)). Local TV then picks up his metro crash calls, as WKRN did for downtown Nashville condos ([WKRN](https://www.wkrn.com/news/local-news/nashville/reventure-app-ceo-nick-gerli-warns-of-downtown-nashville-housing-crash/)). Logan Mohtashami built the opposite brand at HousingWire, arguing that "housing crash addicts have been wrong since 2013" ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/podcast/logan-mohtashami-on-inflation-data-and-why-were-not-seeing-a-housing-crash/)). Lance Lambert's ResiClub runs a free newsletter, a $299-a-year PRO tier and a "terminal" ([ResiClub](https://www.resiclubanalytics.com/p/special-message-resiclub-editor-lance-lambert-ca7a)). Jay Parsons reaches about 150,000 people across LinkedIn and X and averages about half a million impressions a week, mostly on LinkedIn ([NMHC bio](https://www.nmhc.org/globalassets/meetings/speaker-headshots/bios/jay-parsons-bio-2026.pdf)). Being wrong costs these media businesses little. In 2024 Fortune noted that most of the boomtowns Gerli had flagged were still up year over year ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/06/25/pandemic-boomtowns-florida-texas-overvalued-crashing/)). For a licensed local broker the risk runs the other way. A wrong crash call damages credibility with sellers, and a right one scares away listings. Chapter3 should therefore copy the format (chart, thesis, data link) and skip the forecasts.

The agent celebrities grew on video, not text. Glennda Baker went from 122 TikTok followers to more than 565,000 in about a year and self-reported six-figure income from TikTok leads ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)). Ricky Carruth reached 10M monthly organic views, and his content business sells coaching to other agents ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/12/01/how-this-agent-turned-coach-went-from-no-social-media-to-10m-monthly-organic-views/)). On text platforms, the data analysts are the ones who built audiences. A data brokerage whose customers are consumers fits the analyst model and its newsletter funnel better than the coach model.

Locally, attention belongs to news outlets on Facebook. WPDE has about 642K Facebook followers and 77K on X, WMBF about 364K and 52K, and the Sun News about 225K and 47K, according to an unverified aggregator ([Feedspot](https://journalists.feedspot.com/myrtle_beach_news_websites/)). On the agent side, Jerry Pinkas claims "the #1 YouTube Channel (for real estate) in Myrtle Beach" and writes SEO posts on condos and no-HOA neighborhoods ([Home Guide Myrtle Beach](https://www.homeguidemyrtlebeach.com/agents/)). The Short Term Shop publishes Myrtle Beach rental-expense content, which makes it a direct competitor in the short-term-rental niche ([The Short Term Shop](https://theshorttermshop.com/myrtle-beach-short-term-rental-expenses/)). Meanwhile, buyers ask raw questions in public. A local on r/MyrtleBeach warned that near the beach "insurance has become astronomical and now often requires an additional big assessment beyond HOA" ([r/MyrtleBeach thread](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/MyrtleBeach/comments/1evm6ml/thinking_of_buying_a_house_in_myrtle_beach_what)). BiggerPockets investors report HOA insurance pass-throughs of "400+ a month" ([BiggerPockets](https://biggerpockets.com/forums/530/topics/1184792-insurance-premiums-and-weather-events-impacting-occupancy)). **No Grand Strand brokerage turned up doing building-by-building data on X, LinkedIn or Reddit.** That covers rental returns, HOA costs, master-policy insurance increases and permit pipelines, which is exactly what chapter3realty.com already publishes. This is a white space, though the research did not search exhaustively. The window is narrowing: ResiClub now runs webinars teaching agents to "build a local housing market newsletter" ([ResiClub webinar](https://www.resiclubanalytics.com/p/build-local-housing-market-newsletter-resiclub-pro-webinar-recording)).

The proven controversial topics carry very different risks. **Commission takes after the NAR settlement are the most engaging and the most dangerous.** Agents flooded TikTok with complaints ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/tag/settlement/page/4/)), consumers cheer anti-commission posts and agents attack them. Crash calls are risky for the reasons above. Coastal insurance, HOA reserves, special assessments, flood zones and short-term-rental rules are consumer-protective topics where a data brokerage looks trustworthy rather than self-serving. They are Chapter3's best controversy zone. Listing roasts are the trap. A photographer sued Zillow Gone Wild for $300K over two listing photos, and a judge ruled in 2025 that the account had to face the infringement claims ([Inman](https://inman.com/2024/08/05/zillow-gone-wild-gets-hit-with-300k-copyright-infringement-suit); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/zillow-gone-wild-fails-to-avoid-copyright-infringement-claims)). McMansion Hell drew a Zillow cease-and-desist and stopped sourcing photos from Zillow ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMansion_Hell)). In South Carolina, as the next section shows, roasting another firm's local listing is also a license-law violation.

## South Carolina makes every post an ad, so the guardrails must be machine-checkable

The base rule is broader than most agents assume. Since 15 May 2025, S.C. Code § 40-57-135(E)(2) requires a licensee advertising in "any medium" to "identify the full name of the real estate brokerage firm." The Real Estate Commission says this covers "a social media post regarding an area of town generally" and posts "regarding trends, without including any identifying features or specific properties" ([SC REC advertising memo](https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/News/AdvertisingMemo-H.4754.pdf)). Online, a link "from the advertisement" to the firm's homepage also satisfies the rule. A link in a bio arguably does not, so the safe standard is the literal string "Chapter3 Realty" in every post or graphic. The current statute does not require a phone number ([SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)). Advertising another firm's listing requires written authorization and clear credit to the listing firm. The Commission says even "simply referencing a property's address in any form," including in "market updates," is advertising ([SC REC 2025 reminder](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)). A caption added to someone else's listing is the poster's own liability. The Broker-in-Charge must supervise compliance personally, and fines reach **$10,000 per violation** ([SC Code Title 40 Ch. 57](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php)). No South Carolina disciplinary order for a social post was found, but Ohio's regulator has pursued a licensee over personal Facebook comments ([Hondros](https://www.hondros.com/resources/blog/can-real-estate-commission-discipline-personal-facebook-page/)).

| C15 | Staff or partner praise carries a material-connection disclosure in the first line | 16 CFR 255.5, 465.5 | Binding |
| C16 | No bought followers, likes or views; no engagement pods | 16 CFR 465.8 | Binding |
| C17 | Extract every named or identifiable person, firm, builder, HOA, building or official; block factual claims about crime, fraud, defects, finances or competence unless a source is attached | [Milkovich](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/1/); [Erickson v. Jones Street](https://law.justia.com/cases/south-carolina/supreme-court/2006/26150.html); NAR Art. 15 | Binding |
| C18 | Images not made by Chapter3 need a license or permission record; MLS, Zillow and Realtor.com photos are blocked by default | 17 U.S.C. § 106/501; [VHT v. Zillow](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/17-35587/17-35587-2019-03-15.html) | Binding |

| C23 | Archive every post with its check results and approver; keep mortgage-related posts at least 24 months | Reg N 1014.5; § 40-57-135(A)(1) supervision | Binding / best practice |

Four of these checks need explanation because they cut against intuition. **First, the 55+ audience creates familial-status risk.** Age is not a protected class under the Fair Housing Act, but households with children are. Phrases like "empty-nester paradise" or "quiet adult neighborhood" can read as "not for families" to the "ordinary reader" the law uses as its test. Only a verified 55+ community may be advertised as adults-only ([24 CFR 100.75](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75)). Posts for retirees should describe features such as single-level floor plans, distance to hospitals and golf views, not the kind of people who live there. HUD's 2024 guidance on digital advertising was withdrawn in 2026, but the statute is unchanged ([Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-04-10/hud-withdraws-eight-fair-housing-guidance-documents-as-part-of-deregulatory-review/)). **Second, the owner's no-numbers rule is the right rule for social.** Regulation Z applies to "all persons," including brokers, and any stated payment or down payment triggers full APR disclosures. Banning the numbers outright is simpler and safer than trying to disclose correctly inside a joke. **Third, the BrickWood relationship needs care precisely because Chapter3 does not own BrickWood.** Without an ownership-based affiliated business arrangement, RESPA allows no thing of value to flow to Chapter3 for referrals at all, beyond fair-market payment for real services ([12 U.S.C. § 2607](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/2607)). Free boosts, lender-paid co-branded posts and leads traded for promotion are the classic violations. **Fourth, the fines stack.** FTC civil penalties under the review rule reached about $51,744 per violation in 2024 figures ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new)), and South Carolina's telephone law allows $1,000 per text, or $5,000 if willful ([SC § 37-21](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c021.php)).

Membership in NAR changes the list. NAR's Article 15 bars "false or misleading statements about other real estate professionals," including repeating someone else's false claim, and SoP 15-3 requires removing a known false statement from media the member controls ([NAR 2026 Code](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics/2026-code-of-ethics-standards-of-practice)). NAR's 2026 case interpretations show the line: insulting a political party on a business account was not a violation, while racist slurs on a "personal" account used for business were ([NAR 2026 case changes](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/MNREALTOR/488b06e6-56a7-4890-bb7a-ab79ba70337a/UploadedImages/Webpages/Legal/Documents/2026-Summary-of-PS-Changes-Effective-01-01-2026.pdf)). Section 230 does not protect Chapter3's own jokes, only comments posted by others ([47 U.S.C. § 230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230)). The practical joke-writing test therefore follows *Milkovich*: hyperbole that no reasonable reader would take as fact is protected, but an implied provable fact about an identifiable business is not. "This kitchen has more beige than a 1994 beach rental" passes. "That builder's condos are falling apart" does not. The Brain should sort every topic into three tiers before drafting:

| Tier | Topics | Who approves |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Weather, traffic, tourists in general, golf carts, food, humidity, Chapter3's own newness, property features (popcorn ceilings), market data from Chapter3's own datasets, myth-busting with a cited source | Any trained writer; under 2 hours |
| Amber | Current events, insurance and tax policy, short-term-rental ordinances, named officials, named buildings or HOAs, lawsuits, the commission debate, disasters after they pass, client stories | Broker-in-Charge; same day |
| Red (blocked) | Protected-class jokes, immigration, religion as an amenity, crime by area, partisan politics and candidates, active storms and fatalities, other firms' listings, team members as joke targets, mortgage numbers | Not posted |

## The best persona is an honest insider who jokes about weather, not people

The research supports six workable archetypes and rules out one.

| Archetype | Model | Strength | Main risk | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Honest insider | Steak-umm candor, Ryanair self-awareness | Turns compliance-safe education ("what the listing won't tell you about the master policy") into content | Must be backed by data every time | Core |
| Receipts desk / contrarian analyst | Gerli format, Mohtashami counter-punching, moderate controversy | Fights national hype with local numbers; quotable by media | Forecast liability if it makes calls | Core |
| Local know-it-all | Small-town police pages | Builds a following beyond active buyers: tourist season, hurricane prep, where locals eat | Drifting into "who lives where" | Supporting |
| Veteran storyteller | Founder-face trust (Scrub Daddy, Brimfield's chief) | 30+ years of Grand Strand memory; highest trust for relocators and retirees | Leaves if the person leaves; cannot be a joke target | LinkedIn face |
| Recurring character or prop | Duck of Justice, Wendy's Roast Day | Survives staff turnover; gives fans a ritual | Can feel gimmicky; still must show the firm name | Optional, later |
| "Unhinged" brand | Duolingo owl | Huge reach on video | Mutes and reports on X; fair-housing slips; voice-conduct gaps | Reject |

The recommendation is a hybrid. **The brand account is an "honest insider with receipts,"** an institutional voice that knows every building's numbers and says the quiet part out loud, kindly. **Timmy's 30 years supply the trust on LinkedIn.** Abdulla writes and operates both, with a second trained writer as backup. The voice line, in the Wendy's style, could be "**We show you the numbers the brochure skips, and we're nice about it.**" Edelman's 2025 data shows "my employer" is the most trusted institution at 76% ([Fortune on Edelman](https://fortune.com/2025/01/14/edelman-ceo-essay-trust-barometer-25th-anniversary/)), which supports putting a named, licensed human in front of high-stakes buyers. Writing the character down protects the voice against the turnover that broke Steak-umm's and Ryanair's.

**"Willing to be controversial" should mean contrarian about claims, never about people.** Chen and Berger's moderate zone, applied to Chapter3, covers takes like "an ocean view is not a business plan," followed by net returns by building from Chapter3's own data. It covers "the cheapest unit in the building can be the most expensive to own once the assessment lands," with that building's assessment history, and quote-posts of national crash or boom calls answered with Grand Strand months-of-supply figures. It also covers myths relocators bring with them, settled with tax and insurance numbers. Each of these provokes replies and quotes, which X weights at 5, without provoking reports, which X weights at -234. They also cast Chapter3 as protecting the buyer, which keeps the buyer-first rule intact. On commissions, the persona should publish how buyer-broker agreements and fees work in South Carolina as plain data and decline to take sides. If Chapter3 ever makes a prediction, it should log it in a public "prediction ledger" and grade itself each quarter. That turns honesty into a recurring format and avoids the soft accountability of the national crash callers.

The social voice has to differ from Blog Brain's strictly literal voice, because humor runs on hyperbole, irony and figures of speech. The way to keep both is a **two-layer rule: every post carries a literal fact layer that would pass Blog Brain's rules, and humor may sit only around that layer, never in place of it.**

| Dimension | Blog Brain (website) | Social Media Brain |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Pages a beginner can read and act on | Get noticed, start a conversation, send readers to the page |
| Figurative language | None: no metaphors, idioms or teasers | Allowed in the joke layer; never applied to numbers, legal terms, costs, risks or property condition |
| The fact | Stated literally | The core claim is still one literal, checkable sentence with a date and source |
| Teasers | Banned | Still banned: the main point appears in the post itself; "thread below" may add detail but may not hold back the answer |
| Hyperbole | None | Only about non-factual matters (weather, beige carpet, tourist traffic, Chapter3's newness); never about identifiable people or businesses |
| Honesty and buyer-first | Absolute | Absolute: a joke may never make a cost look smaller or a risk look funnier than it is |
| Rates, payments, down payments | Never | Never |
| The MLO on staff | Never named | Not named or shown by default (see owner decisions) |
| Team members | Not joke targets | Not joke targets; self-deprecation targets the firm as an institution ("a brokerage this new") and the industry's habits in general |
| Platform register | One voice | Loosest on X and Threads, closer to the blog on LinkedIn (only 33% want personality there) |

Humor about the area's known downsides (humidity, hurricane-season insurance, golf-cart traffic, HOA paperwork) builds the "honest local" persona the way Ryanair's jokes about cramped seats do. The kill switch below exists because the same jokes become cruel during an active storm.

## Two named humans and one brand voice, replying more than they post

**Account structure.** On X, one brand account carries the persona, because growth there comes from daily reply work that Abdulla can sustain. The X ranker has no brand-versus-personal weighting, and a brand-owned voice outlasts any single writer. A second X account for Timmy is optional. On LinkedIn the order flips. Personal profiles do the growth work and the company page serves as a credibility hub. Company posts reach about 1.6% of followers ([Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)), and LinkedIn's own data shows employees' networks average about 10 times the company's follower count ([LinkedIn Elevate](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/elevate/Resources/pdf/elevate-infographic.pdf)). Timmy's profile carries stories and market lessons from the veteran. Abdulla's profile carries the weekly numbers and the "how we built the data" posts. The page reshares the best of both. Threads mirrors the X brand account in a looser register. On Bluesky, Chapter3 should claim the handle, mirror lightly and use a "Myrtle Beach" keyword feed. Reddit runs from one human account under a disclosed identity, with answers written by hand. Every profile shows "Chapter3 Realty" (check C1), and any staff personal account that promotes Chapter3 becomes a professional account under both South Carolina's "any medium" rule and NAR Case 10-6.

**Content pillars.** No study supports any particular pillar ratio, so the split below is a starting point to tune. It draws on what chapter3realty.com already publishes:

| Pillar | Share | Website asset it draws on | Recurring series |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data and receipts | ~40% | Building-level rental returns, permit tracking, HOA guides | "Friday Numbers" chart; "Permit Watch"; "Building Receipts" (HOA fee, insurance and assessment history, with no units for sale by other firms named); quarterly prediction ledger |
| Place and relocation | ~25% | Relocation guides | "Myth of the Week"; origin-state cost comparisons (Ohio, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania); "what surprised you about moving here?" text polls |
| Stories and personality | ~20% | Timmy's 30 years; anonymized deals (with consent) | "Ask a 30-year broker"; deals that went sideways and what saved them; local life (traffic, tourist season, food) |
| Offers | ≤15% | Free investor analysis tool, newsletter | Short-term-rental math teardown that ends at the tool; newsletter calls to action; keyword DM offers |

A yearly tentpole works the way National Roast Day works for Wendy's. Two candidates fit: a "**Roast My Listing Photos Day**" limited to Chapter3's own listings with written seller consent, and a pre-season (never in-season) "**Hurricane Season Insurance Receipts**" week. Trend-jacking should be rare. About a third of consumers find it embarrassing, and 27% say it only works within 24 to 48 hours ([Sprout Social 2025](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)). The Brain should therefore keep pre-approved templates for predictable news (insurance filings, short-term-rental ordinance votes, NAR rule changes, rate moves described without numbers), so a take can ship inside that window.

**Formats.** On X the winning unit is one chart and one line of thesis. Each week, a numbered thread should end at the investor tool or newsletter (Gerli's pattern), and quote-posts should answer national takes with local data. On LinkedIn, the weekly numbers become a document carousel (the top format), with links placed in the first comment, not the post body. On Reddit, the format is a long, sourced answer that mentions Chapter3 only where the subreddit's rules allow it.

**Cadence and replies.** No rigorous study sets X frequency. The plan below combines measured LinkedIn data with practitioner norms for X, and every figure should be adjusted using Chapter3's own analytics.

| Platform | Original posts | Engagement work | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| X (brand) | 1-3 per day, spaced to avoid the same-session author discount; 1 thread per week | 20-30 substantive replies per day across a list of 30-50 target accounts, at most 1-2 per account per day | Vendor midpoint ([Teract](https://www.teract.ai/resources/twitter-reply-guy-strategy-2026); [ReachMore](https://reachmore.co/blogs/x-reply-strategy-2026)); 48-hour age filter |

| LinkedIn page | Reshares and adapted carousels, 2-3 per week | None | ~1.6% reach |
| Threads | Daily repurposed X posts | Answer own replies | Scale; no real estate data |
| Reddit | None (no broadcast posts) | 2-4 hand-written answers per week after reading each subreddit's rules | [ForkOff audit](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/reddit-marketing/reddit-self-promotion-rules-2026) |

The reply list should mix housing-data voices (the national analysts above), Grand Strand media (WPDE, WMBF, WBTW, the Sun News), short-term-rental analysts, and retirement and relocation accounts. The aim is to earn mutual follows from local accounts, whose replies carry the 15-point boost. Every reply should add a number, a local fact or a real question. "Great point!" replies are exactly what the spam classifier and the mute button are built to catch. LinkedIn says commenting once a week can triple profile views, though it published no data behind the claim ([Lindsey Gamble](https://lindseygamble.com/blog/linkedin-shares-commenting-can-triple-profile-views)). Replies on Chapter3's own posts and DMs matter most of all: 73% of consumers say they would buy from a competitor if a brand does not respond on social ([Sprout Social 2025](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/press/the-days-of-trend-chasing-are-over-new-research-from-sprout-social-reveals-a-third-of-consumers-think-jumping-on-viral-trends-is-embarrassing-for-brands/)). The team should answer the same day.

## Followers become clients through the website's tools, measured in conversations

**Social media already leads as a lead source for agents.** In NAR's 2025 technology survey, 39% of Realtors named social media as producing the most quality leads, ahead of CRM (23%), MLS (17%) and brokerage websites (13%). The survey reports no conversion rate from social leads to closed deals ([NAR 2025 Technology Survey](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf)). The national data voices show the funnel shape: free public data, then a deeper gated tier, then a high-touch service. Chapter3 has already built the expensive middle of this funnel. The building-level return data, the free investor analysis tool, the HOA and relocation guides and the permit tracking are ready-made lead magnets that most local competitors lack. The path runs post → profile with a clear headline and the firm name → one link hub → the tool, or a weekly "Grand Strand by the Numbers" email that Chapter3 owns → consult. Keyword offers in posts ("reply RELOCATE and we'll send the moving checklist") move people into DMs, and DMs should get a same-day answer. Moving a lead from an in-app DM to SMS brings in South Carolina's telephone law, which explicitly covers texts. The Brain should capture written consent with the phone number and enforce check C21 ([SC § 37-21](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c021.php)). Earned media is the multiplier for a data brokerage. Gerli's local-TV pickups show that a sharp, local, data-backed finding gets amplified, and the three largest Grand Strand news outlets reach about 1.2M Facebook followers between them ([Feedspot](https://journalists.feedspot.com/myrtle_beach_news_websites/)).

**Attribution should be cheap and consistent.** Chapter3 should tag every link with UTM codes by platform and by person, add Duolingo's "how did you hear about us?" question to every form with a social option and a person option, tag sources in the CRM, and review leads, appointments and closings from social each month.

Growth expectations need to be honest because the question asks to "grow fast." The only documented zero-follower trajectory in the research is an anecdote: about 470 X followers in 10 weeks from daily replies ([Tom Butler](https://x.com/tomlbutler/status/1923972320973967595)). LinkedIn follower growth fell about 59% platform-wide in the latest van der Blom data ([Mercer MacKay](https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/)), and Buffer finds the biggest lift goes to accounts that post consistently for 20+ weeks ([Buffer](https://support.buffer.com/articles/growing-on-social-media-a-buffer-resource-guide-Vt0q4k0Sqr.md)). Reasonable planning ranges, which are inferences and not benchmarks, are 300 to 1,000 X followers in three months and 1,000 LinkedIn followers per personal profile in three to six months. Reaching 10,000 on either platform in a local niche is a 12-to-24-month goal at best, and Chapter3 may never need it. Five hundred followers who are relocating professionals, short-term-rental investors, lenders and Grand Strand homeowners are worth more than 10,000 generic real estate followers.

The weekly scorecard should rank metrics by business value. Qualified conversations (DMs or replies from prospects) come first, then website-tool sessions and newsletter signups from social, then profile visits, then the weekly follower growth rate. Below those sit replies, quotes and shares per post rather than likes, which follows X's weights and LinkedIn's preference for comments and saves. Monthly appointments and closings attributed to social close the list, tracked separately for each named voice so the owner can see which voice converts. Two negative indicators matter just as much. The first is any rise in unfollows, hidden replies or angry quote-posts after a joke. X's "Under the Hood" visibility tool would show the account's visibility labels, but it currently requires accounts at least a year old with at least 10 posts a month ([ExplainX](https://explainx.ai/blog/x-algorithm-for-you-timeline-open-source-ranking-weights-august-2026)). The second is any compliance check that fires repeatedly, which signals that the voice bible needs a fix.

## The Social Media Brain should draft and check, and never post or reply by itself

Every platform allows a brand to schedule its own original posts through native schedulers or official APIs. **The prohibited zone is automated engagement.** X requires bots to carry an "Automated" label and requires prior approval before AI-generated replies, and even approved bots may not impersonate humans ([X Developer Guidelines](https://docs.x.com/developer-guidelines)). LinkedIn hides scripted comments and restricts repeat offenders. Reddit verifies accounts that show automated behavior, and many subreddits ban AI-written text. The audience penalty is real but specific. Originality.ai, which sells AI detection, classified 53.7% of influential LinkedIn long-form posts as likely AI. Those posts reportedly earned about 45% less engagement, while AI drafts edited by a human averaged 6.85% engagement against 6.22% for fully human posts ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/); [SocialNexis](https://socialnexis.com/guides/ai-posts-engagement-decay-rate)). In Klaviyo's survey, 32% of consumers said brand AI content lowers their trust ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/solutions/ai/consumer-trust-in-ai)), and enthusiasm for AI creator content fell from 60% in 2023 to 26% in 2025 ([Outlier Report](https://www.outlierreport.com/en/news/consumer-excitement-for-ai-content-crashed-from-60-to-26-and-now-anti-ai-is-the-premium)). Humans should therefore supply the opinions, stories and local facts. The AI's job is drafting and checking.

The Brain should share Blog Brain's fact sources and its honesty, buyer-first, no-rates and staff-naming rules, then add a voice layer and the safety machinery below:

| Stage | What the Brain does | Human role |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Intake | Pulls new figures from Chapter3's building, permit and HOA datasets; takes a weekly 10-minute voice memo from Timmy and Abdulla; watches a list of local news sources | Records the memos; flags stories |
| 2. Fact ledger | Ties every number in a draft to a dataset row or public source with a date; blocks any number it cannot source, so it cannot invent statistics | Fixes gaps |
| 3. Drafting | Writes per platform and per voice profile using the two-layer rule; requires at least one specific local detail (a building, a figure, a street-level observation) so drafts pass LinkedIn's slop test | Adds a detail only a local would know |
| 4. Compliance engine | Runs checks C1-C23 with regex for trigger terms, firm name and people-words, and LLM extraction for named entities and factual claims; returns pass, fix or block with the rule cited | Accepts or overrides a "fix" with a logged reason; never overrides a "block" |
| 5. Tier routing | Classifies green, amber or red; sends amber to the Broker-in-Charge queue | Writer approves green; the Broker-in-Charge approves amber |
| 6. Publishing | Hands approved posts to native schedulers or official-API tools with all auto-reply, auto-like, auto-DM and auto-follow features off; sends X link posts natively | A human presses publish or schedules |
| 7. Engagement assist | Builds the daily reply list and suggests angles and data points for each target | A human writes and posts every reply, comment and DM |
| 8. Kill switch | Pauses the whole queue and strips humor when the National Weather Service issues a hurricane watch or warning for Horry or Georgetown County, when there is a local fatality (including rip-current drownings), or during a national tragedy | A human lifts the pause |
| 9. Archive and log | Saves a screenshot, the check results, the approver and a timestamp for every post, kept at least 24 months | The Broker-in-Charge reviews monthly |
| 10. Learning loop | Reports weekly metrics by pillar and person; logs incidents; proposes voice-bible changes | The owner approves changes |

The Brain also needs registries. It should track listing-firm authorizations (C2), homeowner and seller consents (C3 and the Roast Day), client testimonial consents (C14), image licenses (C18), verified 55+ communities (C7), and lead SMS consents (C21). It should also hold a do-not-joke list: active storms and floods, deaths and drownings, protected classes and "who lives there," specific clients, other agents' listings, politics, religion, retiree stereotypes, mortgage hardship and foreclosures, and the team. AI-generated images of real properties count as advertising and should be labeled. AI-generated "clients" are illegal under the FTC review rule ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). No platform requires a per-post disclosure for human-reviewed, AI-assisted posts. A single public line ("software helps us draft and check; a licensed human approves every post") is cheap insurance against the Duolingo-style gap between voice and conduct. Whether to publish that line is the owner's call.

## Recommendation: run a 90-day receipts-desk pilot on X and LinkedIn

Chapter3 should launch the honest-insider-with-receipts persona as a **90-day pilot** with X (brand account) and LinkedIn (Timmy's and Abdulla's profiles plus the company page) as the primary channels. Threads should run as a low-effort mirror, Bluesky as a claimed handle, and Reddit as hand-written answers only. Before the first post, three things must exist: a voice bible signed by the Broker-in-Charge, with the two-layer rule, the do-not-joke list and the topic tiers; the Brain's compliance engine running checks C1-C23; and the hurricane and tragedy kill switch. The pilot should ship the "Friday Numbers" chart, one weekly thread that ends at the investor tool, and the LinkedIn carousel from week one, with 20-30 replies a day on X. At day 90, it should be judged on qualified conversations and tool sessions, not follower counts. The decisions below belong to the owner because they set legal exposure, budget or brand identity.

## Conclusion
