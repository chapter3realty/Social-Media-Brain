# Five Winners Grew by Repeating One Format

The five real estate accounts that grew best, and that a small brokerage can actually learn from, are **Glennda Baker (TikTok)**, **Jerry Pinkas (YouTube, Myrtle Beach)**, **Nick Gerli's Reventure (YouTube and X)**, **Will Friedner's Living in Montana (YouTube)** and **Lance Lambert (X and LinkedIn)**. Each one picked a single format and repeated it hundreds of times. Baker posted daily "talking to a friend" stories about life as an agent. She went from **122 to more than 565,000 TikTok followers in about a year** and credited **$241,000 of 2021 commission** to TikTok. Pinkas, a direct competitor on the Grand Strand, posts warning-style videos for out-of-state movers and retirees ("AVOID MOVING TO SOUTH CAROLINA…") and has **220,000 subscribers and 43.2 million views**. Gerli and Lambert grew by posting one chart about one named place, over and over. Friedner's channel took off on one honest "reasons not to move to Montana" video, and he says it now brings **95% of his leads**. Most follower counts here were measured by a third-party tracker. Nearly every dollar and deal figure is self-reported and unaudited. The biggest lesson is that the accounts with the most deals are not the biggest accounts. They are the ones that answer the exact questions a mover types into YouTube. For Chapter3, the best fit is the **Living in Montana model**: long YouTube videos on narrow Grand Strand searches that Pinkas does not already own, backed by a weekly local data table and a fast follow-up call. Based on similar agents, expect the first lead around month 3 and steady volume around month 10.

## Five accounts made the cut on growth, results and copyability

**How to read the numbers.** Every number in this report carries one of three labels. **Measured** means a third-party tracker read the public count: vidIQ for YouTube (read 6–8 November 2026), the fxtwitter mirror of X profiles (8 November 2026) and the follower count on public LinkedIn pages (8 November 2026). YouTube rounds subscriber counts to three digits, so small changes do not show. **Press-reported** means a journalist printed a count from the public profile, which we did not re-check. **Self-reported** means the creator, their company, a podcast or a vendor said it. Every deal, commission and lead figure in this report is self-reported. None was checked against MLS records.

**How I ranked them.** I graded about forty accounts from the research on three things. **Growth** gets an A if the account is big or fast and a third party measured it at dated points, a B if the numbers are partly self-reported or undated, and a C if they are small or unknown. **Business results** get an A for dollar or deal figures, a B for lead-share or product-user claims, and a C for nothing published. **Copyability** asks whether a new brokerage with a phone and one or two people could run the same play. I ranked on growth plus business results, and I dropped any account that fails the copy test (for example, accounts that need a TV show, $50 million listings or other firms' listing photos).

## 1. Glennda Baker turned 122 TikTok followers into $241K in a year

| | |
|---|---|
| Platform | TikTok first; later Instagram and a podcast |
| Numbers | 122 followers at her October 2020 debut, more than 565,000 about a year later (press-reported) ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)). About 850,000 by January 2023 ([Movoto](https://agenthelp.movoto.com/pro-plus/glennda-baker-1/20/2023)). 880,000+ TikTok and 136,000+ Instagram by July 2023 ([Inman](https://inman.com/2023/07/06/whats-next-for-million-dollar-listing-new-york-star-tyler-whitman)). 870K+ TikTok per Coldwell Banker in October 2023 (company-reported) ([Atlanta Agent](https://atlantaagentmagazine.com/2023/10/13/glennda-baker-leaves-ansley-christies-international-real-estate-for-coldwell-banker/)). X: 1,696 (measured, Nov 2026) ([fxtwitter](https://api.fxtwitter.com/glenndabaker)). No count after 2023 was found |
| Speed | The fastest documented climb by a working agent: about 565K in 12 months. Inman reported one jump from 50K to 300K that began on 14 April 2021 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2021/06/04/heres-how-this-agent-generated-108k-on-tiktok-in-2021-so-far/)) |
| Who runs it | Baker herself, an Atlanta agent with 26 years in the business, with videographer Denver Bailey. She moved from Ansley/Christie's to Coldwell Banker in October 2023 |

**What she posted.** One format: interview-style clips framed as a friend overhearing a chat. Bailey plays the friend off camera and asks the questions. The topics are the everyday life of an agent: career stories, how a big commission check feels, and things buyers and sellers do not know. A recurring story is how she sold 27 houses through Match.com. Follower questions in the comments become the next videos. Her persona is candid and Southern, with the catchphrase **"Oh, my stars!"** and star-print clothes as her signature look. She started at **two videos a week, then went daily**, and she **batch-records 30+ videos in one day each month**. Her daily videos drew 30,000 to 2 million likes, and a "Why are you following me?" video drew more than 8,000 comments ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)). Her most-viewed video had **10.2 million views** in 2022, but its topic could not be confirmed because the source page is now gone ([Curaytor](https://curaytor.com/blog/glennda-baker-what-we-can-learn-from-her-tiktok-success)). Her own advice: "Be you—and just turn up the volume."

**How followers became business.** She reported **$108,000 in commission from TikTok in the first five months of 2021** ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2021/06/04/heres-how-this-agent-generated-108k-on-tiktok-in-2021-so-far/)) and **$241,000 for all of 2021** ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/technology/how-this-agent-is-making-six-figures-on-tiktok)). Both are self-reported. Much of it came from outside Atlanta. In just two days she sent three agent referrals to other markets and got two inquiries from buyers relocating to Atlanta. A national audience pays through referral fees, not just local deals. Later she added a podcast with Tyler Whitman, a brokerage move and the "Queen of Real Estate Video" brand. A podcast title claims she "made over $1.6M from social media videos" ([Everand](https://www.everand.com/podcast/710934340/How-Glennda-Baker-Made-Over-1-6M-From-Social-Media-Videos-Viral-sensation-and-top-Atlanta-real-estate-agent-Glennda-Baker-shares-how-she-built-an-ic)). That is self-reported and probably includes speaking and brand deals.

**What went wrong or could.** The whole business sits on one platform and one person. After the Supreme Court upheld the TikTok ban law in January 2025, Inman told agents to spread out to Reels and YouTube Shorts ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/01/17/scotus-upheld-the-tiktok-ban-heres-what-agents-should-do-now/)). Her strong numbers are from 2021, and no count after 2023 was found. Her leads also skewed toward out-of-town referrals rather than local listings.

**What Chapter3 can copy.** (1) Lock one format: the owner answers an off-camera friend's questions about "what nobody tells you about moving to Myrtle Beach." (2) Hold one batch day a month and record 20–30 short clips, so posting is daily without daily effort. (3) Turn every good comment into the next video. (4) Set up a referral desk, so that viewers buying in Ohio or New Jersey become referral fees instead of lost leads.

## 2. Jerry Pinkas built 43 million views selling the beach to out-of-staters

| | |
|---|---|
| Platform | YouTube, plus a blog of matching search articles |
| Numbers | **220,000 subscribers, 43.2 million views, 1,666 videos** (measured, vidIQ, Nov 2026). Subscribers were flat at the rounded 220K over the last 30 days, while views grew by roughly 33,000–128,000 a day ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@JerryPinkas/)) |
| Speed | Not measured. The channel dates from November 2010. Its breakout videos came in 2023 and 2024 |
| Who runs it | Jerry Pinkas, owner of Jerry Pinkas Real Estate Experts in Myrtle Beach. A 10-person team with about $65M in sales and 214 sides in 2022 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2023/02/16/broker-spotlight-jerry-pinkas-jerry-pinkas-real-estate-experts/)). His site claims 36+ years in real estate, a brokerage opened in 2005 and about $987M in career sales (self-reported) ([Home Guide Myrtle Beach](https://www.homeguidemyrtlebeach.com/agents/)) |

**What he posted.** His top videos are not about Myrtle Beach listings. They are about the decision to move, aimed at out-of-state buyers and retirees ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@JerryPinkas/)):

| Video | Date | Views |
|---|---|---|
| "Florida Home Buyers BLINDSIDED By TREMENDOUS Cost To Own" | Mar 2023 | 3.66M |
| "Top 10 Cities EVERYONE is MOVING TO in America in 2025" | Nov 2023 | 2.4M |
| "Florida Home Buyers BLINDSIDED By Something Terrible Happening in Florida" | Apr 2024 | 2.2M |
| "The BEST Retirement Advice EVER From Retirees + MORE FUN!" | Sep 2023 | 1.84M |
| "AVOID MOVING TO SOUTH CAROLINA - Unless You Can Deal With These 10 Facts \| Living in South Carolina" | Feb 2023 | 1.58M |

The title formula repeats. It uses **ALL-CAPS emotion words** (BLINDSIDED, AVOID, EVERYONE), **numbered lists** ("10 Facts," "Top 10"), a **comparison to Florida**, and a **"| Living in South Carolina" search tag** at the end. His newest upload, "The Silver Tsunami Will Release 13.9 Million Houses" (5 November 2026), drew 20,500 views in ten hours, which shows he now mixes in national housing news. His blog targets the same searches: "Is Myrtle Beach a good place to retire to," "Why is real estate so cheap in Myrtle Beach," golf condos, Surfside Beach oceanfront, Murrells Inlet starter homes and Carolina Forest neighborhoods ([Pinkas blog](https://www.homeguidemyrtlebeach.com/blog/is-myrtle-beach-a-good-place-to-retire-to/)). Exact upload cadence was not published, but 1,666 videos means very high volume. His growth came from steady output, not one lucky hit.

**How followers became business.** The channel feeds a team that sells to out-of-state buyers. He calls it "the No. 1 YouTube Channel for real estate in Myrtle Beach," a self-claim that the measured numbers support ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2023/02/16/broker-spotlight-jerry-pinkas-jerry-pinkas-real-estate-experts/)). No source says what share of the team's 214 sides came from YouTube.

**What went wrong or could.** No public failure was found. Two risks show in the data. First, subscriber growth has stalled. Second, his biggest hits are national topics (Florida, "cities everyone is moving to"), which pull viewers who may never buy on the Grand Strand. The "BLINDSIDED" style works, but a local broker who gets the facts wrong pays more for it than a national commentator does. For Chapter3, one compliance trap sits inside his best format. A "cost to own" video that quotes a monthly mortgage payment or a down payment triggers federal Regulation Z disclosures, including the APR ([12 CFR 1026.24](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.24)). Stick to taxes, insurance and HOA dues, or have the lender's compliance team review any payment numbers.

**What Chapter3 can copy.** (1) The honest-downsides title: "Don't buy a Grand Strand condo until you know these 7 costs." (2) Origin-state comparisons he does not cover: "Moving from Ohio to Myrtle Beach: what changes," "New Jersey vs. Horry County property taxes." (3) Pair every video with a blog post on the same search phrase, as he does. (4) Go narrower than Pinkas. He owns "moving to South Carolina." Chapter3 can own single towns and single condo buildings.

On X, the format is **one chart plus one number-first sentence about a named market**. "A massive supply pile-up is happening in Seattle's housing market right now. Listings just broke 13,000…" drew **150,557 views** on 27 October 2026 ([fxtwitter](https://api.fxtwitter.com/nickgerli1/status/2115172349821546905)). A post on mortgage applications "collapsed 60% from their pandemic peak" drew 148,529 ([fxtwitter](https://api.fxtwitter.com/nickgerli1/status/2110407606657241399)). Longer threads are numbered and end with a pitch: "11) By using Reventure's Home Price Forecast… Head to [app]" ([X](https://x.com/nickgerli1/status/1902022163252580431)). His biggest single post, he says, was an Instagram Reel, "No one is buying these Florida townhouses," at 9.8M+ views (self-reported) ([nickgerli.com](https://www.nickgerli.com/)). National press then writes stories off his posts ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/housing-market-entering-crash-stage-multiple-cities-1920530)), and so does local TV ([WKRN](https://www.wkrn.com/news/local-news/nashville/reventure-app-ceo-nick-gerli-warns-of-downtown-nashville-housing-crash/)).

**How followers became business.** Free content sells a data app. Reventure App claims **250,000+ registered users and 100,000+ downloads** (self-reported) ([nickgerli.com](https://www.nickgerli.com/)). Premium costs $39, pitched at the end of his threads ([X](https://x.com/nickgerli1/status/2032116996460925110)). Consulting and speaking sit on top. No revenue figure is public.

**What went wrong or could.** Critics call the videos fear-mongering clickbait, and "debunk" videos target him ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z_utJUk0Rto)). In June 2024 Fortune noted that, apart from Austin, the boomtowns he flagged were still up year over year ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/06/25/pandemic-boomtowns-florida-texas-overvalued-crashing/)). A data company can shrug off a missed call. A local broker cannot. Sellers read "crash" from their agent as a reason not to list.

**What Chapter3 can copy.** (1) One chart, one sentence, one named place: "Oceanfront condo listings in North Myrtle Beach are up X% in a year." (2) Numbered threads that end with a link to a free Chapter3 tool or newsletter, not a bare ad. (3) Send the strongest local chart to WPDE, WMBF or The Sun News, the way local TV picked up Gerli's Nashville call. (4) Publish indicators (months of supply, price cuts, days on market), never crash forecasts. If the data comes from the MLS, follow its rules and cite it. The SC Real Estate Commission says MLS rules apply on top of state law ([SC REC](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)).

## 4. Living in Montana reached 36K in ten months with one honest video

| | |
|---|---|
| Platform | YouTube |
| Numbers | **36,000 subscribers and 3M+ views within 10 months**, roughly doubling the following year. Top video 3.5M views. Source: an agency spotlight dated 26 February 2026 (self-reported) ([Inbound REM](https://inboundrem.com/real-estate-youtube-spotlight-will-friedner-of-living-in-montana/)). No tracker count was retrieved |
| Speed | Fast for a local agent channel: 36K in under a year |
| Who runs it | Will Friedner, an agent in rural Montana |

**What he posted.** The breakout was a **7-minute candid "reasons NOT to move to Montana" video**. It covered wildlife, local laws, weather, everyday hassles and locals who resist change. About 95% of his channel traffic came from that one video. Follow-ups covered unusual and off-grid towns, legal issues, Yellowstone's effect on the area, the end of the housing boom and population loss. He says his best performers are the plainest: "the videos that do the best are the ones where I just tour a town in Montana." Titles use tension and curiosity. He picked a search phrase with little competition, about 1,100 YouTube searches a month, and was honest about the downsides during the COVID moving surge, in a market few local agents covered ([Inbound REM](https://inboundrem.com/real-estate-youtube-spotlight-will-friedner-of-living-in-montana/)).

**How followers became business.** "95% of my leads come from YouTube," he says. No deal count was published. Agents running the same relocation model do publish deal figures, all self-reported. Karin Carr in coastal Savannah reported **$100K+ in commission from one "moving to Savannah" video**, with her first lead about three months after her first upload ([Jamil Academy](https://www.jamilacademy.com/blog/youtube-for-real-estate-agents)). Malcolm Lawson reported 7–8 closings from one military-transfer video. Levi Lascsak's Dallas relocation channel reported 164 sales in 12 months, with his first closings four months after his first video ([Keeping It Real Podcast](https://blubrry.com/keepingitrealpod/92589391/164-sales-in-12-months-all-from-youtube-levi-lascsak)). These channels are often small. A channel with a few thousand subscribers can still produce six figures, because its viewers are already planning a move.

**What went wrong or could.** One video carried the channel, so one change to YouTube search could cut lead flow sharply. His timing (the COVID moving wave) was lucky. The source is an agency that sells YouTube services, and it gives no deal numbers.

**What Chapter3 can copy.** (1) Make the honest video first: "Reasons NOT to move to Myrtle Beach," covering insurance, flood zones, summer traffic, tourists and HOA assessments. (2) Plain town tours of Carolina Forest, Surfside Beach, Murrells Inlet, Little River, Conway and North Myrtle Beach. (3) Pick phrases with real searches and little competition before you shoot. (4) Film public places and amenities, not identifiable private homes. The SC Real Estate Commission says filming someone's house for social content needs the owner's written permission, even when the house is not for sale ([SC REC](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)). Describe the town, never the people who live there ([24 CFR 100.75](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75)).

## 5. Lance Lambert grew about 37 times on X by posting regional tables

| | |
|---|---|
| Platform | X first, then LinkedIn and a newsletter |
| Numbers | X (@NewsLambert): **109,650 followers** (measured, 8 Nov 2026) ([fxtwitter](https://api.fxtwitter.com/NewsLambert/status/2054395408453951667)). About **2,985 in November 2021** per tracker SpeakRJ (seen in a search result) ([SpeakRJ](https://www.speakrj.com/audit/report/NewsLambert/twitter)). "More than 80K" around mid-2024 ([Movoto](https://www.movoto.com/blog/movotomic-episode-7-lance-lambert/)). LinkedIn: 29,000 (Nov 2026), up from an undated 19,000 ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lance-lambert-0bb5b677)) |
| Speed | About 37 times in five years, the best-measured growth curve of any account in this research. He recalls sitting near 3,000, then 10,000, for years first |
| Who runs it | Lance Lambert, former Fortune real estate editor. He co-founded ResiClub with Anthony Pompliano in October 2023 |

**What he posted.** Easy-to-read data about **specific regions** (Boise, Austin, Tampa, Jacksonville) at a time when national outlets covered only national numbers. His signature is a **table of the 50 largest markets** showing 1-, 2-, 3- and 4-year price change and distance from peak ([X](https://x.com/NewsLambert/status/1814368431812292620)). He frames each news item in 10–15 words and treats X as a "sandbox" to test topics. He avoids fights, sometimes sends corrections by DM instead of dunking, and every six months or so he reminds followers that he does not do "good time to buy" journalism ([Movoto](https://www.movoto.com/blog/movotomic-episode-7-lance-lambert/)). His breakout lined up with the 2022 rate shock, when housing data became news. Bare ads flop. A May 2026 post that only said "subscribe to the ResiClub newsletter" got **3,335 views and 3 likes** (measured) ([fxtwitter](https://api.fxtwitter.com/NewsLambert/status/2054395408453951667)).

**How followers became business.** ResiClub runs a free daily newsletter and a PRO tier at $299 a year ([ResiClub](https://www.resiclubanalytics.com/p/special-message-resiclub-editor-lance-lambert-ca7a)), plus a data terminal and the ResiDay conference (6 November 2026, New York) ([ResiClub](https://www.resiclubanalytics.com/)). Fast Company syndicates the free articles. ResiClub also sells agents a webinar on how to "become your local housing authority" with a local market newsletter ([ResiClub](https://www.resiclubanalytics.com/p/how-to-use-resiclub-pro-to-build-a-top-notch-local-housing-market-newsletter-recording)). No subscriber or revenue numbers are public, which is why he ranks fifth.

**What went wrong or could.** Growth was slow for years before it was fast. Business results are not disclosed. Text-platform reach is also hard for brokers. A Charlotte broker with 10,000 LinkedIn followers who posted about nine times in two days got 1–2 reactions per post ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nancybraun/)). The difference is that Lambert's posts carry real data.

**What Chapter3 can copy.** (1) A weekly "Grand Strand by the numbers" table covering 8–10 towns, with median price, months of supply, price cuts and change since 2020. (2) His neutral tone. No "good time to buy" calls keeps a licensed broker out of trouble. (3) Put the newsletter link at the end of a chart post, never in a post by itself. (4) Post the monthly version on LinkedIn as a PDF carousel. Carousels had the highest LinkedIn engagement rate of any format in 2026 data (7%), yet fewer than 5% of profiles post them ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/07/06/latest-data-reveals-social-media-engagement/)).

## What the five share, and the model Chapter3 should copy

**Five things all five did.** First, **one format, locked**: Baker's overheard chat, Pinkas's warning list, Gerli's place-plus-chart, Friedner's honest town video, Lambert's market table. Second, **a specific place or number in the first line**: "South Carolina," "Seattle," "Montana," "the 50 largest markets." Third, **useful truth over polish**. Downsides, costs and data beat glossy tours at every scale. Fourth, **volume made easy**: monthly batch days, 1,666 videos, a chart every few days. Fifth, **a next step off the platform**: referral fees, a team, an app, a newsletter. None of the five relied on ad revenue. Growth was also slower than it looks. Lambert sat near 3,000 followers for years. Relocation agents report the first lead around month 3, first closings around month 4 ([Jamil Academy](https://www.jamilacademy.com/blog/youtube-for-real-estate-agents)), and steady business around month 10 ([Jamil Academy](https://www.jamilacademy.com/blog/youtube-for-real-estate-agents)). The most common way local agents fail is quitting before then.

**The recommendation: copy Living in Montana first, and add Lambert's table.** Chapter3's buyers (relocators, retirees, STR investors) all start with a search, and the relocation YouTube model is the one with the most published deals per subscriber. Start with 10 long videos: "Reasons NOT to move to Myrtle Beach," a cost-to-own breakdown (taxes, insurance, HOA, flood), one tour per town, one per origin state (Ohio, New York, New Jersey), and one STR explainer per oceanfront area. Cut each one into Shorts and Reels on a monthly batch day, as Baker does. Run Lambert's weekly town table on X, LinkedIn and an email newsletter as the "receipts" that prove Chapter3 knows the numbers. Handle leads the way Will Sawyer does in Greenville, SC: a link in every description to the site and newsletter, a 20–35 minute call booked fast, and leads graded A (moving within 90 days) to D (12+ months). He reports 47 homes sold from YouTube, 60–90 leads a month and $1M+ a year in commission (self-reported) ([Real Estate Legends](https://www.realestatelegends.show/p/here-s-how-he-sells-100-houses-a-year-half-from-youtube)). Gear is cheap. A Monterey agent reported about $280K from YouTube in two years on about $10K of equipment and one video a week (self-reported) ([Keeping It Real Podcast](https://keepingitrealpod.com/podcast-episode/how-to-find-buyers-sellers-through-youtube-toby-fernie/)). If an X and LinkedIn data pilot is already running, keep it as the data layer. YouTube is where the documented deals come from.

**Rules to check before copying any of this.**

| If Chapter3 copies… | The rule | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Zillow Gone Wild roasts, "what $X buys you" tours, market updates that name addresses | SC Code § 40-57-135(E)(1): another firm's listing, photos or even its address count as advertising and need written authorization from the listing firm plus clear credit ([SC REC](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)). Listing photos are also copyrighted, and Zillow Gone Wild was sued in 2024 ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/08/05/real-estate-photographer-sues-zillow-gone-wild-for-using-property-pictures)) | Use only Chapter3 listings, or get the listing firm's OK by email and add "listed by [firm]" |
| Any post, including a chart or a town video | § 40-57-135(E)(2): every ad, even a general trend post, must name the firm ([SC REC](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/AdvertisingMemo-H.4754.pdf)) | Put "Chapter3 Realty" on screen and in the caption |
| Town tours and drive-throughs | The Commission says filming an identifiable private home needs the owner's written permission ([SC REC](https://llr.sc.gov/re/News/Reminder%20of%20Advertising%20Restrictions.pdf)) | Film beaches, streets, shops and amenities, or get the owner's written OK |
| Retiree videos | Fair Housing Act: no statement showing a preference by familial status and other protected classes ([24 CFR 100.75](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75)) | Describe homes and places, not people. Say "55+" only for verified 55+ communities. Never target housing ads by age |
| "Cost to own" videos | Regulation Z: a payment or down-payment figure triggers full disclosures, including the APR ([12 CFR 1026.24](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.24)) | Talk taxes, insurance and HOA dues, or get lender compliance review |
| Texting leads from the funnel | TCPA: automated marketing texts need prior written consent ([47 U.S.C. § 227](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227)) | Add a consent checkbox to every lead form |

## Conclusion

The research turns the usual idea of "growth" upside down. The biggest real estate accounts (Graham Stephan, Enes Yilmazer, Zillow Gone Wild) are now flat or shrinking, and they make money from TV, sponsors and paid features, not from selling homes. The growth that is still happening, and the deals that are documented, sit with smaller accounts that answer one buyer question better than anyone else: what moving there really costs, what the market data says, what a town is actually like. Reach matters less than intent. A 4,000-subscriber channel that ranks for "moving to Carolina Forest" can beat a million-follower page for a brokerage's bottom line.

That favors Chapter3 more than it seems. Pinkas proves the Grand Strand audience is huge, and his broad topics leave the narrow ones open: single towns, single buildings, real insurance and HOA costs, and weekly local data. Those topics also suit the honest, data-backed voice a licensed broker can defend. The plan is less about going viral than about being patient. Ten months of steady, specific videos is the documented price of entry, and most competitors quit before they pay it.
