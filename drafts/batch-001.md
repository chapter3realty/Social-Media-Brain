**LinkedIn** (534 characters)

> Myth of the Week, from the relocation questions we hear most: "South Carolina property tax is low, period."
> 
> South Carolina assesses a home you live in at 4% of its fair market value. A second home or a rental is assessed at 6%. On the same house, the second-home bill is higher.
> 
> The 4% rate is not automatic. The owner applies to the county assessor before the first penalty date for that year's taxes. A buyer who moves here and misses it pays the 6% rate for that year.
> 
> Gus, Chief Roof Inspector
> Chapter3 Realty, Myrtle Beach, SC

Sources:

- Legal residence assessed at 4% of fair market value: SC Code §12-43-220(c)(1), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c043.php, read 2026-11-07
- All other real property assessed at 6%: SC Code §12-43-220(e), same page, read 2026-11-07
- The owner must apply for the 4% ratio before the first penalty date: SC Code §12-43-220(c)(2)(ii), same page, read 2026-11-07

## 015-reddit-condo-answer: Reddit: a hand-written-style answer the owner adapts. Not Gus.

Pillar: education · Tier: green · Checker: **pass**

**Reddit** (1096 characters)

> Disclosure: I work at Chapter3 Realty, a brokerage in Myrtle Beach, so take this with that in mind.
> 
> Before you offer on an oceanfront condo you plan to rent, I'd get these in writing:
> 
> 1. **The HOA's reserve study and current budget.** You want to see how much is saved for the roof, elevators and seawall, and what big repairs are due in the next five years. Low savings plus a big repair usually means a special assessment.
> 2. **Any special assessment that's been voted, planned or discussed.** Ask the seller and read the last year of board minutes.
> 3. **The HOA's rules on rentals.** Some buildings limit short-term rentals or minimum stays. Check the city's rules too: Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach and the county each have their own.
> 4. **Your own insurance quotes** for the unit, including flood insurance, before you're under contract.
> 5. **Real rental numbers, net of everything.** Gross rent minus HOA dues, insurance, taxes, management, cleaning and furnishing. The gross number in a listing tells you very little.
> 
> Happy to answer questions here. I won't DM anyone unless you ask.
